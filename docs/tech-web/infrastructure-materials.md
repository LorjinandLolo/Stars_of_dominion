# Technology Web — Infrastructure & Materials

Playing into Infrastructure & Materials means betting that logistics wins wars and factories win histories. This is the domain of civilizations that measure themselves in tonnes lifted to orbit per hour, in the redundancy of their power grids, in how fast a bombed district comes back online. Its early tiers turn the five engineering tracks under every colony from a cost center into a weapon: pooled grids, priority-routed freight, mass drivers throwing cargo into orbit for the price of electricity. Its later tiers ask the harder question every industrial society eventually faces — what happens to the people when the machines no longer need them. Every major automation tech in this domain moves labor out of the economy and into the streets, and the player must govern that displacement with the parliament, welfare spending, and press tools the rest of the game provides, or watch it curdle into unrest, rebellion, and secession.

The identity this domain enables is the *arsenal civilization*: not the fastest to expand, not the most charismatic, but the one whose worlds are hardest to strangle and whose production survives contact with the enemy. Its Tier 2 fork decides what kind of industrial society you are — dispersed and resilient, monumental and brittle, or privatized and politically entangled — and that choice echoes through sieges, strikes, and parliamentary crises for the rest of the game. Its Tier 4 fork decides what industry is *for* once molecular manufacturing makes scarcity a policy choice rather than a fact: fabrication as a public commons that dissolves the labor question along with the tax base, or fabrication as licensed property that turns your matter compilers into an instrument of interstellar leverage. Either way, by the end of this domain a player is no longer optimizing output numbers; they are deciding what an economy is.

## Tier 1 — Expansion

### Unified Planetary Grid (`infra_planetary_grid`)
- **Domain:** infrastructure | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** phys_fusion_power
- **Concept:** Continental power grids interconnected into a single synchronized planetary machine — the same engineering leap Earth's regional grids never quite made. Fusion abundance makes the interties worth their transmission losses.
- **What becomes possible:** Energy generated anywhere on a world reaches machines everywhere on it. Brownouts become a political decision instead of a physical accident.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Planetary energy pooling with player-directed load shedding. Energy output stops being tied to the district that generated it; during shortage the player issues curtailment directives choosing which sectors of the 64-sector surface board go dark — industry keeps running while habitat blocks lose power (happiness hit), or vice versa. Shortage stops being a flat penalty and becomes a triage decision.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `planetary_grid`. The `power_grid` ceiling this tech lifts does not exist yet and must be built: `InfrastructureTrackDefinition` (data/infrastructure-tracks.ts) carries no per-track cap, and the only ceiling anywhere is the global `MAX_TRACK_LEVEL = 5` in lib/infrastructure/infrastructure-types.ts — so today every faction already takes `power_grid` to 5 with no tech at all. The gate is therefore a new optional `maxLevelBase` field on `InfrastructureTrackDefinition` (set to 3 for `power_grid`, absent on the other four tracks) enforced in lib/infrastructure/infrastructure-service.ts: both the `clampLevel` helper and the `track.level >= MAX_TRACK_LEVEL` rejection inside `canUpgradeTrack` resolve their ceiling through a new `trackCeiling(def, hasGridFlag)` rather than the bare constant. `canUpgradeTrack` takes the flag as a third argument — defaulting to `false` so existing callers stay gated — supplied by the `INFRA_UPGRADE_TRACK` handler in scripts/game-loop.ts, which already has `factionId` and the world in scope, via `hasTechFlag(world, factionId, 'planetary_grid')`. New order type `GRID_CURTAIL { planetId, protect: 'industry' | 'civilian' | 'military' }` handled in scripts/game-loop.ts; the economy pass reads the directive when planetary energy demand exceeds supply. Adds `power_grid` as a valid target class for the espionage sabotage ops in lib/espionage/.
- **Required capacity:** Knowledge alone changes nothing — the pooling bonus and curtailment orders only apply on planets whose `power_grid` track is level 3+, and the tech merely permits levels 4 and 5, which must still be bought at the punishing cost growth (1.65x compounded per level held). Fusion plants or equivalent generation must exist; the grid moves energy, it does not create it.
- **Interactions:** Prerequisite root for Mass Driver Arrays and (transitively) everything energy-hungry in this domain. Grid-dependent buildings (nanoforges, automated ports) list it. Load-shed choices feed the press system — blacking out civilian sectors during a war generates hostile coverage.
- **Strategic advantages:** Worlds ride out energy shocks; specialization becomes safer because an industrial world can lean on grid triage instead of balanced generation; the lifted `power_grid` ceiling is the gateway to every high-draw building later in the domain, and a faction without the tech is capped at level 3 on the one track that everything else in this domain draws from.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Interconnection means cascade. Pre-grid, espionage sabotage of a power district hurt one district; post-grid, a successful grid sabotage op blacks out the whole planet for its duration. Curtailing civilians is remembered: repeated `GRID_CURTAIL` against habitat sectors accumulates unrest and gives opposition parliament factions ammunition.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Wartime blackout scandals in the press; espionage arms races over grid hardening; energy-abundant worlds becoming irresistible sites for the automation buildings of Tiers 2-3.

### Advanced Composites (`mat_advanced_composites`)
- **Domain:** materials | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** — (domain root)
- **Concept:** Carbon-lattice and ceramic-matrix composites: materials engineered layer by layer for strength-to-mass ratios metals cannot reach. The difference between a structure that can stand and a structure that can be lifted.
- **What becomes possible:** Hulls, tethers, and megastructure frames light enough to launch and strong enough to survive orbit. The literal substance the rest of this domain is built from.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** A new production chain and stored commodity: **composites**. The Composite Forge consumes metals and chemicals to produce composites, a new storable resource that Tier 2+ orbital structures, mass drivers, and the orbital elevator require in their build costs. The player now manages a two-stage industrial chain — raw extraction feeding intermediate manufacture — instead of spending raw ores directly.
- **Engine hook:** New building `composite_forge` (data/buildings.ts, category `industrial`, districts `industrial`, `techRequired: 'mat_advanced_composites'`). Extend `STORABLE_RESOURCES` and the `bulk` storage class in lib/logistics/storage-types.ts with `composites`; storage-service overflow/spoilage applies unchanged. Add `composites` as an optional field on `BuildingCost` so orbital-structure and megaproject definitions can price in it.
- **Required capacity:** The forge needs infrastructure level 2 and a steady chemicals supply — knowledge without a chemicals industry produces nothing. Composites must be stored (warehouse capacity) and moved (freight track) like any bulk good; a forge on a blockaded world feeds nothing in orbit.
- **Interactions:** Prerequisite for Mass Driver Arrays, Orbital Industry, Programmable Metamaterials, and all three Tier 2 industrial-organization branches. Composites demand makes chemicals — previously a secondary resource — strategically contested, which the trade and espionage layers can exploit.
- **Strategic advantages:** First mover on composites can export the intermediate good at markup to factions that researched consumers of composites before producers; owning the chain end to end insulates you from that same squeeze.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** A new chokepoint. Composite forges are high-value sabotage targets, and a faction that let a rival supply its composites has handed them a lever — cutting exports stalls the victim's entire orbital construction queue. Chemicals demand roughly doubles on industrializing worlds, straining worlds that skipped chemical plants.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Composites embargoes as a coercion tool short of war; specialized "forge worlds" whose loss cripples an empire's megaproject pipeline; espionage theft of forge output via the order queue's existing sabotage patterns.

### Integrated Distribution Networks (`infra_logistics_network`)
- **Domain:** infrastructure | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** infra_planetary_grid
- **Concept:** Logistics as a planned system rather than an emergent accident: unified scheduling across rail, freight terminals, and orbital lift, plus standing interplanetary supply arrangements.
- **What becomes possible:** A quartermaster's view of the empire. Resources flow to declared priorities instead of pooling where they were produced and spoiling in overflowing warehouses.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Two linked mechanics. (1) **Distribution priority**: the player sets a per-planet split between military, civilian, and construction consumption, which drives the existing `planet.logistics.constructionMultiplier` — starving civilians to rush a fortress becomes an explicit, visible choice with unrest consequences. (2) **Standing supply routes**: automated recurring transfers between owned planets using trade fleets, including an "export overflow" mode that ships surplus before storage-service spoilage destroys it.
- **Engine hook:** New order types `LOGI_SET_PRIORITY { planetId, split }` and `LOGI_STANDING_ROUTE { fromPlanetId, toPlanetId, resource, mode: 'fixed' | 'overflow' }` handled in scripts/game-loop.ts; a new lib/logistics/distribution-directive pass runs before the storage pass and consumes `trade_fleet` capacity (ShipType `trade_fleet`, lib/construction/construction-types.ts). New building `distribution_hub` (category `logistics`) raising `planet.logistics.efficiency`.
- **Required capacity:** Standing routes consume real trade-fleet hulls, which must be built and can be raided or blockaded on the hyperlane graph. Priority splits only matter on planets with freight track level 2+; a world with no freight terminals has nothing to prioritize.
- **Interactions:** Direct prerequisite of Automated Ports. Overflow-export routes interact with storage pressure and the seasons TRADE/PRODUCTION scoring. Blockades (`planet.blockade`) sever standing routes, making the blockade mechanic bite harder against logistics-dependent empires.
- **Strategic advantages:** Turns a multi-planet empire from a set of isolated stockpiles into one economy; construction-priority rushing is the fastest legal way to compress build timelines before automation exists.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Efficiency is dependence. Empires running lean stockpiles with overflow exports are catastrophically exposed to hyperlane interdiction — one raided convoy line and three worlds miss upkeep. Civilian-last priority splits generate steady unrest and hostile press.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Convoy-raiding as a deliberate economic-warfare doctrine; "breadbasket" worlds whose standing routes feed half an empire and whose capture is worth more than a fleet battle.

### Mass Driver Arrays (`infra_mass_driver`)
- **Domain:** infrastructure | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** infra_planetary_grid, mat_advanced_composites
- **Concept:** Kilometers-long electromagnetic accelerators that fling cargo pods into orbit for the marginal cost of electricity — brutally efficient for freight, instantly lethal to anything fragile, including passengers.
- **What becomes possible:** Bulk surface-to-orbit lift decoupled from rocketry and trade fleets. And, unavoidably, a gun the size of a mountain range.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Cheap bulk lift with a dual-use dilemma.** The mass driver moves bulk resources (metals, chemicals, composites, food — never manpower or population) from surface to the planet's orbital warehouses without spending trade-fleet capacity, powered by grid energy. The same installation can fire kinetic slugs: at ships blockading the planet (a surface-based anti-blockade weapon), or — with a war declared — at another planet's surface as crude, inaccurate bombardment whose collateral damage the press system reports in full.
- **Engine hook:** New building `mass_driver_array` (category `space`, `uniquePerPlanet: true`, high energy upkeep, `techRequired: 'infra_mass_driver'`). Orders `MD_LAUNCH_CARGO { planetId, resource, qty }` (surface stores → orbital stores, bypassing the lift component of logistics), `MD_FIRE_ORBITAL { planetId }` (adds defensePower against blockading fleets for one combat pass), and `MD_KINETIC_STRIKE { planetId, targetPlanetId }` (bombardment via the siege/bombardment path in lib/combat/, with a guaranteed collateral event fed to the press system and a legitimacy cost in the government layer).
- **Required capacity:** Requires `power_grid` track 3+ and a standing orbital station to receive cargo; firing modes need ammo upkeep. The array occupies a surface sector and cannot be hidden — every faction with sensor coverage knows you have one, and what it could do.
- **Interactions:** Bridges the gap before Orbital Elevator; feeds Orbital Industry's foundries with raw mass. `MD_FIRE_ORBITAL` interacts with the blockade system as the only surface-based counter. Kinetic strikes interact with press propagation and parliamentary opposition — bombardment without parliamentary sanction burns political capital.
- **Strategic advantages:** Lift cost near zero for bulk goods; a fortress world with a mass driver cannot be cheaply blockaded; the deterrent value of a visible planetary gun is itself diplomacy.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Building one changes how neighbors treat you — it is a strategic weapon in every rival's threat assessment whether you ever fire it or not, and espionage AI (which is live) will target it. Kinetic strikes are war crimes in the press's vocabulary: expect legitimacy loss, faction-wide unrest ticks, and coalition-forming against you. The array itself is a single high-value sector: one successful strike or sabotage removes both your lift capacity and your deterrent at once.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Arms-control diplomacy over mass drivers; first-strike incentives against known arrays in the opening hours of a war; pariah runs where a player accepts the press storm and uses kinetic strikes as their primary siege tool.

## Tier 2 — Specialization

### Automated Ports (`infra_automated_ports`)
- **Domain:** infrastructure | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** infra_logistics_network, comp_distributed_compute
- **Concept:** Container terminals, marshalling yards, and orbital docks run end-to-end by machine scheduling — no shift changes, no fatigue, no dockworkers. The first place most civilizations meet mass technological unemployment.
- **What becomes possible:** Freight that moves at machine tempo around the clock, through strikes, plagues, and curfews alike.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Port automation and the displaced-labor ledger.** Converting a world's freight layer to automated operation zeroes the manpower component of its freight track upkeep and sharply raises `planet.logistics.efficiency` — and moves every eliminated job into a new per-planet **displaced labor** pool. Displaced labor generates unrest each tick until absorbed by new construction demand, retraining spending (a recurring credits cost enacted through parliament), or emigration. Automation is a loan against social stability, and this tech opens the account.
- **Engine hook:** New building `automated_port` (category `logistics`, upgradesFrom a freight-terminal-line building, manpower upkeep 0, energy upkeep high). New `Planet` field `displacedLabor: number` (lib/construction/construction-types.ts) with a game-loop tick pass converting it to unrest pressure, modifiable by a parliament welfare policy (`UNLOCK_POLICY` effect `retraining_programs`). Order `PORT_AUTOMATE { planetId }` performs the conversion through the order queue.
- **Required capacity:** Freight track 3+, `power_grid` 3+, and an active distributed-compute presence (the scheduling has to run somewhere). The retraining policy needs treasury income; automating during a credit crunch means eating the unrest raw.
- **Interactions:** Direct prerequisite of Orbital Elevator and Lights-Out Manufacturing, which both deepen the displaced-labor mechanic. Automated ports keep working during organized-labor strikes (see Arcology Manufactories), during sieges, and under unrest that would idle manned facilities — a deliberate asymmetry.
- **Strategic advantages:** Manpower freed from freight flows into armies and factories; logistics efficiency compounds every other system on the planet; strike-proof and plague-proof throughput is decisive in long wars.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** The displaced-labor pool is real and patient. Unabsorbed, it feeds unrest, gives rebellion and secession events fuel, and hands opposition parliament factions a permanent grievance. Automated ports are also software: they join the espionage attack surface, and a successful port-systems op idles a planet's entire distribution layer at once — something no strike of human dockworkers could ever achieve.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Welfare-state versus iron-fist responses to displacement diverging faction identities; luddite rebellion flavors on high-displacement worlds; the empire-wide automation debate that Tier 3 and 4 force to a conclusion.

### Orbital Industry (`mat_orbital_industry`)
- **Domain:** materials | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** mat_advanced_composites, space_orbital_logistics
- **Concept:** Microgravity and hard vacuum permit manufacturing impossible under an atmosphere: void-cast foamed alloys, flawless crystal growth, kilometer-scale structures assembled without ever bearing their own weight.
- **What becomes possible:** A second industrial base above the clouds — and the first goods in your economy that no surface factory can make at any price.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Orbit-exclusive production.** Orbital foundries produce **microgravity fabrications**, an intermediate good required by capital-ship hulls (shipyard tier 3), Tier 3+ orbital structures, and the orbital elevator's counterweight stage. Surface industry cannot substitute. The player's heavy shipbuilding and megaproject capability now physically lives in orbit, where it must be defended — orbital combat and blockades stop being about a distant support layer and start being about the heart of your industry.
- **Engine hook:** New orbital structure `orbital_foundry` (lib/orbital/orbital-types.ts, category extended or `logistics`, `requiresStation: true`, `techRequired: 'mat_orbital_industry'`). New effect id `ORBITAL_INDUSTRY_EFFECT` surfaced as `OrbitalRatings.industryOutput`; `microgravity_fabs` joins the storable set stored in orbital warehouses, subject to `blockade.orbitalStoresCut`.
- **Required capacity:** An active orbital station, composites flowing up the gravity well (trade fleets, mass driver, or elevator), and infrastructure to service the orbit from the ground (`infrastructureRequired` on the structure). Knowledge without lift capacity is a foundry with no feedstock.
- **Interactions:** Prerequisite of Molecular Manufacturing and Orbital Elevator. Consumes mass-driver cargo throughput; multiplies the value of orbital defense structures; a rival's `orbitControlLost` now also means their capital shipbuilding is gone.
- **Strategic advantages:** Gates the top of the military tree behind your own orbital security; foundry worlds project shipbuilding power; exports of microgravity fabs to orbit-poor factions are lucrative and politically potent.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Concentration in the most bombardable real estate you own. A blockade now cuts your capital-ship pipeline, not just trade; losing orbital control means watching your industrial crown jewels burn from the ground. Surface industry lobbies (and their parliament blocs) resent the investment drain skyward.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Wars fought entirely for orbital supremacy over foundry worlds; "orbital aristocracy" social stratification stories; the elevator becoming existential rather than convenient.

### Distributed Microfabrication (`mat_swarm_fabrication`)
- **Domain:** materials | **Tier:** 2 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `industrial_organization`
- **Prerequisites:** mat_advanced_composites, comp_distributed_compute
- **Concept:** Industry dissolved into the city: thousands of small networked fabrication cells embedded in every district, coordinated by distributed compute, each individually trivial and collectively a heavy industry.
- **What becomes possible:** A planet with no industrial heartland to bomb — production is everywhere, so it is nowhere in particular.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Dispersed industry with a damage floor.** Microfab cells are cheap, buildable in any district type including civilian, and spread production across the 64-sector board. Planetary industrial output gains a hard resilience property: no single bombardment pass, sabotage op, or sector loss can remove more than a small fraction of it. In exchange, this branch permanently caps heavy centralized industry — the heavy-industry building line is locked and planetary `shipyardTier` contribution caps at 2. You are choosing to be unkillable and mid-weight.
- **Engine hook:** New building `microfab_cell` (allowedDistricts `['any']`, low output, low cost, modest manpower upkeep — this branch *keeps* industrial employment). unlockFlag `dispersed_industry` read by the bombardment/siege damage pass in lib/combat/siege/ to apply the per-strike industrial damage cap; tech's locked group removes `heavy_industry_complex` upgrades from the build menu.
- **Required capacity:** Comms track 3+ (the cells are a network before they are factories) and broad district availability — this branch rewards wide, built-out surface boards and punishes cramped worlds.
- **Interactions:** Synergizes with Self-Assembling Infrastructure (dispersed repair) and with defensive doctrines; anti-synergizes with Orbital Industry ambitions since your surface can't feed a capital shipyard's appetite alone. Employment-positive: partially offsets displaced labor from Automated Ports.
- **Strategic advantages:** Near-immunity to industrial decapitation; sieges against you grind; your economy degrades gracefully where rivals' economies snap.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** A permanent ceiling on heavy industry — no domestically produced capital hulls without imported microgravity fabs, leaving your battleline dependent on orbital foundries or allies. Distributed compute coordination is itself an espionage target: a successful network op briefly de-coordinates the swarm, and dispersed industry is impossible to air-gap.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Turtle civilizations that outlast wars they cannot win in the field; import dependence on capital hulls shaping alliance politics; urban-industrial culture with no factory towns to strike.

### Arcology Manufactories (`mat_industrial_arcologies`)
- **Domain:** materials | **Tier:** 2 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `industrial_organization`
- **Prerequisites:** mat_advanced_composites, plan_closed_ecologies
- **Concept:** The factory-city as a single sealed organism: kilometer-high arcologies with closed ecological life support, housing workers, industry, and everything between under one roof.
- **What becomes possible:** Industrial output per hectare that dispersed civilization cannot approach — and cities that keep breathing when the planet outside cannot.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Multi-sector megabuildings and organized labor.** The Industrial Arcology is the game's first multi-sector footprint building, occupying 2-3 adjacent sectors on the surface board and combining massive industrial output with habitat capacity. Its closed ecology keeps it producing through sieges, blockades, and environmental collapse. Concentrating tens of millions of workers creates **organized labor** as a recurring political force: strike events that idle the arcology, demands routed through parliament, and a standing choice between concession (credits, policy) and suppression (unrest, press coverage, radicalization).
- **Engine hook:** New building `industrial_arcology` with a `footprintSectors: number` extension to `BuildingDefinition`/`PlanetTile` placement (lib/construction/construction-types.ts — tiles claim adjacent `sectorIndex` values). unlockFlag `closed_ecology_industry` exempts arcology output from siege attrition and blockade food pressure. New event stream `organized_labor` in the game loop feeding parliament motions and the press system.
- **Required capacity:** Water and power_grid tracks 4+, food security for the sealed ecology's spin-up, and contiguous free sectors — a cluttered surface board may have no room. Closed-ecology hardware comes from the plan_closed_ecologies line; the knowledge without it is a tower that suffocates under siege.
- **Interactions:** Strike events interact with Automated Ports (automated freight keeps moving during strikes — a reason unions hate your ports) and with the press and government systems. Arcology housing absorbs displaced labor, partially answering the automation problem by re-employing it — this branch's quiet virtue.
- **Strategic advantages:** The highest surface output density in the game; siege-proof cores that make conquering your worlds a matter of storming towers; population capacity that feeds armies.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** A 3-sector target containing a tenth of the planet's population: orbital strike doctrine against arcologies is a mass-casualty event with press and legitimacy shockwaves — for both sides. Organized labor never goes away; every concession raises the next demand, every suppression radicalizes, and a general strike during a war is a strategic event. Losing one arcology loses more than most planets.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Union political parties entering parliament; hostage-city dynamics in war termination talks; company-state identity where the state and the arcology administration blur.

### Chartered Industry (`mat_charter_manufacturing`)
- **Domain:** materials | **Tier:** 2 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `industrial_organization`
- **Prerequisites:** mat_advanced_composites, econ_interstellar_banking
- **Concept:** The state stops running factories and starts chartering them: industrial development leased to charter corporations that raise their own capital, build at their own pace, and pay royalties on everything.
- **What becomes possible:** Industrialization that costs the treasury nothing and runs itself — owned, in every sense that matters, by someone else.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Leased industrial districts with autonomous corporate expansion.** The player charters industrial districts to charter corporations: the corp pays construction and upkeep, auto-builds and upgrades industry in its leased sectors without occupying the player's build queue, and remits credit royalties every tick. In exchange each corp accumulates **influence** — a shadow currency that buys parliament pressure, resists unfavorable policy, and grows with its holdings. Nationalizing a charter later is always possible and always expensive: a large political-capital spend, a market-confidence shock to credit income, and a hostile press cycle.
- **Engine hook:** New order types `CORP_CHARTER_DISTRICT { planetId, sectorIndex, corpId }` and `CORP_NATIONALIZE { corpId }` in scripts/game-loop.ts; corp auto-build runs as a game-loop pass writing into the normal `BuildOrder` pipeline with a corp-owned flag. Corp influence integrates with the government system's political-capital and parliament mechanics; suborning a corporation joins the espionage op catalog as a new op targeting rival charter states.
- **Required capacity:** A functioning credit economy (interstellar banking) and worlds attractive enough to invest in — corps decline charters on low-stability, blockaded, or resource-poor planets. The state keeps needing tax administration; royalties are income, not control.
- **Interactions:** Corp royalties feed the seasons WEALTH scoring; corp influence collides with every government-system mechanic from cabinet appointments to coup risk; rival espionage can flip your largest corp into a fifth column. Corp-owned industry ignores your distribution priorities — they ship for profit, not for your war.
- **Strategic advantages:** Fastest industrial expansion per credit of state treasury in the game; the build queue stays free for military and megaprojects; a rich private sector deepens your credit and trade scores.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have created power you do not command. Corps resist wars that hurt trade, fund parliament factions, and — suborned — become the enemy's hands inside your economy. Nationalization is a one-way political explosion. In a civil war or secession crisis, chartered worlds follow the money, not the flag.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Corporate-state fusion identities; megacorp-vs-crown succession crises; espionage campaigns waged entirely through corporate proxies.

## Tier 3 — Dominance

### Orbital Elevator (`infra_orbital_elevator`)
- **Domain:** infrastructure | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** mat_orbital_industry, infra_automated_ports
- **Concept:** A tether from equatorial anchor to counterweight beyond geostationary orbit, climbed by continuous freight cars. It converts the gravity well from a wall into a road — and stakes a civilization's fortunes on a single cable.
- **What becomes possible:** Surface-to-orbit throughput measured like rail freight, not rocketry. Whole populations, armies, and industries flowing up and down at will.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Unlimited lift, single point of failure.** The elevator is a staged megaproject (anchor, ribbon, counterweight — each a distinct construction phase consuming composites and microgravity fabs). Complete, it removes the surface-orbit throughput bottleneck entirely: orbital stores never cut off from blockade severity below full investment, trade routes skip lift costs, and — uniquely — *population* moves cheaply to orbit, enabling mass evacuation and orbital-habitat settlement. It is also the single most consequential sabotage target in the game: a severed ribbon wraps the planet, ruining a wedge-line of surface sectors, killing populations, and cratering stability empire-wide as the press carries the images.
- **Engine hook:** New megaproject pattern: building `orbital_elevator` (`uniquePerPlanet: true`, category `space`) with staged construction via order `CON_MEGAPROJECT_STAGE { planetId, projectId, stage }`. Completion sets planet flag suppressing `blockade.orbitalStoresCut` below a severity threshold and unlocks order `EVAC_POPULATION { planetId, destination }`. New catastrophe event `ribbon_fall`: marks a wedge of the 8-ring × 8-wedge surface board `ruined`, population loss, faction-wide stability shock; wired as the failure outcome of a new top-tier espionage sabotage op and of orbital bombardment against the counterweight.
- **Required capacity:** Transit and freight tracks 4+, an active orbital station, mass composites and microgravity-fab stockpiles, and years of build time. Not constructible on `megaplanet` types (the tether mass closes over the material limit). Defense of the counterweight requires standing orbital defenses — an undefended elevator is an invitation.
- **Interactions:** Multiplies Orbital Industry (feedstock at rail prices) and mega_orbital_habitats (population lift). The evacuation order is the practical mechanism behind late-game planet abandonment. Its existence changes rival espionage target priority and your own counterintelligence posture permanently.
- **Strategic advantages:** The elevator world becomes the logistical heart of an empire — capital-ship yards fed at trivial cost, armies staged to orbit in days, blockade-resistant trade. Prestige weight in seasons scoring.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have built the single target whose destruction hurts you most, and everyone knows its coordinates. Counterweight defense becomes a permanent fleet commitment; every war you enter includes the question "can they reach the ribbon?" Insurance, in the form of redundant mass drivers, is expensive and admits the fear.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Ribbon-fall atrocities as war-defining press events; elevator worlds as de facto capitals; terrorist-cell espionage plots targeting the anchor city; treaties specifically protecting elevators as civilian infrastructure.

### Programmable Metamaterials (`mat_metamaterials`)
- **Domain:** materials | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** mat_advanced_composites, phys_directed_energy
- **Concept:** Materials whose microstructure is engineered to do the impossible with energy: surfaces that bend sensor returns around themselves, armor that stiffens in response to directed-energy heating, cladding that manages heat like a living skin.
- **What becomes possible:** Matter that participates in the fight — hulls that argue with sensors and buildings that shrug at orbital fire.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Two doors. (1) **Signature management**: fleets can run dark, masking themselves from the sensor-relay network at the cost of disconnecting from command networks (no mil_command_networks coordination bonuses while masked) — a genuine stealth-versus-coordination decision each deployment. (2) **Hardened cladding**: surface buildings and orbital structures can be refitted with adaptive plating that blunts orbital-strike and bombardment damage in exchange for permanently higher energy upkeep — grid capacity becomes armor.
- **Engine hook:** Order `FLEET_SIGNATURE_MASK { fleetId, on }` handled in the movement/sensor layer (interacts with `sensor_relay` ships and `OrbitalRatings.sensorStrength`); masked fleets excluded from command-network modifier application. Order `CON_REFIT_CLADDING { planetId, tileId }` adds a `hardened` modifier read by the siege/bombardment damage pass; upkeep delta drawn from the planetary grid.
- **Required capacity:** Cladding refits demand grid headroom (`power_grid` 4+ for wide deployment) and composites; signature masking requires refitted hulls (a per-fleet refit cost), so knowledge covers nothing already in the field.
- **Interactions:** Counterplays mil_orbital_strike doctrine directly; synergizes with espionage insertion (masked couriers improve op infiltration in lib/espionage/); tension with Unified Planetary Grid load management — armor that browns out cities under strain is its own dilemma.
- **Strategic advantages:** Blunts the dominant Tier 3 offensive doctrines; masked fleet movements restore surprise to a sensor-saturated midgame; hardened elevators and arcologies protect exactly the assets this domain makes you dependent on.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Energy dependence deepens — a grid sabotage now also degrades your armor. Masked fleets fighting without command coordination lose engagements they'd win connected, tempting commanders into the worst of both. Rivals answer with sensor investment, ratcheting an detection arms race that consumes research both sides wanted elsewhere.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Cat-and-mouse deep-space campaigns off the hyperlane graph; strike doctrines pivoting to logistics targets when hardened cores stop being worth the ammunition; black-budget scandals when masked fleet losses surface in the press.

### Molecular Manufacturing (`mat_molecular_manufacturing`)
- **Domain:** materials | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** mat_orbital_industry, comp_machine_intelligence
- **Concept:** Programmable assemblers building products atom by atom from feedstock — the point where "what do you make" becomes a software question and matter becomes approximately fungible.
- **What becomes possible:** A forge that reads a file and produces anything: alloys, pharmaceuticals, machine parts, rare-earth substitutes. Scarcity of *kind* ends; scarcity of *amount* and *energy* remains.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Matter compilation.** Nanoforges convert any stored resource into any other at lossy, energy-priced exchange ratios — metals into chemicals, bulk anything into small amounts of rares. Stockpile composition stops being destiny: a blockaded world compiles what it cannot import. The systemic consequence is deliberate: compiled commodities depress market prices, and extraction-specialized worlds watch their reason to exist evaporate — a new *economic obsolescence* pressure that turns mining worlds into unemployment crises with secession risk.
- **Engine hook:** New building `nanoforge` (districts `industrial`/`research`, extreme energy upkeep, `techRequired`). Order `NANO_COMPILE { planetId, from, to, qty }` executes conversions against storage-service stockpiles using a conversion matrix in a new lib/economy/compilation config; the economy price pass discounts compiled resource classes faction-wide. A game-loop check flags extraction-specialized planets (SpecializationState) with high compile exposure for the displaced-labor/unrest pipeline.
- **Required capacity:** Grid track 5 on the host world — a nanoforge draws like a city. Feedstock still has to exist and arrive: compilation moves scarcity to logistics and energy, it does not abolish it. Machine-intelligence oversight capacity (a compute-building presence) gates forge count per planet.
- **Interactions:** Root of the entire Tier 4 fork. Interlocks with trade (why import what you compile?), with espionage (theft of this tech is the single highest-value op in the catalog — a thief skips a tier of prerequisites), and with the government system as obsolete mining worlds turn politically volatile.
- **Strategic advantages:** Blockade resistance approaching immunity for supplied-and-powered worlds; rares independence; the strategic initiative that comes from an economy that can re-spec itself in days.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have made your own mining sector obsolete — the displacement crisis this time is not a district's dockworkers but entire planets, with secession and civil-war fuel attached. Market depression cuts trade income and TRADE season scoring. Proliferation is existential: if espionage exfiltrates your compiler designs, every consequence above happens to the whole galaxy at once, and your monopoly rents with it.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Mining-world rust-belt rebellions; compiler nonproliferation diplomacy; the Common Forge / Licensed Matter schism at Tier 4.

### Lights-Out Manufacturing (`infra_autonomous_factories`)
- **Domain:** infrastructure | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** infra_automated_ports, comp_machine_intelligence
- **Concept:** Factories that need no lights because nothing inside has eyes: machine-intelligence production lines that self-schedule, self-maintain, and never stop. Automation graduates from the docks to the whole industrial economy.
- **What becomes possible:** Industry decoupled from population — output that no strike, plague, siege attrition, or conscription wave can touch.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Per-building automation conversion, at social cost.** The player converts individual industrial buildings to autonomous variants tile by tile: manpower upkeep drops to zero, energy upkeep rises, and output continues through strikes, sieges, and unrest events that idle manned industry. Every conversion adds its former workforce to the planet's displaced-labor pool — the account opened by Automated Ports, now drawing at scale. Simultaneously, every autonomous line joins the espionage attack surface: hijacked factories can have output diverted, sabotaged, or turned into industrial accidents by rival ops.
- **Engine hook:** Order `IND_AUTOMATE { planetId, tileId }` sets an `automated` flag on the tile; the economy upkeep pass swaps manpower for energy and the siege/strike passes exempt flagged tiles. `displacedLabor` accrual reuses the Automated Ports pipeline. New espionage op `factory_hijack` added to the lib/espionage/ catalog targeting planets by automated-tile count — more automation, more surface.
- **Required capacity:** Grid 4+, a machine-intelligence compute presence on-world, and the political capacity to absorb displacement: without funded retraining policy or arcology/emigration absorption, each conversion is a direct unrest purchase. Conversions are per-tile and reversible only at full rebuild cost — automation is a commitment.
- **Interactions:** Compounds with every industrial-organization branch differently: swarm cells automate cheaply but multiply attack surface; arcologies automating triggers the mother of all labor confrontations; chartered corps automate on their own schedule whether you like it or not. Feeds Self-Assembling Infrastructure and both Tier 4 branches.
- **Strategic advantages:** War economy immune to manpower shocks; conscription no longer competes with production; output continuity under siege converts long defensive wars from attrition losses into stalemates you win.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** The endgame of the displacement arc: worlds where most of the population is economically unnecessary and knows it. Unrest, radical parliament factions, rebellion risk, and a legitimacy question no stability modifier fully answers. A coordinated factory-hijack campaign in wartime can turn your strike-proof economy against you in a single night.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Post-work societies drifting toward the Common Forge; automation-tax politics; AI-hijack false-flag operations blaming rival factions for industrial disasters.

## Tier 4 — Transformation

### Self-Assembling Infrastructure (`infra_self_assembling_infrastructure`)
- **Domain:** infrastructure | **Tier:** 4
- **Prerequisites:** mat_molecular_manufacturing, comp_autonomous_administration
- **Concept:** Construction as ecology: assembler swarms seeded onto a site that grow infrastructure the way coral grows reef — consuming stockpiled matter, following a blueprint, repairing damage as a reflex rather than a decision.
- **What becomes possible:** Cities that heal. War damage that undoes itself. Building as an act of *planting* rather than managing.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Queue-free seeded construction and autonomous repair.** Seeded swarms construct buildings and infrastructure track levels without occupying the build queue, drawing resources directly from planetary stores at their own pace — construction parallelism limited only by stockpile and oversight, not queue slots. Standing swarms auto-repair ruined tiles and damaged orbital structures every tick, including sectors ruined by bombardment or ribbon-fall. The counterweight is the **gray bloom**: swarms operating beyond the planet's machine-oversight capacity risk a replication fault that consumes sectors instead of building them — a spreading crisis the player must contain by sacrificing the bloomed sectors or spending emergency compute.
- **Engine hook:** Order `CON_SEED_SWARM { planetId, blueprint | tileId }` bypassing `buildQueue` and debiting storage-service stockpiles per tick; a repair pass over `constructionState: 'ruined'` tiles and damaged `OrbitalSlot`s. New crisis event `gray_bloom` in the game loop, probability scaled by (active swarms − oversight capacity from comp buildings via comp_autonomous_administration); containment via order `SWARM_PURGE { planetId, sectorIndex }` which ruins the sector deliberately.
- **Required capacity:** Nanoforge feedstock economics (molecular manufacturing deployed, not just known), autonomous-administration oversight infrastructure on every seeded world, and deep stockpiles — swarms eat matter at rates that empty unprepared warehouses and starve everything else on the distribution network.
- **Interactions:** Repairs make Distributed Microfabrication's dispersed board effectively unkillable and give Arcology worlds a path back from strike catastrophes; ribbon-fall recovery drops from decades to seasons. Blooms interact with the press (nothing photographs worse) and with rival espionage — a bloom *induced* by sabotage is indistinguishable from an accident.
- **Strategic advantages:** Reconstruction ceases to be a postwar era and becomes a background process; parallel construction lets a mobilizing empire outbuild anyone still queueing; scorched-earth strategies stop working against you.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have released self-replicating builders into your own biosphere and must fund their supervision forever. Oversight-capacity loss — a compute strike, an administration crisis — turns your construction fleet into a latent hazard on every developed world simultaneously. Bloomed sectors are total losses, and blooms on rivals' borders read as weapons tests whatever you intended.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Bloom-scarred exclusion zones as permanent map features; assembler-warfare treaties; the quiet realization that planets no longer strictly need their populations to run — feeding both Tier 4 fabrication ideologies below.

### The Common Forge (`mat_fabrication_commons`)
- **Domain:** materials | **Tier:** 4 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `fabrication_endstate`
- **Prerequisites:** mat_molecular_manufacturing, soc_digital_governance
- **Concept:** Molecular fabrication declared a public utility: a compiler in every district, patterns in the civic commons, material want abolished as deliberately as smallpox. The end of the labor question by dissolving the economy that asked it.
- **What becomes possible:** Post-scarcity as policy. Citizens fabricate what they need; employment, wages, and consumer markets become historical curiosities.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Post-scarcity transition.** Communal fabricators in civilian districts flip the planet to a post-scarcity regime: the displaced-labor pool is abolished permanently (displacement unrest ends, on every world so converted), happiness gains a high floor, and immigration pull rises sharply. The costs are structural, not cosmetic: credit tax income from converted worlds collapses to a fraction, trade routes touching them wither (with matching TRADE season-score loss), and charter corporations — if your Tier 2 was Chartered Industry, especially — turn from partners into enemies, funding opposition factions and coup risk through the government and espionage systems. You are trading the entire market economy for permanent social peace.
- **Engine hook:** New building `communal_fabricator` (civilian districts). Planet flag `post_scarcity` read by: the displaced-labor pass (pool zeroed and closed), the economy tax pass (income multiplier collapse), the trade pass (route value decay), the migration system (pull bonus), and the government layer (parliament realignment event; corp hostility modifier). Conversion is per-planet via order `FAB_COMMONS_DECLARE { planetId }` — an empire can be post-scarcity in its core and mercantile at its rim, and that contradiction generates its own politics.
- **Required capacity:** Nanoforge coverage and grid capacity on every converted world, digital-governance infrastructure to administer the commons, and a treasury that can survive losing its tax base — or an empire willing to keep some worlds unconverted as revenue colonies, with everything that implies.
- **Interactions:** Resolves the displacement arc that Automated Ports opened and Lights-Out Manufacturing brought to crisis. Anti-synergizes catastrophically with Chartered Industry; harmonizes with arcology welfare politics and with soc digital participation. Post-scarcity worlds are nearly rebellion-proof — but a *blockaded* post-scarcity world that loses fabricator feedstock suffers unrest whiplash beyond anything a market world experiences.
- **Strategic advantages:** Unmatched stability and legitimacy; population growth and immigration; total immunity to automation unrest and most economic warfare; a moral position the press systems of every faction must engage with.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Fiscal starvation — fleets and armies still cost credits your converted core no longer produces, forcing dependence on rim-world revenue, resource exports, or allied subsidy. Corporate and mercantile factions inside and outside your borders now have existential reasons to see the experiment fail. Feedstock supply becomes the single thread civilization hangs by, and your rivals know it.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Revenue-colony resentment inverting the old politics (the rim subsidizing the utopian core); ideological cold wars as your commons broadcasts its existence; migration crises as rival populations demand what your citizens have.

### Licensed Matter (`mat_sealed_foundries`)
- **Domain:** materials | **Tier:** 4 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `fabrication_endstate`
- **Prerequisites:** mat_molecular_manufacturing, comm_quantum_secure
- **Concept:** Molecular fabrication as sovereign property: every compiler cryptographically sealed, every pattern licensed, every act of creation metered and billed. Scarcity retained — deliberately, profitably — as an instrument of rule.
- **What becomes possible:** The most valuable monopoly in history, enforced by quantum-secure licensing no forger can break — only steal.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Fabrication monopoly and export leverage.** Sealed foundries generate enormous per-compile credit royalties and steady political capital (patronage flows to whoever controls the seals). The signature mechanic is **licensed export**: selling metered fabrication rights to other factions creates formal dependence relationships — client factions gain compile capacity, you gain a revocation lever (`commitment trap` in the tech grammar) whose pull instantly degrades their economy. Domestically, artificial scarcity in sight of abundance breeds black-market fabrication: an ongoing illicit-fab event chain that feeds unrest, DISRUPTION scoring against you, and organized crime for your espionage services to fight instead of rivals.
- **Engine hook:** Planet/faction flag `licensed_fabrication`; nanoforge compiles route royalties through the economy pass and political capital through the government layer. New order types `LIC_EXPORT { factionId, capacity }` and `LIC_REVOKE { factionId }` — revocation applies an economy debuff to the client and a diplomatic incident via the press system. Black-market event chain in the game loop scaled by unrest and inversely by esp counterintelligence investment; rival theft of the master patterns (a top-tier espionage op) permanently breaks the monopoly flag empire-wide.
- **Required capacity:** Quantum-secure comm infrastructure on every foundry world (the seals are cryptographic and must stay ahead of attack), enforcement capacity (espionage/counterintel budget), and clients — the export mechanic is worthless without factions willing to accept dependence, which your diplomacy must manufacture.
- **Interactions:** Perfect synergy with Chartered Industry (the corps become your licensing enforcers and share the rents) and with econ_automated_markets pricing. The revocation lever interacts with alliance and war systems as coercion short of force. Black markets interact with the espionage catalog in both directions — and every faction you license is now a theft vector aimed at your master patterns.
- **Strategic advantages:** The largest credit income stream in the late game; political capital generation that lubricates every government mechanism; peaceful hegemony over clients who calculated that dependence beat exclusion; funded fleets the Common Forge cannot match.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You are ruling hungry people who can see the food. Artificial scarcity is a standing legitimacy wound the press and rival infowar suites will press forever; black markets grow in exact proportion to your success. Everything rests on the seals: a single successful exfiltration of the master patterns — one op, one defector, one suborned client — and the monopoly, the royalties, the leverage, and the reason anyone tolerated you evaporate simultaneously.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** License-revocation wars; client-state revolts timed to pattern-theft attempts; a galaxy-spanning heist narrative with your foundry seals as the prize; the historical judgment, delivered by other factions' presses, of the civilization that ended scarcity and sold it back at a markup.
