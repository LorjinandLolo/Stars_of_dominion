# Technology Web — Planetary Engineering & Megastructures

Playing into this domain means treating the map itself as a product. Where other domains change what your civilization *does*, this one changes what your civilization *stands on*: sealed cities on toxic plains, warrens under mountain ranges, farms on the seafloor, a sky held up by a single superconducting ring at L1, and eventually land that was never part of any planet at all. Every tier asks the same escalating question — how much of your environment are you willing to make artificial, and what happens to the people who owe their air to the answer? The domain's payloads are overwhelmingly *where-you-can-exist* payloads: new buildable terrain on the 64-sector surface board, new population layers (undercities, benthic districts, orbital habitats, flotillas), and new persistent edits to the board itself. Almost every capability arrives welded to a dependence: life support that can be sabotaged, climate that must be actively maintained, a magnetosphere that can be switched off by an enemy, a ring whose debris falls on your own cities.

The identity this domain enables is the deepest in the web: it decides whether your civilization is a gardener, a moleman, or a nomad. The Tier 2 Settlement Doctrine fork commits you to living under open sky, inside hermetic shells, or off-planet entirely — three civilizations that colonize different worlds, fear different weapons, and grieve different losses. The Tier 4 fork answers the late-game question every spacefaring society eventually faces: is a planet a home to be perfected, or four billion trillion tonnes of feedstock? A gaia-path civilization ends the game defending gardens it can never abandon; a disassembly-path civilization ends it as a swarm of rings, statites and habitat fleets that owns no planets and cannot be conquered by taking any. Both are reachable through the same early techs, which is exactly the point.

## Tier 1 — Expansion

### Closed Ecologies (`plan_closed_ecologies`)
- **Domain:** Planetary Engineering | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** none (domain root; canonical anchor)
- **Concept:** Materially closed life-support loops — air, water and nutrient cycles recycled at better than 99% closure, the engineering lineage that runs from Biosphere 2 through station ECLSS systems. A settlement that recycles almost everything no longer cares what is outside the wall.
- **What becomes possible:** Sealed settlements on ground that would kill an unprotected colonist — frozen wastes, toxic basins, volcanic slopes — and the certification standard every later habitat, warren and station in this domain inherits.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Habitability stops being a veto and becomes a cost. Introduces the **life-support integrity** mechanic: sealed structures carry a loop rating; if their energy/chemicals upkeep goes unpaid, the population inside takes *casualties*, not just unhappiness — a categorically different failure mode from open housing.
- **Engine hook:** Two steps, because the block this tech bypasses does not exist yet. **(1) Build the gate.** `canBuildOnTile` (`lib/construction/construction-service.ts:20-73`) today validates only civilization, `techRequired`, tile emptiness, `infrastructureLevel`, resources, `allowedDistricts` and `uniquePerPlanet` — it has no terrain or habitability input, and `SurfaceSector.habitability` (`lib/planet-surface/types.ts:77`) has no consumers anywhere in `lib/`. The only terrain rule in the game is an ad-hoc one in the worker's `PLANET_CONSTRUCT_BUILDING` handler (`scripts/game-loop.ts:1449-1469`), which blocks ocean sectors by regex-matching the building id. Replace it with a shared check: resolve `PlanetTile.sectorIndex` (`lib/construction/construction-types.ts:106`) against the deterministic board produced by `generateSurface`/`computeSectorOccupancy` (`lib/planet-surface/generator.ts`, `occupancy.ts` — the same call the worker already makes for placement) and reject builds whose sector terrain or habitability falls below the building's requirement, on both worker and client. **(2) Bypass it.** New building `sealed_arcology` in `data/buildings.ts` carrying a new `ignoresHabitability: true` flag that exempts a building from the habitability half of that gate (terrain rules still apply); unlockFlag `closed_ecology_certified`; the worker's population tick in `scripts/game-loop.ts` gains a life-support solvency check for flagged buildings. Step 1 is also the prerequisite work for Benthic Settlement's `allowedTerrain` field.
- **Required capacity:** Knowledge alone does nothing. Deployment needs a powered grid (heavy energy upkeep), a chemicals supply line for loop makeup losses, and enough logistics capacity to deliver both to the frontier sector being sealed.
- **Interactions:** Prerequisite of nearly the whole domain. `ESP_SABOTAGE_FACILITY` against a sealed structure escalates from an economic op to a mass-casualty threat — a new consequence tier in the espionage op catalog, with matching press-system fallout for the perpetrator if attributed.
- **Strategic advantages:** Opens the hostile-terrain land rush a full era early: toxic, frozen and volcanic sectors on the surface board become claimable industrial ground while rivals still queue for plains.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Every sealed settlement is a hostage to its own machinery — blockade, grid failure or sabotage kills citizens. Sealed populations seed a distinct social group ("Sealed") in their region with lower baseline loyalty to open-sky governments; air rationing during shortages is a recurring press-crisis generator.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Arcology hostage crises during invasions; governments deliberately settling loyalists in sealed housing to keep them dependent; the Hermetic Doctrine's entire civilizational bet.

### Subterranean Engineering (`plan_subterranean_engineering`)
- **Domain:** Planetary Engineering | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** `mat_advanced_composites`
- **Concept:** Large-void tunneling with composite-lined bores, using rock overburden as free radiation shielding, thermal mass and armor, and shallow geothermal gradients for baseline heat and power.
- **What becomes possible:** Cities under the mountains — permanent settled volumes beneath the surface board, invisible from orbit and nearly immune to bombardment.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **undercity layer**. A new order excavates beneath a mountain, volcanic or frozen sector, adding subterranean building slots that exist in parallel with the surface slots of the same sector — a second, hidden board under the first.
- **Engine hook:** New order `PLANET_EXCAVATE_UNDERCITY` (order-queue pattern, handled beside `PLANET_CONSTRUCT_BUILDING`); undercity slots stored in world state keyed by sector index, respecting the deterministic-surface contract (the generated board is never mutated, overrides live in the synced world snapshot). `MIL_BOMBARD_PLANET` resolution applies ~75% damage mitigation to undercity structures; orbital sensor sweeps and `ESP_*` recon are the only ways an enemy learns what is down there.
- **Required capacity:** A qualifying sector (mountain/volcanic/frozen terrain), infrastructure track level 2+, sustained metals and manpower for the bore, and energy for ventilation once occupied.
- **Interactions:** Volcanic-sector undercities can later host the `mantle_tap` (see Tectonic Engineering). Hidden military industry pairs with espionage tech-obscuring play; during ground invasions, undercities function as a defender's redoubt — attacker ground-combat penalty until cleared.
- **Strategic advantages:** Bombard-proof industry and a war economy the enemy cannot count from orbit; fortress worlds that fight on after losing their skies.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Deep Ward populations are hard to police — unrest propagates out of sight of the press system and your own surveillance until it surfaces as organized militancy; evacuation is nearly impossible during disasters; geothermal tapping on volcanic sectors adds a seismic-event risk tick.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Secessionist undercities that hold out for whole seasons; buried weapons programs discovered by rival intel as diplomatic scandals; conquered planets with unconquered basements.

### Benthic Settlement (`plan_benthic_settlement`)
- **Domain:** Planetary Engineering | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** `plan_closed_ecologies`, `mat_advanced_composites`
- **Concept:** Pressure architecture and ocean thermal energy conversion (OTEC) — the temperature gradient between surface and deep water as a permanent power plant, and the water column itself as radiation shield, thermal buffer and food source.
- **What becomes possible:** Permanent settlement of the seafloor: aquaculture, pressure-hulled districts and power stations anchored in the one terrain type no one can build on today.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Ocean sectors become buildable.** Oceanic-archetype planets flip from marginal colonies to prime real estate; on every world, the ocean share of the 64-sector board joins the economy.
- **Engine hook:** New buildings `benthic_habitat` (housing + food) and `otec_plant` (energy) in `data/buildings.ts`, carrying a new `allowedTerrain: ['ocean']` field checked by the shared terrain gate introduced with Closed Ecologies (the sector-resolving check in `canBuildOnTile` that supersedes the worker's id-regex ocean block); unlockFlag `ocean_colonization`. Without that gate there is nothing for `allowedTerrain` to be read by — this tech does not ship standalone validation.
- **Interactions:** Climate Control's storm events and any later climate warfare hit coastal and benthic holdings hardest; benthic food surpluses feed sealed and orbital populations; deep-sea facilities are naturally hard targets for orbital strike doctrine but soft targets for espionage insertion.
- **Required capacity:** Ocean sectors on the target planet, closed-ecology certification (the hulls are sealed habitats), composites supply, and surface-side port logistics to move goods between the seafloor and the rest of the economy.
- **Strategic advantages:** On oceanic worlds this can double usable sector count overnight; OTEC energy is cheap, renewable and independent of the planetary grid's fuel logistics; large food export economies.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Benthic districts depend on surface links a blockade or storm can cut; their populations develop a distinct "Benthic" social identity whose loyalty drifts when surface governments treat them as extraction labor; rescue and policing at depth are slow and expensive.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Benthic separatism on ocean worlds; submarine smuggling economies invisible to orbital customs; the Hermetic Doctrine claiming the seafloor as naturally theirs.

## Tier 2 — Specialization

### Climate Control (`plan_climate_control`)
- **Domain:** Planetary Engineering | **Tier:** 2 (canonical anchor)
- **Prerequisites:** `plan_closed_ecologies`, `comp_distributed_compute`
- **Concept:** Management of a planet's radiative energy budget — albedo modification, stratospheric aerosols, greenhouse-gas dialing — which is less a machine than a continuous computation: models ingesting planet-wide sensor data and actuating thousands of small interventions per day.
- **What becomes possible:** Weather as policy. A planet's climate stops being a fact about the world and becomes a maintained state of your infrastructure.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **planetary climate target**: set a desired climate band per planet; each tick the worker nudges baseline habitability toward it and gradually converts marginal terrain at the edges (desert→plains, frozen fringe→plains) through the sector-override layer. Crucially, the state is *maintained, not achieved* — if the regulator network loses power or is destroyed, the planet drifts back, sector by sector.
- **Engine hook:** New order `PLANET_SET_CLIMATE_TARGET`; new building `climate_regulator` (array-style, several per planet, effectiveness integrity-scaled like orbital structures); terrain conversions written as JSON overrides keyed by sector index in world state, applied on top of the deterministic board on both worker and client; unlockFlag `climate_authority`.
- **Required capacity:** A `comp_distributed_compute`-class modeling capability, a regulator network covering the planet (multiple buildings across multiple regions), continuous grid energy, and infrastructure track 2+. One regulator on one continent does nothing.
- **Interactions:** Gate for Terraforming and Tectonic Engineering. Every regulator is an attack surface: `ESP_SABOTAGE_FACILITY` against climate infrastructure is a de facto weather weapon, with crop-failure and storm consequences landing on coastal/benthic sectors. Agriculture scheduling synergizes with seasons scoring in the PRODUCTION category.
- **Strategic advantages:** Reclaimed sectors are new economy from nothing; marginal worlds become breadbaskets; you choose which regions flourish — a patronage tool the parliament will notice.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** **Climate debt**: pushing a planet beyond its natural band accumulates instability that discharges as disasters. Populations in reclaimed sectors owe their homes to the government — if control lapses, the unrest is proportional to what was granted. Legitimacy becomes coupled to the weather report.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Climate sieges (destroy the regulators, wait a season); electoral politics fought over whose desert gets greened next; the Open Sky Compact.

### Orbital Habitats (`mega_orbital_habitats`)
- **Domain:** Megastructures | **Tier:** 2 (canonical anchor)
- **Prerequisites:** `plan_closed_ecologies`, `space_orbital_logistics`
- **Concept:** Rotating pressure vessels at civic scale — O'Neill cylinders and Stanford tori spun for gravity, running fully closed ecologies. Land stops being something you find and becomes something you manufacture.
- **What becomes possible:** Permanent civilian populations in orbit: people who are born, work, vote and die without ever standing on a planet.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Population off-world.** A new orbital structure category houses citizens in a planet's orbital layer, and population can migrate between surface and habitat. Population growth decouples from planet count — the first crack in the assumption that a civilization is its planets.
- **Engine hook:** New category `habitat` in `data/orbital-structures.ts` with structure `orbital_habitat`, carrying a new `populationCapacity` field folded into `computeOrbitalRatings` in `lib/orbital/orbital-service.ts`; new order `HAB_TRANSFER_POPULATION { planetId, direction, amount }` in the order queue; unlockFlag `orbital_population`.
- **Required capacity:** A space station on the planet (habitats are `requiresStation: true`), infrastructure track 3+ groundside to lift the mass, closed-ecology certification, and continuous food/volatiles imports — habitats farm little and buy much.
- **Interactions:** Foundation of the Voidborn Mandate, Nomad Flotillas, the Orbital Ring and Planetary Disassembly (you need somewhere to put people). Habitat populations count toward POPULATION season scoring. Charter corporations compete to operate habitats — revenue with corruption events attached.
- **Strategic advantages:** Grow past your planets' carrying capacity without terraforming a single world; population safe from ground invasion; a demographic reserve that survives losing the surface.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** A habitat is a hull. Orbital combat now has civilians in it: destroying an inhabited habitat is an atrocity event in the press system with a legitimacy shock for the attacker — and a temptation for anyone who wants your population gone without a ground war. Habitat dwellers form a "Spacer" social group whose interests diverge from groundsiders within a generation.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Overflow habitats above overcrowded capitals; siege dilemmas over whether to evacuate or defend the shells; the slow cultural exit of a species from its own gravity wells.

### Open Sky Compact (`plan_open_sky_compact`)
- **Domain:** Planetary Engineering | **Tier:** 2 — *Settlement Doctrine fork* (`mutuallyExclusiveGroup: plan_t2_settlement_doctrine`)
- **Prerequisites:** `plan_climate_control`
- **Concept:** A civilizational commitment, part policy and part creed: the species' destiny is under open sky, and worlds are to be healed toward habitability rather than walled out. Ecology as constitutional principle.
- **What becomes possible:** A society whose settlement pattern, politics and self-image are organized around making planets genuinely livable — and which measures its worth in reclaimed ground.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Biosphere trajectory**: planets holding their climate target gain slow, compounding planet-wide habitability growth, and populations in reclaimed sectors get a durable loyalty bonus ("the government gave us the sky"). Unlocks `PLANET_DEDICATE_PRESERVE` — dedicating wilderness regions as protected preserves that generate happiness and INFLUENCE-category season score instead of resources: deliberately unproductive land as a source of legitimacy.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `doctrine_open_sky` read by the climate tick (habitability compounding) and by loyalty computation for reclaimed-sector social groups; new order `PLANET_DEDICATE_PRESERVE { regionId }`; mutuallyExclusiveGroup lock via `lockedTechIds` per the HoI4-style hard-lock support in `PlayerTechState`.
- **Required capacity:** Working climate-regulator networks on the worlds you intend to heal; wilderness regions still un-built (preserves need something left to preserve); the political capital to forgo sealed-expansion shortcuts.
- **Interactions:** Halves the effective cost of the Terraforming and Gaia lines; synergizes with `bio_enviro_adaptation` (open-world colonists) and with press/influence play — preserves are propaganda that photographs itself.
- **Strategic advantages:** The strongest long-run population loyalty engine in the domain; terraformed worlds under this doctrine are the most stable territory in the game.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Your population resents sealed housing (`sealed_arcology` happiness penalty), slowing exploitation of hostile worlds; worlds you *cannot* heal become standing political grievances; you are structurally slower to expand than a hermetic rival and permanently dependent on climate infrastructure an enemy can target. Locks Hermetic Doctrine and Voidborn Mandate.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Green-legitimist governments that fall when a terraform stalls; wars framed as liberating worlds from "shell-dweller" occupation; the Gaia Transformation as national telos.

### Hermetic Doctrine (`plan_hermetic_doctrine`)
- **Domain:** Planetary Engineering | **Tier:** 2 — *Settlement Doctrine fork* (`mutuallyExclusiveGroup: plan_t2_settlement_doctrine`)
- **Prerequisites:** `plan_closed_ecologies`, `infra_planetary_grid`
- **Concept:** The opposite creed: the outside is weather, and weather is someone else's problem. Standardized sealed modules — arcology, warren, benthic hull — become the universal unit of settlement, stamped identically onto any world in any condition.
- **What becomes possible:** A shell civilization: terrain-agnostic, climate-indifferent, able to colonize a volcanic hellscape and a garden world with the same blueprints and the same enthusiasm.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Two doors, both consequences of treating settlement as a manufactured product rather than a construction project. (1) **Settlement stamping.** `PLANET_STAMP_SETTLEMENT` deploys a prefabricated sealed-colony module from stockpile onto any qualifying sector as a single order that resolves in one tick — no build queue, no survey, no local adaptation. Colonization stops being a matter of construction time and becomes a matter of *inventory*: modules are mass-produced in advance at a `module_yard` and warehoused, so the doctrine's real strategic verb is deciding how many worlds' worth of civilization to have sitting in a warehouse before anybody knows you want them. (2) **The countable doors.** `PLANET_SEAL_CITY` closes a sealed settlement: while sealed, nothing enters or leaves that sector — hostile agent insertion against it fails outright, press circulation and unrest propagation into and out of it stop, and the sector's own output and happiness decay every tick the doors stay shut. Sealing is the doctrine's answer to plague, insurrection, infiltration and inconvenient journalism alike, and the government must decide each time how long it can afford to hold its breath. Alongside these: sealed, subterranean and benthic structures build faster, cost less and gain a loop-integrity bonus, every archetype colonizes at full effectiveness, and the `arcology_spine` building becomes available — a vertical city in a single sector with habitat-block-scale manpower output an order of magnitude up, the densest population structure on any surface board.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `doctrine_hermetic`. New order `PLANET_STAMP_SETTLEMENT { planetId, sectorIndex }` handled beside `PLANET_CONSTRUCT_BUILDING` in the worker, drawing from a new faction-level `prefabModules` stockpile (produced by the new `module_yard` building, stored like any other resource) and writing a completed `sealed_arcology` straight onto the resolved sector — bypassing the build queue and, via `ignoresHabitability`, the habitability gate introduced with Closed Ecologies. New order `PLANET_SEAL_CITY { planetId, sectorIndex, on }` writing a per-sector `sealed` state into the world-state override layer, read by espionage insertion rolls in `lib/espionage/`, by press propagation, and by the unrest and production passes of the worker tick. Plus cost/speed modifiers on `ignoresHabitability` construction; new building `arcology_spine` (infrastructure 4, `allowedDistricts: ['urban','industrial']`); ground-combat resolver reads the doctrine flag for terrain morale modifiers (below).
- **Required capacity:** A unified planetary grid (sealed civilization is grid civilization), chemicals throughput for vat food, module yards and the warehouse capacity to keep their output on the shelf, and the willingness to let open-field agriculture die.
- **Interactions:** Natural partner of `soc_surveillance_state` — `PLANET_SEAL_CITY` is the physical half of what surveillance does informationally, and a sealed city under a surveillance grid is effectively airtight to both agents and stories. Synergizes with Subterranean Engineering and Benthic Settlement (its favored children); stamped modules are how a hermetic power converts a won war into settled ground before the peace treaty is signed.
- **Strategic advantages:** Colonize *anything*, immediately, including worlds no other doctrine can touch until Tier 3 — and, with a stocked warehouse, faster than any rival can react to, since the lead time was paid seasons ago. Immune to climate warfare: there is no climate inside. The arcology spine wins population races on hostile frontiers, and sealing turns a rebellious or infiltrated city into a problem you can simply pause.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Food becomes a chemical industry — a chemicals blockade is now a famine weapon. Standardization cuts both ways: every stamped colony is the same building, so a rival who penetrates one module holds the floor plan of all of them, and successful espionage against a stamped settlement carries a bonus against every other until the blueprint is revised at real cost. A city left sealed too long does not simply resume — prolonged seals compound into a durable grievance in that sector's social groups, and "the doors never reopened" is a secession argument. Sealed generations fight poorly under sky: your ground formations take a combat penalty in open terrain and gain one in urban/underground sectors, bending your whole military doctrine indoors. Climate and terraforming research lose political constituency (research-cost penalty on that line). Cohesion between hermit-cities erodes without deliberate investment. Locks Open Sky Compact and Voidborn Mandate.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Prefab stockpiles as a visible war warning — rivals counting your module yards; cities sealed "temporarily" for years and the politics of who holds the key; city-state particularism inside single planets; sieges decided by air-plant sabotage rather than armies; a species that slowly forgets what rain was for.

### Voidborn Mandate (`plan_voidborn_mandate`)
- **Domain:** Megastructures | **Tier:** 2 — *Settlement Doctrine fork* (`mutuallyExclusiveGroup: plan_t2_settlement_doctrine`)
- **Prerequisites:** `mega_orbital_habitats`, `space_orbital_logistics`
- **Concept:** The third answer: neither heal the worlds nor burrow into them — leave them. Planets are gravity wells with minerals at the bottom; the species' real home is the volume between them.
- **What becomes possible:** A civilization whose center of demographic and political gravity is in orbit, running its planets as staffed extraction sites rather than homes.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Habitat-primary society.** Habitat population growth accelerates and habitats gain formal political representation — a Habitat Caucus bloc seated in parliament with its own agenda. Unlocks the `extraction_colony` planet stance: a skeleton-crew operating mode with no unrest from low amenities and no population growth — planets as machines, not constituencies.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `doctrine_voidborn`; Habitat Caucus registered as a parliament bloc in the government system (same machinery as existing party blocs, fed by habitat population share); new planet stance `extraction_colony` alongside `PLANET_SET_SPECIALIZATION` handling in the worker; mutuallyExclusiveGroup lock as above.
- **Required capacity:** Substantial existing habitat capacity (the Mandate formalizes a migration already underway — a mostly-groundside civilization taking this doctrine gets a decade of turbulence, not a bonus), plus the orbital logistics to feed it.
- **Interactions:** Direct gate-greaser for Nomad Flotillas (cheaper, faster, politically frictionless) and Planetary Disassembly (your population already left). `soc_federal_autonomy` interacts sharply: habitats demand the same autonomy planets get.
- **Strategic advantages:** Your demographic core is unreachable by ground invasion; planet loss stops being civilization loss; extraction stances squeeze hostile worlds no one else can staff.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Groundside populations feel abandoned — planet-side loyalty penalty and elevated secession risk on your populous surface worlds, which may vote themselves out from under you. Spacer physiology: armies recruited from habitats fight at a penalty in planetary gravity, weakening your invasion arm permanently. Everything depends on orbital logistics; a lost station is a starving habitat. Locks Open Sky Compact and Hermetic Doctrine.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Surface secessions that your habitat parliament shrugs at; orbital dynasties ruling planets they have never visited; the nomadic exit.

## Tier 3 — Dominance

### Terraforming (`plan_terraforming`)
- **Domain:** Planetary Engineering | **Tier:** 3 (canonical anchor)
- **Prerequisites:** `plan_climate_control`, `phys_fusion_power`, `bio_enviro_adaptation`
- **Concept:** Full planetary conversion: changing atmospheric mass and composition, seeding hydrospheres by redirected comet impact, and deploying engineered pioneer biospheres — centuries of natural process compressed into decades by fusion-scale energy budgets.
- **What becomes possible:** Rewriting worlds. A frozen ball becomes a continental world; a poisoned one becomes merely arid; the map you were dealt stops being the map you play.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **terraform project**: a staged, multi-season megaproject that migrates a planet's archetype along defined paths (frozen→continental, arid→continental, toxic→arid; continental→gaia is Tier 4 only), progressively rewriting sector terrain and habitability. Each completed stage is a public world event with press coverage and TERRITORY/STABILITY season scoring.
- **Engine hook:** New order `PLANET_BEGIN_TERRAFORM { targetArchetype }` with a project record in world state ticked by the worker; terrain rewrites emitted as sector-index-keyed overrides on the deterministic board (never regenerating it — the client and worker stay in agreement by contract); comet-redirection stages require orbit control (`ORBIT_CONTROL_THRESHOLD` from `lib/orbital/orbital-types`). unlockFlag `terraforming_program`.
- **Required capacity:** A fusion-scale energy surplus sustained for the whole project, a full climate-regulator network, orbital control of the target planet, and `bio_enviro_adaptation`-line pioneer organisms. Blueprints without these are trivia.
- **Interactions:** Atmosphere-stripped worlds additionally require an Artificial Magnetosphere before any terraform can hold. The Open Sky Compact accelerates and stabilizes every stage; Tectonic Engineering lets you choose the coastlines you are about to create.
- **Strategic advantages:** Manufactures prime territory inside your own borders; a completed terraform is the single largest STABILITY and legitimacy dividend in the domain; it converts marginal systems into core systems.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Mid-terraform planets are climatically chaotic — elevated disaster ticks for seasons. Success destroys the adapted settlements that came before it: benthic districts drained, frozen-world undercities flooded, their populations displaced into militant social groups with long memories. The project is hostage to orbital blockade at every comet stage, and its energy bill can bankrupt a war effort.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Displacement rebellions on "successfully" terraformed worlds; terraform-stage deadlines driving desperate wars for orbit; rival factions racing to finish or to interrupt a conversion.

### Artificial Magnetosphere (`plan_artificial_magnetosphere`)
- **Domain:** Planetary Engineering | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** `phys_directed_energy`, `infra_planetary_grid`
- **Concept:** A superconducting dipole stationed at the planet–sun L1 point, deflecting the solar wind around the planet — a real proposed remedy for Mars' stripped atmosphere. With the wind held off, an atmosphere thickens instead of bleeding away.
- **What becomes possible:** Holding an atmosphere onto a world that cannot hold one itself — the missing precondition for terraforming the galaxy's dead, irradiated real estate.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **atmosphere gate**. Certain planets are flagged `atmosphere_stripped` (derived deterministically from planet id, like the rest of the board): they cannot hold climate targets or terraform stages at all until a dipole flies. The dipole is a station-independent orbital structure that also suppresses radiation-storm events and contributes shield strength against energy weapons.
- **Engine hook:** New orbital structure `magnetosphere_dipole` in `data/orbital-structures.ts` (`requiresStation: false`, `uniquePerPlanet: true`); an `atmosphere_stripped` derivation added to planet-surface generation; the climate/terraform ticks check for an active dipole on flagged worlds; its upkeep is beamed power, priced against `phys_directed_energy` infrastructure.
- **Required capacity:** A directed-energy power-beaming installation on the grid to feed it, high-grade composites and rares for the coil, and the orbital logistics to assemble a structure that sits far outside the normal orbital layer.
- **Interactions:** Hard gate for terraforming volcanic and toxic-irradiated archetypes; its shield contribution stacks with `shield_projector_array`; espionage sabotage of the *ground-side beam facility* is a two-cushion shot at the sky itself.
- **Strategic advantages:** Unlocks the worlds nobody else can use — the stripped planets rivals fly past become your expansion frontier; the defensive side effect hardens core worlds against energy bombardment.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** One structure holds up the sky. If the dipole is destroyed, every terraformed gain on that world begins to decay on a seasons-long timer — habitability sliding back, sector by sector, in full view of the population and the press. It is therefore the highest-value strike and sabotage target in any system it occupies, and its loss mid-war is a legitimacy catastrophe.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** "Dipole diplomacy" — surrender demands backed by a targeting solution on someone's magnetosphere; treaties that classify dipoles as civilian infrastructure; black-ops dipole sabotage as deniable slow genocide, and the wars fought over calling it that.

### Tectonic Engineering (`plan_tectonic_engineering`)
- **Domain:** Planetary Engineering | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** `plan_climate_control`, `comp_predictive_systems`, `phys_fusion_power`
- **Concept:** Fault lubrication and locking, controlled magma venting, and continental-scale grading — earthquake control matured into the ability to decide, slowly and at fantastic energy cost, where the land goes.
- **What becomes possible:** Editing geography: raising land from shallow seas, flattening ranges, opening or sealing the passes armies march through.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Surface-board rewriting.** A new order regrades a target sector over many ticks: ocean→plains land reclamation, mountains→plains grading, and — the strategic heart — creating or deleting `chokepoint` flags. Military geography becomes a product you manufacture. Also stabilizes volcanic sectors (removing their disaster ticks) and unlocks the `mantle_tap`, a volcanic-terrain building with the largest single-building energy output on any surface.
- **Engine hook:** New order `PLANET_REGRADE_SECTOR { sectorIndex, targetTerrain }` writing terrain and chokepoint overrides into the world-state override layer (deterministic-board contract preserved; conquerors inherit your edits); `comp_predictive_systems` quality gates the failure roll; new building `mantle_tap` (`allowedTerrain: ['volcanic']`).
- **Required capacity:** Fusion-scale energy dedicated per regrade, a seismic prediction network (compute), heavy manpower, and climate authority on the planet — you cannot re-coast a world whose weather you don't control.
- **Interactions:** Sequenced before a terraform, it decides the coastlines the new biosphere will get; with the fortress-doctrine military line it builds designed killing grounds; displaced-population mechanics feed the government system's grievance machinery.
- **Strategic advantages:** Fortress worlds with engineered chokepoint rings that make invasion arithmetic miserable; land reclamation on crowded core worlds; `mantle_tap` energy without fuel logistics.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Regrades displace whoever lived there — social groups losing home terrain gain militancy permanently. A failed or sabotaged regrade triggers earthquakes damaging buildings across neighboring sectors (espionage has a new favorite op). Edits are irreversible in practice and inherited by conquerors — you may be building your invader's fortress. Parliament fights viciously over who is granted the new land.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Land-grant corruption scandals in the press; chokepoint arms races between neighboring fortress worlds; regrade sabotage disguised as natural disaster, and the intelligence work of proving otherwise.

### Nomad Flotillas (`mega_nomad_flotillas`)
- **Domain:** Megastructures | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** `mega_orbital_habitats`, `space_advanced_drives`
- **Concept:** Habitat engineering meets high-thrust drives: hang drive sections and radiators on a rotating habitat and it stops being infrastructure and becomes a *vessel* — a city that can leave.
- **What becomes possible:** Mobile civilian populations. A habitat can undock from its planet and travel, carrying its people, stores and industry with it. Taken far enough: a civilization with no planets at all.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **flotilla** — a new mobile entity class on the hyperlane graph. Flotillas move like slow fleets, carry population, storage and a compact onboard economy, and can moor at friendly or neutral planets to trade and resupply. A flotilla can serve as a **seat of government**: losing every planet no longer ends your civilization — the state survives as long as the fleet does. This is the planetless play direction, open to any doctrine but cheapest and most stable under the Voidborn Mandate.
- **Engine hook:** New world-state collection `flotillas[]`; orders `HAB_UNDOCK_FLOTILLA { planetId, slotId }`, `HAB_MOOR { flotillaId, planetId }`, with movement resolved in the worker beside `MIL_MOVE_FLEET` pathing on the hyperlane graph; government-continuity check treats a designated flotilla as a valid capital; POPULATION season scoring counts flotilla population, TERRITORY does not.
- **Required capacity:** An orbital habitat to convert, advanced-drive manufacturing, deep onboard storage (food and volatiles for the voyage), and escorts — a flotilla has civilians and no meaningful guns.
- **Interactions:** Moorage rights become a diplomatic instrument (treaty clauses, `DIP_*` gambits); `space_deep_navigation` later frees flotillas from the hyperlane graph entirely; Planetary Disassembly needs flotillas or habitats to receive the evacuated.
- **Strategic advantages:** An uninvadable demographic core; a state that can retreat from a lost war intact; trade flotillas that bring the market to the frontier; insurance against everything except starvation.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Slow, fragile, and hungry — a blockaded or intercepted flotilla starves in weeks, and escort duty ties down your navy permanently. A government afloat pays a standing legitimacy discount ("the leadership fled") and home-territory bonuses evaporate. Moorage dependence hands leverage to every port you visit.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Refugee civilizations outliving their homeworlds; nomad trader and raider identities; civil wars that end not with a surrender but with half the fleet leaving; flotillas as the escape vector that makes Planetary Disassembly thinkable.

## Tier 4 — Transformation

### Orbital Ring (`mega_orbital_ring`)
- **Domain:** Megastructures | **Tier:** 4 (canonical anchor)
- **Prerequisites:** `infra_orbital_elevator`, `mat_molecular_manufacturing`, `mega_orbital_habitats`
- **Concept:** An actively supported ring girdling the planet — a rotating mass stream inside a stationary sheath, magnetically suspended, with elevator taps to the ground along its whole circumference. Surface-to-orbit cost falls effectively to freight rates.
- **What becomes possible:** The planet and its orbit fuse into one economic body. Orbit stops being a place you launch to and becomes the planet's tallest district.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **ring layer**: a staged megaproject (seasons long, economy-dominating) that, completed, adds a band of new orbital slots above the existing layer and opens **ring districts** — population and industry living on the ring itself, fed by taps to the ground along its whole circumference. Note what the ring does *not* do: the throughput ceiling was already gone, removed by the orbital elevator that this project is built from. What the ring adds is **redundancy and fusion**. Hundreds of distributed taps retire the single-ribbon point of failure — no one severed cable can cut the planet off, and `ribbon_fall` stops being a strike that ends a world in an afternoon. And where the elevator merely suppressed `blockade.orbitalStoresCut` below a severity threshold, the ring dissolves the distinction: surface and orbital stores become one pool, one ledger, drawn on from either end, with no blockade severity able to partition a planet from its own sky. Ring-mounted defense makes the orbit of a ringed world nearly unassailable.
- **Engine hook:** New service `lib/megastructures/` with per-planet `ring` object in world state; order `MEGA_BEGIN_PROJECT { type: 'orbital_ring', planetId }` (order-queue pattern); ring ratings folded into `computeOrbitalRatings` so the rest of the game reads them through the existing interface; unlockFlag `ring_engineering`. The pooled ledger is the ring flag collapsing the planet's surface and orbital store entries into a single account in the distribution layer and clearing `blockade.orbitalStoresCut` unconditionally rather than below a threshold; the same flag suppresses the elevator's `ribbon_fall` catastrophe on that planet, since there is no longer a single ribbon to fall.
- **Required capacity:** A functioning orbital elevator (the ring is built *from* it), molecular manufacturing throughput, an economy able to absorb seasons of dominated industrial output, and the political capital to defend that opportunity cost in parliament — rings have brought down governments before they brought up cargo.
- **Interactions:** Charter corporations bid for ring segments — revenue, plus a steady drip of corruption events for the press system; a ringed world is the natural fortress-capital of an Open Sky or Hermetic civilization, and the natural shipyard-anchorage of a Voidborn one; Dyson-swarm beamed power can feed the ring's suspension grid.
- **Strategic advantages:** The strongest single-planet economy multiplier in the game; near-total orbital security; capital-ship industry at ring scale; the definitive statement that a world is *yours*.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have not abolished the falling sky, only socialized it. The ribbon could be cut by one raid and ruined a wedge; the ring cannot be cut by one raid at all, but if integrity falls below threshold in wartime the deorbiting debris devastates surface sectors all the way around — permanent `ruins` terrain overrides on your richest world, written by your own megastructure. A harder target with a worse failure. A ringed planet cannot be abandoned, evacuated or triaged; you will defend it with everything, and your enemies know exactly where you must stand and fight.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Wars won by threatening a ring rather than breaking it; ring-segment corporate states with private agendas; the ring as hostage in secession crises — a breakaway region under the ring shares your fate whether it likes it or not.

### Dyson Swarm (`mega_dyson_swarm`)
- **Domain:** Megastructures | **Tier:** 4 (canonical anchor)
- **Prerequisites:** `mat_molecular_manufacturing`, `comp_machine_intelligence`, `space_deep_navigation`
- **Concept:** Not a shell — a cloud: millions of independent collectors and statites in engineered orbits, built by self-replicating fabricators eating asteroid mass, intercepting an ever-growing fraction of the star's entire output.
- **What becomes possible:** The star itself becomes an industrial asset. Energy ceases to be an economic constraint and becomes an economic *weapon* — something you have so much of that its price is a policy decision.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The first **system-scale project**, anchored to a star rather than a planet. The swarm grows in tranches, each adding system-wide energy output that dwarfs planetary grids. At a threshold fraction, the **energy price collapse** fires: a permanent economic regime change — energy-sector buildings obsolete, energy-worker unemployment shocks, market disruption scored under the DISRUPTION season category, and unlocked energy-to-matter industries (antimatter feedstock with `phys_antimatter`; exportable beamed power as a new trade good with `phys_directed_energy`).
- **Engine hook:** `MEGA_BEGIN_PROJECT { type: 'dyson_swarm', systemId }` handled by the `lib/megastructures/` service with a per-system swarm record in world state; swarm logistics are machine-run (a standing `comp_machine_intelligence` dependency); the star's dimming is a **public signal** — visible to every faction without espionage, like a tech-tree `PublicSignal` that cannot be obscured.
- **Required capacity:** Molecular-manufacturing seed factories, deep-space navigation to operate off the hyperlane graph at the star, machine-intelligence administration (human crews cannot run a million-element swarm), and asteroid mass in-system to eat.
- **Interactions:** Powers Planetary Disassembly's mass drivers and the Orbital Ring's suspension grid; beamed-power exports plug into the trade system and `econ_automated_markets`; swarm tranches are targetable by fleet operations — raiding a swarm is economic warfare without a single civilian casualty, a new low-escalation conflict mode.
- **Strategic advantages:** Post-scarcity energy underwrites every other megaproject at once; power exports make client states; the swarm is too distributed to destroy outright.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Everyone can see your star dimming — the galaxy's clearest possible signal of ambition, and a standing invitation to preemptive coalitions. Your economy restructures around free energy and cannot easily go back; tranche raids become the standard way rivals bleed you below the threshold of war. The machine administration that runs the swarm is a dependency your parliament never voted for.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Energy-export hegemony and the resentments it breeds; swarm-raid privateering; the quiet realization that the machine layer operating your star has opinions about `comp_autonomous_administration`.

### Gaia Transformation (`plan_gaia_transformation`)
- **Domain:** Planetary Engineering | **Tier:** 4 — *Worlds' Purpose fork* (`mutuallyExclusiveGroup: mega_t4_worlds_purpose`)
- **Prerequisites:** `plan_terraforming`, `bio_synthetic_biology`
- **Concept:** Beyond habitable: *alive*. A designed, self-regulating biosphere — the Gaia hypothesis as an engineering target — in which climate, ecology and geochemistry balance one another without machines. The regulators are switched off because the planet no longer needs them.
- **What becomes possible:** Garden worlds in the fullest sense: planets that maintain themselves, forever, and mean something to everyone who sees them.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The final terraform stage, continental→**gaia**. Gaia worlds hold habitability 100 across all 64 sectors, are self-maintaining (no regulator upkeep, immune to climate sabotage and climate siege — there is nothing to switch off), and each one projects a civilization-wide legitimacy and INFLUENCE aura: standing season score and diplomatic weight, a wonder that votes.
- **Engine hook:** Archetype transition to `gaia` via the terraform project machinery; unlockFlag `gaia_engineering`; the aura implemented as a global modifier in `PlayerTechState.globalModifiers` scaled by gaia-world count; industrial-building restrictions enforced in construction validation on gaia planets.
- **Required capacity:** A completed continental terraform as substrate, `bio_synthetic_biology`-line ecosystem design capacity, and seasons of biosphere seeding during which the world produces almost nothing.
- **Interactions:** The natural crown of the Open Sky Compact (its trajectory bonuses apply in full); gaia worlds are diplomacy engines — pilgrimage clauses, prestige summits, `DIP_*` gambits staged in famous gardens; the press system treats anything that happens on a gaia world as a top-tier story.
- **Strategic advantages:** The most stable, loyal, self-sufficient territory achievable; a legitimacy engine that compounds with each garden; an identity rivals cannot copy without the mutex fork you chose and they didn't.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Gaia worlds refuse heavy industry — industrial and military-industrial buildings are banned or heavily penalized there, and forcing them triggers biosphere-revolt disaster events. Populations grow so attached that relocating or militarizing a gaia world triggers unrest approaching rebellion. Every rival wants your gardens intact, which makes them the one thing you can never trade, and permanently locks Planetary Disassembly: a civilization that grows worlds cannot also eat them.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Wars fought *carefully* over planets neither side will bombard; gaia-world pacifist blocs in parliament obstructing war economies; a late game where your power is measured in places worth defending rather than fleets.

### Planetary Disassembly (`mega_planetary_disassembly`)
- **Domain:** Megastructures | **Tier:** 4 — *Worlds' Purpose fork* (`mutuallyExclusiveGroup: mega_t4_worlds_purpose`)
- **Prerequisites:** `phys_antimatter`, `mat_molecular_manufacturing`, `mega_orbital_habitats`
- **Concept:** The cold accounting at the end of megastructure engineering: a planet is a poorly organized warehouse of mass at the bottom of a gravity well. Mass drivers, crust lifting, and eventually core tapping convert worlds into feedstock for the structures that replace them.
- **What becomes possible:** Eating planets. Uninhabited worlds and moons become mass streams feeding rings, swarms and flotillas; inhabited ones can follow, if you are willing to do what that requires.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **disassembly project**: begun on an uninhabited body, it converts the planet over seasons into a colossal ongoing metals/rares stream and a flat acceleration to every other megaproject in the system. On an *inhabited* world it demands total evacuation first — mass population transfers to habitats and flotillas — after which the planet is removed from play, its hyperlane node persisting as a minable debris field. This is the game's one truly irreversible act of geography.
- **Engine hook:** `MEGA_BEGIN_DISASSEMBLY { planetId }` in the `lib/megastructures/` service; evacuation phase implemented as forced `HAB_TRANSFER_POPULATION` flows with per-region compliance checks against the government system's social groups; terminal state swaps the planet entity for a `debris_field` node on the hyperlane graph; unlockFlag `world_eater`.
- **Required capacity:** Antimatter-scale energy (or a Dyson swarm's beamed power), molecular-manufacturing mass-driver arrays, and — the real constraint — habitat and flotilla berths for every soul you displace. The bottleneck is not physics; it is where the people go.
- **Interactions:** The Voidborn Mandate makes evacuations orderly instead of catastrophic; Nomad Flotillas receive the displaced; Dyson swarms drink the mass. Espionage and press systems light up around every inhabited-world disassembly: leaked evacuation orders are the biggest story the press system can carry.
- **Strategic advantages:** The fastest megastructure economy in the game — rings and swarms built in half the seasons; an end-state civilization of rings, statites and fleets that owns no planets and therefore cannot be conquered by taking any; total conversion of dead systems no other doctrine values.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Evacuating unwilling populations spawns resistance movements, rebellions and secession attempts at every stage, and the diplomatic reaction is existential: pariah status, coalitions, and a permanent atrocity ledger in every rival's press. A disassembled world is gone for everyone, forever — including the version of your civilization that might one day have wanted it back. Locks Gaia Transformation: a civilization that eats worlds cannot also grow them.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Interventionist wars racing an evacuation clock; "world-eater" reputations that outlive the governments that earned them; the fully planetless civilization as an end-state — and the unsettling question of what, exactly, anyone could ever take from it.
