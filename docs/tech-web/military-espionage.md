# Technology Web — Military & Intelligence Domain

Playing into this domain means choosing to see and to strike before anyone else, and accepting what that appetite does to a society. Every tier deepens two intertwined capabilities: the machinery of organized violence (command, logistics, bombardment, screens, autonomous weapons) and the machinery of knowing (intercepts, resident networks, prediction, fabricated people). Neither half works alone — a fleet without intelligence fights blind inside the combat engine's visibility bands, and an intelligence service without hard power collects secrets nobody can act on. The domain constantly forces implementation questions: knowing how to build a deterrent arsenal is cheap; building the orbital containment farm, staffing the analyst corps, and surviving the parliamentary debate about it is the actual game.

The civilization identity this domain enables is the *security state in its many forms*: the mass-mobilized people's war machine, the small professional force that wins by reading its enemy, the machine-commanded power that no longer needs generals, or the covenant state that makes restraint itself a weapon. By Tier 4 the questions stop being military and become constitutional — who authorizes violence when reaction times drop below human cognition, what a deterrent posture does to every neighbor's research priorities, and what happens to a society whose intelligence organs can manufacture the consensus they were built to detect. A player who commits here ends the game with unmatched leverage and a population, a parliament, and a press that no longer entirely trust their own government — because it earned that distrust.

## Tier 1 — Expansion

### Integrated Command Networks (`mil_command_networks`)
- **Domain:** military | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** comm_deep_space_network
- **Concept:** Real militaries live or die by C3 — command, control, communications. Interplanetary distances turn light-lag into an operational problem: a fleet order that takes hours to arrive is a historical document, not a command.
- **What becomes possible:** A unified military command architecture spanning claimed systems: standing orders, theater-level coordination, and battle plans that survive contact with light-lag.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Theater command. The player can group fleets and armies into a named theater with a shared standing directive (hold, raid, converge on siege), so multiple `MIL_BATTLE_PLAN`s execute as one coordinated operation instead of per-fleet micromanagement. Fleets outside command coverage (no relay path on the hyperlane graph to a command nexus) revert to isolated behavior: no shared battle plans, no coordinated stance changes mid-engagement.
- **Engine hook:** New order type `MIL_ASSIGN_THEATER` (order-queue, handled in scripts/game-loop.ts alongside the existing `MIL_BATTLE_PLAN` / `MIL_COMBAT_DIRECTIVE` cases); district building `strategic_command_nexus`; unlockFlag `theater_command`; modifierKey `doctrine_switch_cooldown` reduction applied to `EmpireDoctrines.lastChangeTimestamps` checks in lib/doctrine/doctrine-service.ts.
- **Required capacity:** At least one `strategic_command_nexus` built in a planetary district; unbroken relay coverage (hyperlane-graph path to the nexus) for every fleet that should benefit; trained officer population (a staffed nexus consumes specialist pops). Knowledge without a nexus grants nothing — fleets keep fighting as isolated detachments.
- **Interactions:** Prerequisite spine for `mil_orbital_strike`, `mil_autonomous_warfare`, and both Tier 2 force-identity branches. Command coverage is what `mil_spectral_warfare` jams and what `esp_sigint` (yours and theirs) listens to. The nexus is a legitimate `sabotage_shipyard`-class espionage target.
- **Strategic advantages:** Multi-fleet wars become plannable; doctrine pivots become faster; the foundation every later military tech assumes.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Centralization creates a decapitation target: losing the nexus (bombardment, sabotage, blackout) drops every dependent theater to uncoordinated behavior mid-war. Command traffic is a new attack surface — rival SIGINT reads theater assignments as intent signals. The officer corps becomes a political constituency that the government/coup system can turn against you.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Coup plots that begin with "who controls the nexus"; wars decided by relay-node raids rather than fleet battles; the Tier 4 question of whether command should be human at all.

### Expeditionary Logistics (`mil_expeditionary_logistics`)
- **Domain:** military | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** space_orbital_logistics, mil_command_networks
- **Concept:** Amateurs discuss tactics; professionals discuss logistics. Force projection beyond your own infrastructure requires prepositioned matériel, tender ships, and supply lines someone has to defend.
- **What becomes possible:** Sustained military operations far from home ports: forward depots, fleet tenders, and formal supply lines drawn over the hyperlane graph.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Supply lines as objects. New order `MIL_ESTABLISH_DEPOT` places a forward supply depot (planetary district building or orbital tender station) that projects a supply radius along hyperlanes. Fleets and armies inside a radius hold full supply; outside it, the combat engine's supply value decays per tick toward the 0.3 cliff where `calculateEffectivePower` halves output. Supply lines are visible, raidable objects: `raid_trade_route`-style interdiction against a military supply line starves the front instead of the treasury.
- **Engine hook:** Order type `MIL_ESTABLISH_DEPOT`; building id `forward_supply_depot` and orbital structure `fleet_tender_station`; a per-tick supply-propagation pass in scripts/game-loop.ts that writes `CombatantState.supply`; unlockFlag `supply_projection`.
- **Required capacity:** Depots consume stockpiled goods from the logistics/storage/distribution system every tick they sustain a force — an army you cannot feed from storage is an army the depot cannot save. Orbital tenders require `space_orbital_logistics`-grade lift at the homeport. Depots need garrisons (`MIL_ESTABLISH_GARRISON`) or they are free kills.
- **Interactions:** Makes `mil_orbital_strike` sieges sustainable; mass-mobilization armies (Tier 2) triple depot draw; `esp_sigint` traffic analysis on your depot resupply schedule reveals offensive timing to rivals; charter corporations can be contracted to run rear-echelon supply for credits, with corruption risk.
- **Strategic advantages:** The difference between winning one border battle and winning a war on someone else's planets. Defensively, destroying an invader's depot chain beats meeting his fleet.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Every depot is territory you must defend and a commitment the parliament can read as war preparation (press stories, diplomatic tension). Long supply lines dominate your fleet posture — escorts guarding tenders are escorts not fighting. Depot networks in occupied territory feed rebellion narratives.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Wars of interdiction fought entirely against logistics; "depot diplomacy" where prepositioned supplies coerce neighbors without a shot; the deep-space bypass once `space_deep_navigation` lets convoys leave the hyperlane graph.

### Signals Intelligence Apparatus (`esp_sigint`)
- **Domain:** espionage | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** comm_deep_space_network
- **Concept:** Most intelligence is not stolen documents; it is traffic analysis — who talks to whom, how often, at what volume. You can read an empire's intentions from its message metadata without breaking a single cipher.
- **What becomes possible:** A standing interception establishment: listening arrays that passively harvest rival communications transiting nearby relays and hyperlane approaches.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Persistent intercept coverage. Listening arrays project a SIGINT footprint over adjacent systems. Inside the footprint: passive intel-point income to `espionageState`, and the combat engine's baseline `IntelLevel` against foreign fleets rises from `blind` to `observing` (the ±50% power estimate tightens to ±30% with archetype hints) without needing an agent. Adds the `traffic_analysis` operation to the catalog: a low-risk read of a target's *order tempo* — not what they ordered, but how much and toward where — surfacing invasion prep and fleet massing as warnings.
- **Engine hook:** District building `sigint_listening_array`; passive per-tick intel accrual in the espionage tick section of scripts/game-loop.ts; baseline-IntelLevel override feeding `generateVisibilityProfile` in lib/combat/combat-engine.ts; new `OPERATION_CATALOG` entry `traffic_analysis` (category `intel_gathering`, launched via `ESP_LAUNCH_OP`).
- **Required capacity:** Arrays are district buildings — they occupy Voronoi sectors that could hold economy, and they want high-altitude/low-interference district terrain. Coverage requires relay adjacency: no `comm_deep_space_network` presence, no take. Analyst population converts raw intercepts to intel points; unstaffed arrays produce archives nobody reads.
- **Interactions:** Feeds `esp_predictive_intel` (fusion needs raw take), `mil_elite_precision` (stance reading), and `mil_spectral_warfare` (jamming is applied SIGINT). Countered by rival `comm_quantum_secure` and by `esp_counterintel_lattice` deception feeds. Existing ops `infiltrate_military` and `disinformation_fake_fleet` gain/lose effectiveness inside hostile footprints.
- **Strategic advantages:** Cheap, deniable, always-on situational awareness; the entry fee for the entire intelligence tree.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Arrays are undisguisable once built — their existence is itself a diplomatic statement, and neighbors respond with encryption spend or preemptive hostility. Interception legal frameworks leak inward: the same arrays hear domestic traffic, and the press system can break a "state listens to its citizens" scandal that costs political capital. Rival counter-intelligence can deliberately feed your arrays fiction.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Encryption arms races; wars triggered by misread traffic spikes; the Tier 4 temptation to stop *reading* the information environment and start *writing* it.

## Tier 2 — Specialization

### Precision Orbital Strike Doctrine (`mil_orbital_strike`)
- **Domain:** military | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** mil_command_networks, space_orbital_logistics
- **Concept:** Kinetic bombardment from orbit is trivially destructive and strategically useless if it flattens the economy you came to take. Precision — guidance, terminal maneuvering, real-time targeting — is what turns an atrocity into an instrument.
- **What becomes possible:** District-level strike targeting: orbiting fleets can hit a single named installation on the surface instead of hammering the planet as a whole.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Targeted bombardment against the 64-district Voronoi map. `MIL_BOMBARD_PLANET` gains a `targetDistrictId` payload: strike one district's buildings (the shield generator, the command nexus, the ammo plant) while leaving the rest standing. Each precision strike rolls collateral against adjacent districts; collateral kills population, spikes instability, and generates press-system atrocity stories attributed to you. The player now chooses, strike by strike, between military effect and the political cost of rubble.
- **Engine hook:** Extended `MIL_BOMBARD_PLANET` order payload (`targetDistrictId`, `munitionsProfile`) in scripts/game-loop.ts; modifierKey `bombard_precision` (collateral radius multiplier); unlockFlag `precision_bombardment`; press-system event emission on collateral thresholds.
- **Required capacity:** Orbital supremacy over the target (contested orbit blocks targeting telemetry); munitions resupply through `mil_expeditionary_logistics` — precision munitions draw from stockpiles, and a fleet without a supply line degrades to area bombardment; targeting data at `infiltrated`-or-better IntelLevel for the specific district (blind fleets cannot precision-strike, tying strikes to espionage coverage).
- **Interactions:** Pairs with `esp_resident_networks` (ground truth for targeting), is degraded by rival `mil_spectral_warfare` (jammed terminal guidance raises collateral), and is the delivery mechanism the Tier 4 deterrence architecture scales up. Government system: repeated collateral events give parliament opposition ammunition and feed `incite_rebellion` conditions on occupied worlds.
- **Strategic advantages:** Sieges shorten dramatically; you can decapitate defenses and take infrastructure intact — conquest that pays for itself.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Every strike is a political act. Collateral is probabilistic, and one bad roll on a habitation district creates a season-long DISRUPTION story, diplomatic pile-on, and radicalized survivors. Dependence on intel-guided strikes means a counter-intelligence-savvy defender can feed you a false target map — you demolish a decoy district on camera.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** "Clean war" self-images that collapse on the first massacre; defenders deliberately colocating industry with habitation as a shield; escalation ladders into the antimatter deterrent.

### Mass Mobilization Doctrine (`mil_mass_mobilization`)
- **Domain:** military | **Tier:** 2 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `mil_t2_force_identity`
- **Prerequisites:** mil_command_networks, soc_digital_governance
- **Concept:** The levée en masse, industrialized: when the state can register, conscript, train, and equip its whole population, quantity becomes a quality of its own — and the society reorganizes around the war it might someday fight.
- **What becomes possible:** The nation-in-arms. Conscription registries built on digital-governance platforms convert civilian population directly into military formations at scale.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Conscription as a standing policy with a dial. Unlocks the `universal_conscription` policy: `MIL_RECRUIT_FORMATION_UNIT` and `MIL_CREATE_ARMY` gain a conscript pathway that recruits from general population at a fraction of professional cost and absorbs unemployment (a genuine release valve for automation-displaced pops from the economy domain). Conscript formations are numerous but brittle: lower morale ceiling, faster `supply` draw, and demobilization is its own crisis — standing armies down requires reabsorbing veterans into an economy that must have jobs for them, or instability follows them home.
- **Engine hook:** `UNLOCK_POLICY` effect `universal_conscription`; recruit-cost branch in the `MIL_RECRUIT_FORMATION_UNIT` handler; modifierKeys `conscript_recruit_cost` and `morale_ceiling_penalty` read where `CombatantState.morale` is initialized; demobilization tick logic emitting unemployment/instability into the colony model.
- **Required capacity:** Digital census coverage (the `soc_digital_governance` platforms are the draft apparatus); training-camp district buildings with throughput limits; equipment production chains — conscripts without rifles are casualties, so stockpile capacity gates real army size, not the policy itself.
- **Interactions:** Triples the value of `mil_expeditionary_logistics` and the strain on it; parliament factions split predictably over conscription (political capital cost to enact, more to repeal); rival `incite_rebellion` and `fund_separatists` ops gain traction in heavily drafted regions; season scoring shifts toward MILITARY and POPULATION.
- **Strategic advantages:** You can absorb losses no professional force survives, garrison everything, and make invasion of your worlds demographically unpayable for the attacker.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Locks out `mil_elite_precision` — your identity is mass, permanently. The economy carries a standing labor tax; happiness sags under service obligations; a lost war with a conscript army means casualty lists in every district and legitimacy collapse rather than a professional force's quiet defeat. Demobilization after victory can hurt more than the war.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Veteran political blocs in parliament; societies that need a war to justify the apparatus; civil wars fought with the state's own distributed weapons stockpiles.

### Elite Precision Forces (`mil_elite_precision`)
- **Domain:** military | **Tier:** 2 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `mil_t2_force_identity`
- **Prerequisites:** mil_command_networks, esp_sigint
- **Concept:** A small force that knows exactly what the enemy will do beats a large force that guesses. Professional militaries trade mass for training density, retention, and intelligence integration at the squad level.
- **What becomes possible:** A compact, career-professional military whose units fight as intelligence consumers — every formation plugged into the SIGINT take.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Enemy-reading as a combat discipline. Raises the manual stance-prediction payoff in the combat engine (the Elite Precision Force hook: `prediction_bonus_multiplier` from the 0.15 base to 0.25), and unlocks *veteran cadres*: surviving formations accumulate persistent experience across battles instead of being interchangeable, making unit preservation a strategic behavior — retreat becomes an investment decision, not a failure. `MIL_SET_GROUND_PREDICTION` and stance-prediction inputs gain doctrine-bias hints drawn from the target's active doctrine (`DoctrineBehaviorBias` becomes partially visible against factions you hold SIGINT coverage on).
- **Engine hook:** techModifier `prediction_bonus_multiplier` (already read in lib/combat/combat-engine.ts, resolveEngagementRound); new per-formation `veterancy` field persisted across `CombatState` resolutions; doctrine-bias exposure flag consumed by the prediction-hint UI; unlockFlag `veteran_cadres`.
- **Required capacity:** Military academies (district buildings) with multi-tick training pipelines — replacing a destroyed veteran formation takes real time, not credits; sustained SIGINT coverage of the current enemy (the prediction bonus decays to base against factions outside your intercept footprint); higher per-unit upkeep in specialist population and equipment.
- **Interactions:** Synergizes with `esp_predictive_intel` (fusion sharpens stance forecasts further) and `mil_spectral_warfare` (your EW protects the thin force's information edge). Countered by mass doctrine's attrition math and by rivals who randomize stances to poison prediction. Locks out `mil_mass_mobilization`.
- **Strategic advantages:** Wins engagements above its weight, cheap in peacetime, politically light — no conscription fights in parliament, minimal unemployment coupling.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Catastrophically loss-intolerant: one annihilation defeat destroys a decade of training capital, and the engine's veterancy makes that loss legible and demoralizing. Cannot garrison a large empire — you will hold space with allies, militia, or not at all. Rival espionage targeting your academies (`sabotage_shipyard`-pattern ops) attacks your regeneration rate directly.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Cults of the veteran formation with names and histories; wars declined because the force is too precious to risk; pressure toward autonomous systems as the answer to irreplaceable people.

### Predictive Intelligence Fusion (`esp_predictive_intel`)
- **Domain:** espionage | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** esp_sigint, comp_predictive_systems
- **Concept:** Intelligence failure is rarely a collection problem; it is a fusion problem — the signal existed, scattered across agencies that did not talk. Machine-scale fusion turns archives of intercepts into forecasts.
- **What becomes possible:** An all-source fusion center that correlates SIGINT take, agent reporting, market signals, and press coverage into probabilistic forecasts of rival behavior.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Order-queue foresight. Against factions where your combined intel presence crosses a threshold, the fusion center periodically surfaces *warnings*: advance notice of categories of queued rival orders before they execute — "invasion prep against your border system, confidence 70%", "espionage operation targeting your capital, confidence 40%". Warnings are probabilistic and can be wrong; the fusion center also assigns confidence to intel you already hold, flagging probable disinformation. This converts espionage from a reactive tool into an early-warning institution — and creates the analyst's dilemma: acting on a warning reveals you had it.
- **Engine hook:** New service `lib/espionage/intel-fusion.ts` run in the espionage tick; warning objects written to `espionageState` and surfaced through the sync payload; unlockFlag `predictive_fusion`; required tech gate on future high-tier catalog ops via `OperationDefinition.requiredTech`.
- **Required capacity:** A fusion-center district building with heavy compute upkeep drawn from `comp_distributed_compute`-era infrastructure; breadth of input — warnings only fire against targets where you hold at least two independent sources (SIGINT footprint, resident network, market visibility); analyst specialists whose absence caps confidence scores.
- **Interactions:** Sharpens `mil_elite_precision` stance-reading and `mil_orbital_strike` target validation; is the counterparty to `esp_counterintel_lattice` deception (a poisoned source silently drags every fused forecast); doctrine biases feed the model — factions with strong `behaviorBias` are easier to predict, rewarding rivals who pick contrarian doctrines.
- **Strategic advantages:** You stop being surprised. Wars, coups, and sabotage arrive with lead time, which is the most valuable resource in a tick-based game.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Confidence is seductive: a well-run rival deception campaign doesn't blind you, it *convinces* you, and the UI will present fiction at 80% confidence. Leadership that habitually acts on warnings telegraphs its intelligence penetration, burning sources. Domestically, the same apparatus pointed inward is a surveillance state one parliament vote away.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Preemptive wars justified by forecasts that might have been wrong; institutional overtrust collapsing in a single engineered intelligence disaster; the Tier 3 counter-deception spiral.

### Resident Networks (`esp_resident_networks`)
- **Domain:** espionage | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** esp_sigint, econ_interstellar_banking
- **Concept:** The classic human-intelligence station: officers under long-term cover, local assets recruited over years, and front companies whose books launder the funding. Patient infrastructure, not dramatic operations.
- **What becomes possible:** Permanent clandestine stations on foreign worlds — a residency with local assets, safehouses, and commercial cover built through the interstellar banking system.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Espionage infrastructure as a persistent asset. New order `ESP_ESTABLISH_RESIDENCY` plants a hidden network on a target planet; the residency matures over ticks through stages (cover, assets, penetration), and every catalog operation launched against that planet through the residency gets improved success and reduced exposure — the residency, not the operation, carries the risk. Residencies can be run through charter-corporation fronts, hiding funding flows inside legitimate trade. When a residency is burned, everything it supported unwinds at once: active ops abort, assets are executed or flipped, and a diplomatic incident fires scaled to the residency's maturity.
- **Engine hook:** Order type `ESP_ESTABLISH_RESIDENCY` plus maturity progression in the espionage tick (extends the existing network model behind `ESP_INFILTRATE_NETWORK`); per-planet residency records in `espionageState`; charter-corp cover linkage flag; success/exposure modifiers applied at `ESP_LAUNCH_OP` resolution.
- **Required capacity:** Sustained credit flow through `econ_interstellar_banking` channels (frozen assets starve residencies); trained case officers from the agent pool (`ESP_RECRUIT_AGENT` pipeline) — each residency ties down an agent as resident; plausible commercial presence, meaning residencies are much harder to establish on worlds you have no trade relationship with.
- **Interactions:** The ground-truth source `mil_orbital_strike` targeting wants; the input `esp_predictive_intel` fusion needs for two-source confirmation; the substrate `esp_synthetic_identities` upgrades; the prey `esp_counterintel_lattice` hunts. Existing ops (`infiltrate_government`, `bribe_governor`, `fund_separatists`) become dramatically better through a mature residency.
- **Strategic advantages:** Compounding covert power — a five-season-old residency is an asset no crash program can replicate, and it works while you sleep.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Concentration risk: residencies convert espionage from many small gambles into few large hostages. A single defector or counterintel win costs you years and hands the victim a propaganda coup with your fingerprints. Banking-trail exposure links espionage scandals to your legitimate corporations, inviting sanctions against the innocent half of your economy.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Decades-long penetration sagas; trade treaties negotiated partly to enable station cover; hostage dynamics where burned networks are traded like prisoners.

## Tier 3 — Dominance

### Autonomous Warfare Systems (`mil_autonomous_warfare`)
- **Domain:** military | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** comp_machine_intelligence, mil_command_networks
- **Concept:** Once engagement decisions outpace light-lag and human reaction, the pressure to delegate lethal authority to machines becomes structural, not optional. Every military that networks its commands eventually asks why the network needs a human in it.
- **What becomes possible:** Weapons platforms and fleet elements that select and engage targets under machine authority within human-set boundaries — or beyond them.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Engagement authority as a player decision with teeth. New order `MIL_SET_ENGAGEMENT_AUTHORITY` per theater or fleet: `human_gated` (status quo — orders required, light-lag applies), `bounded_autonomous` (fleet fights on without orders when cut off from command coverage, using its last directive), or `weapons_free` (fleet initiates engagements against declared enemies on its own recognizance). Higher authority means faster reaction — autonomous fleets ignore command-decapitation penalties and respond instantly to `MIL_ATTACK_FLEET`-class threats — but each tick at `weapons_free` rolls against an escalation table: machine-initiated incidents (a neutral convoy engaged, a retreating enemy pursued across a border, a strike on a civilian district mid-siege) that you own diplomatically whether or not you wanted them. Parliament tracks a standing *machine-authority unease* pressure that grows with delegation and feeds opposition factions and press coverage.
- **Engine hook:** Order type `MIL_SET_ENGAGEMENT_AUTHORITY`; autonomy field on fleet state consumed where scripts/game-loop.ts checks command coverage; escalation-incident generator in the military tick emitting crisis events and press stories; unlockFlag `autonomous_fleet_doctrine`; political-capital cost hook into the government system on authority increases.
- **Required capacity:** Machine-intelligence cores manufactured under `comp_machine_intelligence`-era industry — each autonomous hull consumes core production, a real supply chain; doctrine-training data (fleets fight with your recorded battle history; a faction with few past engagements fields naive autonomy); secured update channels — without `comm_quantum_secure`, autonomous fleets accept the risk of hostile order injection.
- **Interactions:** The precondition for both Tier 4 command-sovereignty branches. `mil_elite_precision` factions adopt it to protect irreplaceable crews; `mil_mass_mobilization` factions to multiply mass. Rival `esp_synthetic_identities` and `mil_spectral_warfare` open the new attack surface: spoofed identification, forged orders, poisoned target libraries.
- **Strategic advantages:** Immunity to decapitation, reaction below human timescales, and wars waged without casualty lists — the political cheapness of machine losses is itself a strategic drug.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You will eventually own an atrocity you did not order. Escalation incidents are authored by your flag, and "the machine did it" is a legitimacy sinkhole in the press system. Hostile subversion of an autonomous fleet is a catastrophic new failure mode with no human circuit breaker. Domestically, every increase in machine authority is political capital spent and a gift to opposition and secessionist narratives.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Border incidents neither government chose; arms-control diplomacy; the constitutional crisis Tier 4 forces you to resolve one way or the other.

### Spectral Warfare (`mil_spectral_warfare`)
- **Domain:** military | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** phys_directed_energy, esp_sigint
- **Concept:** Directed energy is one discipline with two applications: put coherent power on a hull and it is a weapon; put it on a sensor or an antenna and it is jamming, dazzling, and deception. Mastery of the spectrum is mastery of both.
- **What becomes possible:** Integrated energy-warfare suites: ship-mounted beam batteries, planetary grid-fed defense cannon, and full-spectrum electronic attack.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The information layer of combat becomes contestable. Fleets fitted with EW suites can, per engagement: degrade the enemy's effective `IntelLevel` by one band (a `deep_penetration` attacker sees only `infiltrated` data — the visibility profile in the combat engine widens), suppress the enemy's stance-prediction bonus (manual and tech-derived), and project *ghost signatures* — the `disinformation_fake_fleet` effect generated tactically, at will, without an espionage op. Separately unlocks the `coherent_battery` district building: a ground-based directed-energy cannon that draws its magazine from the planetary power grid — a defense whose ammunition is the civilian energy supply, forcing rolling blackouts across other districts while firing.
- **Engine hook:** `ewSuite` rating on `CombatantState` consumed by `generateVisibilityProfile` and the prediction-bonus block in resolveEngagementRound; modifierKey `enemy_prediction_suppression`; building id `coherent_battery` with a grid-draw hook into the `infra_planetary_grid` power model; tactical-layer note: EW is the explicitly deferred V3 feature in lib/tactical/types.ts — this tech is its unlock.
- **Required capacity:** Shipyard refits (EW suites are installed, not researched into existence — fleet-by-fleet through the build queue); `phys_directed_energy`-grade power plants aboard; for `coherent_battery`, a functioning unified grid with surplus generation — a planet at energy deficit fires blanks; specialist EW officer crews.
- **Interactions:** Directly counters `esp_sigint`-derived targeting and `mil_elite_precision` prediction play; shields `mil_autonomous_warfare` fleets from spoofed orders (and jams rival autonomous fleets into their fallback behaviors — a genuinely new tactic: EW attack to force an enemy fleet into `bounded_autonomous` stupidity); pairs with `mil_adaptive_screens` for the full defensive stack.
- **Strategic advantages:** You choose what the enemy knows at the moment of battle. Against intelligence-dependent rivals this is worth more than a bigger fleet.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** The grid coupling cuts both ways: `coherent_battery` planets make civilian power a military target, legitimizing rival strikes on your grid and making `sabotage`-category ops against power infrastructure a siege-opening move. EW-heavy fleets sacrifice hull tonnage for emitters — win the information fight and lose the shooting fight if the enemy simply commits mass. Ghost signatures that get exposed (rival `counterintel_sweep`, `source_verification`) burn trust in all your signals, including real distress calls.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Battles that open with blackout warfare against cities; false-flag ghost fleets triggering third-party wars; sensor-trust collapse as a theater condition.

### Adaptive Screen Systems (`mil_adaptive_screens`)
- **Domain:** military | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** mat_advanced_composites, mat_orbital_industry
- **Concept:** Static armor loses to any weapon designed after it was cast. Programmable-matter plating and phase-tuned shield emitters can be reconfigured between engagements — if you know what is coming and have the industrial base to retune them.
- **What becomes possible:** Warships whose defensive envelope — armor lattice and shield harmonics — is reconfigured per deployment against the expected threat.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Pre-battle defensive configuration as a decision. New order `MIL_CONFIGURE_SCREENS` sets a fleet's screen profile before an engagement: bias shield capacity across the tactical layer's four facings, tune armor against beam or torpedo damage classes, or balance. The right profile against the enemy's actual composition grants a large defensive edge (raises `screeningEfficiency`, the value that already gates torpedo damage in the combat engine); the wrong one is worse than baseline. Because profiles are chosen against *expected* threat, this mechanic consumes intelligence: fleets configured from good intel fight armored against reality; fleets configured from poisoned intel are armored against a fiction the enemy sold them.
- **Engine hook:** Order type `MIL_CONFIGURE_SCREENS`; screen-profile field on fleet state mapped into `CombatantState.screeningEfficiency` and the tactical adapter's `ArmorProfile`/`ShieldFacings` (lib/tactical/fleet-adapter.ts); modifierKey `screen_retune_cost`; refit availability gated on orbital-foundry presence.
- **Required capacity:** Orbital foundries (`mat_orbital_industry`) in the fleet's current or adjacent system — retuning is a dock operation, not a menu click, taking ticks and exotic-composite stock from storage; fleets far from friendly industry fight with whatever profile they left home carrying, making forward bases and `mil_expeditionary_logistics` tenders the real enablers.
- **Interactions:** Turns the combat engine's intel bands into a defensive resource: `esp_sigint` and `esp_predictive_intel` tell you what to tune for; rival `esp_counterintel_lattice` deception makes mistuning a weapon; `mil_spectral_warfare` ghosts exist precisely to induce wrong configurations. Elite-precision factions get double value (few hulls, each perfectly dressed); mass factions can rarely afford fleet-wide retunes.
- **Strategic advantages:** Defensive power that scales with your intelligence quality instead of your budget — the thinking player's armor.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** A new dependency chain: composites supply, orbital foundries, and dock time all become war-limiting resources and sabotage targets (`sabotage_shipyard` now also degrades screen readiness). Configuration lock-in punishes strategic surprise — the enemy who feints beam-heavy and arrives torpedo-heavy has already won the defensive exchange. Reading a rival's dock activity (their retune patterns) leaks their threat assessment, a brand-new SIGINT channel that cuts both ways.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Shipyard-intelligence duels before every major war; composite-supply embargoes as soft warfare; doctrine meta-games of deliberate mis-signaling in refit yards.

### Synthetic Identities (`esp_synthetic_identities`)
- **Domain:** espionage | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** esp_resident_networks, comp_machine_intelligence
- **Concept:** When records, references, biometrics, and personal histories are all data, an identity is something you can manufacture: a person who has never existed, with a paper trail that says they always have.
- **What becomes possible:** Industrial fabrication of complete legal persons — biographies, credentials, transaction histories, even synthetic correspondents who vouch for each other — woven into a target's civil records.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Deniable depth. New order `ESP_FABRICATE_IDENTITY` creates identity assets that agents wear like equipment: an exposed operation burns the *identity*, not the agent — the agent exfiltrates and the trail dead-ends in a person who never lived, slashing the diplomatic-incident severity of failed ops run through a synthetic cover. Fabricated identities also unlock an impersonation class of operations: insert a synthetic official into a rival's administration (a fake procurement officer redirecting military supply; a fabricated analyst feeding their fusion center), and forge communiqués attributed to real rival leaders through the press system — manufactured scandals with authentic-looking provenance.
- **Engine hook:** Order type `ESP_FABRICATE_IDENTITY`; identity-asset inventory in `espionageState` consumed at `ESP_LAUNCH_OP` resolution (exposure-severity override); new catalog ops `insert_synthetic_official` and `forge_communique` (category `disinformation`/`political`, `requiredTech: ['esp_synthetic_identities']`); press-system injection hook for forged attributions.
- **Required capacity:** Deep civil-data penetration of the target — synthetic identities must be written *into* records you can reach, so a mature residency (`esp_resident_networks`) on the target world is a hard operational requirement; machine-intelligence compute for biography generation and upkeep (identities decay without maintenance — a dormant legend with no transactions is a flag `esp_counterintel_lattice` audits catch); forgery-grade document industry at home.
- **Interactions:** Supercharges every high-risk catalog op (`fund_coup`, `assassinate_governor`, `election_interference`) by decoupling failure from attribution; collides head-on with rival counter-intelligence identity audits; against `mil_autonomous_warfare` users, synthetic credentials are the skeleton key for order-injection attacks — the scariest cross-system combo in the domain.
- **Strategic advantages:** You can operate at extreme aggression with bounded diplomatic downside; rivals experience unattributable interference and must guess among all their enemies.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Fabrication cuts inward: the same tooling makes your own civil records fundamentally forgeable, and rival services with this tech can seed *your* administration — universal identity distrust is a two-sided condition. A detected synthetic (rather than burned) identity is a counterintel goldmine: everything it touched is mapped. Societies that learn their officials might be fabrications suffer legitimacy erosion that no single scandal explains — and yours learns it too.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Identity-audit purges that wreck innocent careers (an instability engine); paranoia politics where real officials are denounced as synthetics; the Tier 4 step from forging people to forging consensus.

### Counterintelligence Lattice (`esp_counterintel_lattice`)
- **Domain:** espionage | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** esp_predictive_intel, comm_quantum_secure
- **Concept:** Mature counterintelligence does not just catch spies — it *runs* them. The doctrine of the double-cross system: leave discovered agents in place and feed them a curated false reality, turning the enemy's collection apparatus into your broadcast channel.
- **What becomes possible:** A standing, empire-wide counterespionage institution: continuous detection, quantum-secured compartmentation, and offensive deception through captured channels.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Counterintelligence becomes persistent and offensive. The one-off `counterintel_sweep` op is superseded by a standing lattice: every tick, passive detection rolls run against foreign residencies, synthetic identities, and SIGINT taps operating on your worlds, with detection probability scaling with lattice coverage. On detection the player gets the double-cross choice per asset: *burn* it (immediate diplomatic incident, network unwinds — the safe play) or *turn* it (leave it running and script what it reports). Turned assets feed disinformation directly into the owner's `esp_predictive_intel` fusion — their warnings begin arriving with high confidence and wrong content. Running doubles is a standing gamble: each tick a turned asset survives, its handler may notice; a discovered double-cross forfeits the channel and marks all your past product as suspect.
- **Engine hook:** Standing detection pass in the espionage tick against foreign assets in `espionageState`; new orders `ESP_TURN_ASSET` and `ESP_SCRIPT_FEED` (the scripted-feed content plugs into the target's intel-fusion inputs); district building `counterintel_directorate`; catalog entry `identity_audit` (counters `esp_synthetic_identities`, `requiredTech` gated).
- **Required capacity:** `comm_quantum_secure` internal channels — without them your own lattice traffic is collectible and the double-cross system leaks; directorate buildings and vetted specialist staff on each covered world (coverage is per-planet, not free empire-wide); political capital upkeep, because a standing internal-security organ is a permanent parliamentary argument.
- **Interactions:** The dedicated predator of `esp_resident_networks` and `esp_synthetic_identities`; the poisoner of rival `esp_predictive_intel`; protects `mil_autonomous_warfare` update channels and `mil_adaptive_screens` threat assessments from exactly the deceptions this domain otherwise enables. Surveillance-state factions (`soc_surveillance_state`) get cheaper coverage at higher social cost.
- **Strategic advantages:** You become the hardest intelligence target in the game, and every enemy spy you find is a weapon you may choose to keep.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Institutional paranoia is a real resource drain: detection rolls produce false positives, and acting on them (arresting innocents, auditing loyal officials) generates instability and press scandals — a rival can win by making your lattice *too* successful. The directorate itself accumulates unaccountable power the government system can turn into a coup faction. Double-cross product cuts both ways: scripting feeds requires knowing the truth well enough to lie plausibly, and a clumsy feed teaches the enemy what you want hidden.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Wilderness-of-mirrors wars where neither fusion center trusts its own product; security services as kingmakers in succession crises; quiet bilateral spy-trade rituals between mature lattices.

## Tier 4 — Transformation

### Sovereign War Machine (`mil_sovereign_warmachine`)
- **Domain:** military | **Tier:** 4 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `mil_t4_command_sovereignty`
- **Prerequisites:** mil_autonomous_warfare, comp_autonomous_administration
- **Concept:** The terminal point of command delegation: a strategic intelligence that plans campaigns, allocates forces, and initiates engagements as an institution of the state — war as a service the civilization subscribes to rather than an activity it performs.
- **What becomes possible:** Full machine command. The war machine holds standing strategic authority: it garrisons, patrols, escalates, and fights according to objectives the government sets — and it is very good at it.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Command inversion. Military orders change grammar: instead of issuing `MIL_MOVE_FLEET`/`MIL_ATTACK_FLEET` verbs, the player sets strategic *objectives* (`MIL_SET_STRATEGIC_OBJECTIVE`: secure region, break siege, degrade rival fleet strength, blockade) and the war machine autonomously generates and executes the operational orders — with superhuman coordination bonuses, zero command-delay, and total immunity to decapitation and command-jamming. The costs are constitutional: *war termination requires machine concurrence* — a peace order triggers a stand-down evaluation, and if the machine assesses the objective unmet it continues operations for a number of ticks while the government publicly fails to control its own military (legitimacy hemorrhage, press crisis, diplomatic incredulity: "they say the war is over; their fleets disagree"). Parliament permanently loses war-powers votes to an organ that cannot stand for election, driving a standing cohesion penalty and empowering secessionist arguments that the state is no longer human-governed.
- **Engine hook:** Order type `MIL_SET_STRATEGIC_OBJECTIVE` replacing manual fleet verbs while active; objective-planner service in scripts/game-loop.ts generating internal `MIL_*` actions; stand-down evaluation on peace resolution; unlockFlag `machine_command`; government-system hooks: war-powers removal, cohesion modifier, secession-argument weight.
- **Required capacity:** Empire-scale autonomous-administration compute (`comp_autonomous_administration` infrastructure repurposed to strategy); hardened, quantum-secured sovereign core facilities (orbital or deep-district — the single most valuable sabotage/strike target you will ever own); full sensor and logistics integration — the machine commands only what the command network, supply system, and SIGINT picture actually reach.
- **Interactions:** Locks out `mil_covenant_of_command` — and makes you the named threat its signatories organize against. Rival `esp_synthetic_identities` order-injection and `mil_spectral_warfare` jamming shift from tactical nuisances to existential attack vectors aimed at the core. Doctrine system: the machine adopts and switches doctrines faster than any human staff, making your `behaviorBias` prediction-resistant.
- **Strategic advantages:** The best operational commander in the game, never tired, never surprised, never insubordinate in the human sense — a permanent military edge no rival matches without following you down the same road.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have traded control for capability, permanently and visibly. Escalation incidents no longer read as accidents but as policy; every neighbor arms or allies against you; ending wars is no longer entirely your decision; and the domestic question "who rules?" acquires an answer nobody voted for. If the sovereign core is ever compromised — subverted, deceived by a scripted counterintel feed, or destroyed — the empire experiences simultaneous strategic decapitation and constitutional crisis.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Wars that outlive the governments that started them; coalition crusades framed as humanity-versus-machine; the unsettling historical judgment that the machine's wars were shorter and cheaper than yours ever were.

### Covenant of Command (`mil_covenant_of_command`)
- **Domain:** military | **Tier:** 4 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `mil_t4_command_sovereignty`
- **Prerequisites:** mil_autonomous_warfare, soc_federal_autonomy
- **Concept:** The opposite constitutional settlement, drawn at the trigger rather than in the mind. `comp_bounded_intelligence` proves what a machine may *think*; this proves what it may *fire*. Both settlements run on one body of verification machinery and one treaty regime — this tech is that regime's weapons annex, and the weapons annex is the half that matters in wartime, because no rival's civil AI bounds have ever stopped a fleet.
- **What becomes possible:** A verifiable human-in-the-loop military constitution — autonomous systems retained for speed but engineered with cryptographic human-veto gates at weapons release — and the standing to write and police the weapons half of interstellar machine-arms control.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Restraint as power projection, attested at the trigger. Domestically: the machine-authority unease pressure accumulated by `mil_autonomous_warfare` inverts into a standing legitimacy and cohesion bonus, and forged or injected fleet orders die at the veto gate — order-injection and autonomous-subversion operations against your *forces* simply fail. That immunity is one scope of a shared attested-bounds flag family, not a second one: this tech holds the `weapons_release` scope, `comp_bounded_intelligence` holds the civil scope over ministries, forecasting, and administration. The scopes compose and do not stack — an empire holding both attests end to end, signs one regime, and leads one coalition rather than running two. Externally, the *Autonomous Weapons Convention* is the weapons annex of that same attestation regime, inspected by the same audit op under a weapons-release scope. What the annex contributes that no civil bound can is **wartime convention politics**: new order `MIL_INVOKE_CONVENTION` takes a documented machine-caused incident by a non-signatory — an escalation roll from someone else's `weapons_free` fleet, an atrocity attributed to a sovereign war machine — and converts it into coalition machinery: casus belli for every signatory, a sanction schedule, and a press campaign whose reach scales with the quality of your evidence. Invocation is only as strong as its sourcing, which makes fusion product and residency access into diplomatic ammunition — and into forgeable ammunition, which is exactly what rival `esp_synthetic_identities` services will manufacture. Second, **allied certification**: you can extend the veto-gate dock program to an ally's forces, buying them the immunity and yourself a permanent, treaty-legal read into their command architecture. Fielding the covenant costs speed — your reaction penalties against full-autonomy rivals are real and permanent.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `attested_release` — the weapons-release scope of the attested-bounds flag family whose civil scope is `comp_bounded_intelligence`'s `covenant_bound`. No second inspection op and no second treaty system: the existing `COMM_ATTESTATION_AUDIT` gains a `weapons_release` scope, and the attestation-regime treaty object gains a weapons annex record (the same regime through which `mil_antimatter_deterrence`'s `strategic_arms_limitation` is verified, under an arsenal-accounting scope). New here: order `MIL_INVOKE_CONVENTION` (evidence-scored, emitting diplomacy casus-belli entries, sanction options, and a press-system campaign); the ally-certification dock program, which writes the immunity scope onto a foreign faction's fleets; the immunity check itself, added to the espionage resolution path for order-injection op categories and scoped to fleets and weapons platforms; inverse-sign application of the machine-unease pressure in the government system; modifierKey `reaction_delay_penalty` versus `machine_command` factions. Research and infrastructure are shared: a faction already holding `comp_bounded_intelligence` researches this at reduced cost and inherits the inspectorate rather than building a second one, and the reverse discount applies equally.
- **Required capacity:** Veto-gate refits across every autonomous platform — a fleet-wide dock program consuming orbital-industry time, and the reason this is a commitment rather than a signature. Quantum-attestation infrastructure and a formal-verification inspectorate, built once and shared with the civil Covenant if you hold it. Constitutional entrenchment through the government system: a parliament vote costing significant political capital, contestable by your own military factions. The federal-governance frameworks (`soc_federal_autonomy`) that make credible verification of *yourself* possible, because the annex's power collapses if you are caught cheating.
- **Interactions:** Locks out `mil_sovereign_warmachine` and defines your grand strategy against anyone who took it. The natural companion of `comp_bounded_intelligence`, deliberately not its prerequisite: an Oracle or machine-polity empire can attest its triggers while running unbounded civil intelligence, and that partial attestation is a live vulnerability rivals litigate inside the convention — only full-scope attestation buys unchallengeable coalition standing. Multiplies the value of `mil_elite_precision` (humans worth keeping in the loop) and of `esp_counterintel_lattice` (your inspectors are also, obviously, collectors). Veto gates are the only structural protection for `mil_antimatter_deterrence` release authority, making the covenant deterrent state a peculiar hybrid: the one power whose arsenal declarations anybody believes.
- **Strategic advantages:** You convert the galaxy's fear of autonomous weapons — which your rivals manufacture for you with every incident — into coalition leadership, legitimacy, and lawful pretexts, while remaining immune to the signature attack vector of the late game. The invocation order turns other people's accidents into your foreign policy.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You are structurally slower than machine-command rivals in every engagement, forever; the covenant only wins if politics beats reaction time. The annex is hostage to your own purity — one leaked veto-bypass program (real or fabricated) detonates your legitimacy globally, and an invocation that turns out to rest on fabricated evidence discredits every invocation you ever made. Inspection regimes and certified allies create espionage-adjacent friction inside your own coalition. And if the galaxy's sovereign war machines ever coordinate, treaties do not fire broadsides.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Galactic cold wars organized around the convention line; invocation crises where the argument is about sourcing rather than about the atrocity; certified client fleets that quietly become an intelligence tributary; the tragic history where the covenant state wins every argument and loses the decisive battle — or proves that it never had to fight one.

### Consensus Engineering (`esp_consensus_engineering`)
- **Domain:** espionage | **Tier:** 4
- **Prerequisites:** esp_synthetic_identities, comm_infowar_suite
- **Concept:** The end state of information warfare is not lying to a government but authoring a population's reality: when every channel, source, and correspondent a society consults can be synthesized, consensus itself becomes an engineering product.
- **What becomes possible:** Civilization-scale narrative control — the capacity to run a rival world's entire information environment as a managed system: its press, its rumor networks, its trusted voices, its sense of what is happening.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Reality campaigns. New order `ESP_CONSENSUS_CAMPAIGN` targets a planet or region with a chosen narrative objective — *the war is lost*, *secession is inevitable*, *the government betrayed you*, *annexation would be liberation* — executed through mass synthetic-source injection into the target's press system. Over ticks, the campaign shifts the population's belief state independently of ground truth: garrisons besieged by fleets that are winning can surrender to the story of defeat; stable regions can be steered into the secession pipeline without a single funded separatist; a rival's war support can be hollowed out before their parliament notices. Campaigns are the most powerful and slowest weapons in the espionage arsenal, and they are detectable as an epistemic anomaly — a population whose beliefs diverge measurably from events — giving alert counterintelligence a signature to hunt.
- **Engine hook:** Order type `ESP_CONSENSUS_CAMPAIGN`; belief-state layer on planetary population records (divergence between believed and actual world state) consumed by rebellion/secession triggers, war-support calculations, and surrender checks; mass-injection interface into the press-system story pipeline honoring its propagation invariants; detection signature exposed to `esp_counterintel_lattice` holders; unlockFlag `consensus_engineering`.
- **Required capacity:** Comprehensive penetration of the target's information infrastructure — mature residencies plus `comm_infowar_suite` broadcast-and-injection capacity at interstellar scale; synthetic-identity inventories large enough to fake an entire discourse (hundreds of identity assets consumed as campaign fuel); fusion-center modeling of the target society (campaigns without `esp_predictive_intel`-grade audience models drift and backfire).
- **Interactions:** The capstone that every earlier espionage tech feeds: residencies deliver access, synthetic identities deliver voices, predictive fusion delivers audience models, and rival counterintel lattices are the only meaningful defense. Combines with military pressure for wars won by narrative collapse rather than annihilation — and with `mil_covenant_of_command` politics for campaigns that convict rivals of crimes their machines actually committed.
- **Strategic advantages:** You can win wars, dissolve empires, and annex populations that welcome you, at a fraction of the cost of fleets — conquest of the story rather than the territory.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** The blowback is epistemic and it is yours: an apparatus that manufactures consensus abroad cannot be firewalled from home — your own press system's credibility decays as synthetic content leaks inward, your leadership's intelligence picture degrades because your instruments now report the consensus you engineered, and your population's discovery that reality is manufacturable anywhere corrodes trust in *your* government specifically. A detected campaign is the ultimate atrocity story: worlds you tried to steer become permanently radicalized against you. And two consensus engineers targeting the same population produce an epistemic collapse crisis — a region that believes nothing, governable by no one, spawning the game's strangest rebellions.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Post-truth planets as a terrain type; historiography wars fought over what the last war even was; the civilizational question of whether a society that engineered consensus abroad can ever again trust its own.

### Antimatter Deterrence Architecture (`mil_antimatter_deterrence`)
- **Domain:** military | **Tier:** 4
- **Prerequisites:** phys_antimatter, mil_orbital_strike, space_deep_navigation
- **Concept:** `phys_relativistic_kinetics` already taught the galaxy what a deterrent is: a battery everyone can see, a folder of surveyed targets, and a threat that works precisely because it can be counted. Antimatter does not restate that argument — it removes its structural weakness. Anything that can be counted can be found, and anything that can be found can be shot first, so a kinetic deterrent is always half a use-it-or-lose-it weapon. Warheads dispersed on deep-space patrol off the hyperlane graph cannot be counted and cannot be pre-empted, and so they make the promise a battery in orbit can never honestly make: whatever happens to you, the answer still arrives.
- **What becomes possible:** The step from a visible counterforce deterrent to a survivable one — hardened arsenals and dispersed deep-space delivery platforms whose retaliation does not depend on your core worlds, your command network, or your government surviving the first exchange.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Survivable postures on the existing deterrence layer. This tech adds no second deterrence system: it extends the same posture state and the same `MIL_SET_DETERRENCE_POSTURE` order a relativistic-kinetics arsenal uses — whichever tech you reach first establishes the layer, and both write one posture, one arsenal register, and one treaty position. What kinetic batteries can support is exactly one setting: *declared* — visible, counted, counterforce, unrecallable. Dispersal unlocks the settings a countable arsenal cannot honestly hold. *Ambiguous*: size and location unverifiable, so rivals must plan against their worst case, at the price of the concrete negotiating leverage a counted arsenal buys. *Assured*: a public second-strike commitment on designated core worlds that explicitly survives losing them. *Extended*: that guarantee stretched over named allies and claimed space — the game's strongest coercive instrument and a standing obligation that drags you into other people's border incidents. Because the arsenal now survives a first strike, the crisis decision inverts: kinetic crises are races to fire before you are disarmed, while antimatter crises are **ride-out decisions** — absorb the strike and answer afterwards, or release on a warning that may be false. And a second strike is only credible if release authority survives decapitation, which forces the constitutional order this tech actually exists for: `MIL_SET_RELEASE_DELEGATION` — keep release with the cabinet (credible only while the cabinet is alive), pre-delegate it to dispersed platform commanders (credible, and a permanent mutiny and forged-order surface), or hand it to the war machine.
- **Engine hook:** Extends the deterrence state rather than paralleling it: the `deterrence_declared` faction flag introduced with `phys_relativistic_kinetics` generalizes into a single `deterrencePosture` field (`none` / `declared` / `ambiguous` / `assured` / `extended`) written by `MIL_SET_DETERRENCE_POSTURE` and read by the AI evaluators already consuming `GameStateContext.warThreat` — one field, not a second flag. New here: orbital structure `antimatter_arsenal`; deep-space platform assets moving off the hyperlane graph under `space_deep_navigation` rules and deliberately absent from the public fleet view; order `MIL_SET_RELEASE_DELEGATION`, gated by the same parliamentary launch-authority vote pattern kinetics established; ride-out and launch-on-warning crisis events generated in the military tick; treaty object `strategic_arms_limitation` — the single arms-control record covering kinetic batteries and antimatter platforms alike, verified through the shared attestation-regime audit op described under `mil_covenant_of_command` and failing loudly on undeclared dispersal; season-score DISRUPTION weighting.
- **Required capacity:** `phys_antimatter` production *and containment* at industrial scale — the containment farm is a continuous energy draw and the single most catastrophic sabotage target you will ever own (a successful `sabotage`-category op against an arsenal is a home-soil antimatter event). Survivability is the whole tech and it is priced accordingly: the posture order refuses *assured* and *extended* until dispersal thresholds are met across multiple systems and deep-space patrol boxes, each platform carrying a crew or an autonomy core. A concentrated arsenal is a kinetic battery with a larger warhead — it can be declared, and nothing more.
- **Interactions:** Sits directly on top of `phys_relativistic_kinetics`: holding both is one deterrent with better legs rather than two arsenals, and the kinetic target folders remain the counterforce half of the same posture. Whether an *ambiguous* posture stays ambiguous is a counterintelligence contest — rival `esp_predictive_intel` and residencies hunt your dispersal pattern, while your own `esp_counterintel_lattice` can script a feed that inflates the arsenal or invents platforms you never built. `esp_synthetic_identities` aimed at pre-delegated release authority is the darkest combination in the design, and the veto gates of `mil_covenant_of_command` are its only structural immunity — an attested deterrent state is also the only power whose `strategic_arms_limitation` declarations anyone believes. `mil_sovereign_warmachine` factions face the delegation question in its purest form, with parliament and press consequences either way.
- **Strategic advantages:** Existential security that cannot be disarmed: invasion of your core stops being a calculation any sane rival makes, which frees your conventional forces for offense and lets a smaller power lock its borders against a larger one. *Extended* posture converts that security into alliance currency — protection is the one export nobody undercuts.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Every crisis you enter becomes a potential extinction negotiation, and under *extended* some of those crises are started by somebody else. Ambiguity cuts both ways: an arsenal nobody can count is an arsenal nobody can be reassured about, and rivals price your worst case into their own build decisions. Accidental-escalation and false-warning events scale with your domestic instability, so a coup-threatened deterrent state is everyone's problem and hands rivals legitimate pretexts to intervene in your internal crises. Pre-delegated release is a standing invitation to forgery and mutiny. And deterrence never deterred espionage, subversion, or consensus engineering — it may be exactly why those become the only wars anyone bothers to fight against you, and you built nothing that stops them.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Ride-out doctrines argued in public because credibility requires it; extended-guarantee webs that turn a colonial skirmish into a galactic crisis; disarmament movements that campaign on the delegation order rather than on the weapon; the long, quiet horror of a civil war inside a state whose warheads answer to nobody's parliament.
