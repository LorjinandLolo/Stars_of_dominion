# Biology & Medicine

Playing into Biology & Medicine means treating your population — not your fleet, not your factories — as the thing you engineer. Where other domains bolt machinery onto the world, this domain rewrites the settlers, the crops, the ecosystems, and eventually the species itself. Early investment feels like public infrastructure: genome registries, vat-food plants, seeded ecologies that slowly green a frontier sector. By mid-game it becomes a chain of genuinely uncomfortable decisions: do you edit the germline of every child born under your flag, or sell enhancement through charter corporations to whoever can pay? Do you terraform the toxic world, or terraform the colonists? Every capability in this domain works on people, so every capability generates politics — parliament debates, press scandals, adapted populations that stop thinking of themselves as yours.

The identity this domain enables is the civilization that outlasts and out-adapts rather than out-shoots. A biology-focused empire colonizes worlds nobody else can hold, feeds itself from industrial districts when agricultural worlds are blockaded, grows buildings instead of building them, and keeps its founding generation alive — and in office — for centuries. The price is written into its own demographics: divergent subspecies with separatist instincts, gerontocratic parliaments that never turn over, living industry that can catch a plague, and a genome archive that is the single most damaging thing an enemy spy can steal. At Tier 4 the domain stops asking "what can we grow" and asks "what should we become" — and forces the player to answer permanently.

## Tier 1 — Expansion

### Population Genomics (`bio_genomic_medicine`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** comp_distributed_compute
- **Concept:** Whole-population genome sequencing becomes cheap enough to run as routine public health: every citizen's genome is on file, and epidemiology becomes a data science rather than a guessing game.
- **What becomes possible:** A civilization-wide Genome Archive — the medical ledger of your species. Outbreaks are traced in hours, hereditary disease is mapped planet by planet, and every later intervention in this domain (gene mods, adaptation packages, longevity treatment) has a dataset to work from.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Introduces the plague/health event layer: planets can now suffer outbreak events, and this tech gives the tools to contain them (quarantine order per surface region, mortality mitigation). It also creates the Genome Archive as a stealable strategic asset — a new high-value espionage target that did not exist before you built it.
- **Engine hook:** New building id `genome_archive` (research category, one per planet, capital-region biased). New service `lib/bio/health-service.ts` ticked from scripts/game-loop.ts alongside `PopulationService.tickPopulation`, emitting outbreak events that apply a mortality term (population service currently has growth only — this adds the negative branch). New order `BIO_QUARANTINE_REGION { planetId, regionId }` in the order queue. Espionage: add op catalog entry `steal_genome_archive` (launched via existing `ESP_LAUNCH_OP`), which flags the victim's faction as `genomeCompromised` — a precondition later ops exploit.
- **Required capacity:** Knowledge alone does nothing: you need a constructed `genome_archive` on a planet plus distributed-compute coverage (comp_distributed_compute infrastructure) to enroll that planet's population. Unenrolled planets get no outbreak mitigation and cannot receive any later bio program.
- **Interactions:** Prerequisite spine for nearly the whole domain. Quarantine interacts with the logistics/distribution service (a quarantined region stops contributing haulage). `steal_genome_archive` feeds `bio_tailored_pathogens` targeting precision for whoever stole it.
- **Strategic advantages:** Outbreaks stop being random attrition; population season scoring (POPULATION, STABILITY categories) stabilizes; everything downstream in the domain unlocks per-planet only where the archive exists, so early adopters get a long head start.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** The archive is a permanent attack surface — once stolen, it cannot be un-stolen, and it makes enemy bioweapons and synthetic-identity ops against you materially better. Privacy backlash: planets with low happiness treat mandatory enrollment as surveillance, generating unrest ticks until happiness recovers; the press system can run "genetic registry" scandal stories if an opposition party holds parliament seats.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Civilizations that skip it become the soft targets of the late-game bioweapon meta; civilizations that build it everywhere drift toward surveillance-state politics (natural synergy, and shared scandal exposure, with soc_surveillance_state).

### Industrial Biofabrication (`bio_industrial_biofab`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** infra_planetary_grid
- **Concept:** Cultured protein, precision fermentation, and tissue-scaffold vats scale from labs to district-sized plants: food becomes an industrial product synthesized from energy and feedstock chemicals rather than grown on land.
- **What becomes possible:** Cities that feed themselves. A forge world with a black sky can produce calories in the same district that produces hull plate.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** A new production chain — energy + chemicals → biomass → food — that runs off terrain that agriculture cannot use. Biofab plants can be placed in industrial and urban sectors (the 64-sector surface board), for the first time decoupling food from `agricultural` region kinds and from foodOutput-penalized specializations (mining_world, forge_world).
- **Engine hook:** New building id `biofabrication_plant` (industrial category, tags `food`), valid on `urban`/`industrial`/`ruins` terrain. Economy service: new conversion recipe consuming energyOutput and chemicalsOutput, producing foodOutput; plugs into the existing storage/distribution ledgers in lib/logistics. Unlock flag `ENABLE_BIOFAB_CHAIN`.
- **Required capacity:** A planetary grid connection (infra_planetary_grid track level) and chemicals supply via the distribution network. A blockaded planet with no chemical imports and a weak grid cannot actually run its plants — the tech is knowledge; the calories are logistics.
- **Interactions:** Rescues forge/mining specializations from their food penalty. Makes `plan_closed_ecologies` habitats cheaper to provision. Later feeds biomass to `bio_synthetic_biology` living industry as its input resource.
- **Strategic advantages:** Siege resistance (fortress worlds that cannot be starved out), freedom to specialize planets aggressively, and independence from agricultural trade partners.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Food is now downstream of the power grid — a grid-sabotage espionage op or orbital strike on power infrastructure becomes a famine weapon against you, which it never was before. Agricultural regions lose economic purpose: their farmer social groups (SurfaceRegion.socialGroups) take loyalty and employment hits, raising unrest exactly on your oldest, most settled worlds. Agricultural trade partners lose leverage and know it — expect diplomatic friction with breadbasket empires.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Empires that fully convert can weaponize food-independence in cold wars (embargoes stop working on them); the displaced-farmer grievance is raw material for rebellion seeding and enemy `ESP_INCITE_UNREST` ops for decades.

### Ecological Succession Engineering (`bio_ecoforming`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** plan_closed_ecologies
- **Concept:** Instead of terraforming with machinery, you seed engineered pioneer species — lichens, soil bacteria, hardy grasses — that run ecological succession on fast-forward, each generation preparing the ground for the next.
- **What becomes possible:** Greening a world sector by sector with living systems that maintain themselves, at a fraction of the cost of climate machinery — and a fraction of the speed and control.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Sector-level ecological engineering on the 64-district surface board: target a sector, seed it, and its habitability climbs over many ticks — and then spreads along sector adjacency to neighbors, whether you asked it to or not. This is the first mechanic that edits `SurfaceSector.habitability` at runtime, and the first one the player does not fully control.
- **Engine hook:** New order `BIO_SEED_ECOSYSTEM { planetId, sectorIndex }` handled in scripts/game-loop.ts. New service `lib/bio/ecology-service.ts` ticks seeded sectors: +habitability per tick toward a terrain-dependent ceiling, with a spread roll along `SurfaceSector.neighbors[]`. Spread into `mining`/`industrial` region kinds applies a small extraction-output penalty (rewilded pit-heads). Determinism note: habitability changes are worker-authoritative state, respecting the Voronoi determinism contract (seeds unchanged; only habitability mutates). **New engine work this tech owns:** `SurfaceSector.habitability` (lib/planet-surface/types.ts:77) has no consumers anywhere in lib/ today, so seeding a sector currently changes a number nobody reads. This tech ships the first consumer — a habitability pass in `PopulationService.tickPopulation` that recomputes `planet.popCapacity` (lib/construction/construction-types.ts:154) as the existing housing-derived capacity scaled by the mean habitability of the sectors the planet's tiles occupy, joined through `PlanetTile.sectorIndex` (construction-types.ts:106). That pass is the shared spine for the rest of this line: adaptation, pantropy and Gaian Synthesis all move habitability and all expect it to mean something.
- **Required capacity:** A seed-bank facility (`ecoforming_station` building) on-planet and a live biosphere baseline — sectors with `toxic` or `volcanic` terrain refuse seeding at this tier (that requires `bio_enviro_adaptation`-grade organisms or full `plan_terraforming`). Frozen sectors seed at half rate.
- **Interactions:** The cheap-slow alternative to `plan_climate_control`; stacks with it (climate control raises the ceiling ecoforming climbs toward). Rising habitability pays out through the habitability→`popCapacity` pass introduced above — the same pass `bio_enviro_adaptation` and `bio_pantropy` write into, which is why greening a world and adapting its settlers compound instead of running on separate books. Sets up `bio_gaian_synthesis` at Tier 4.
- **Strategic advantages:** Near-zero upkeep habitability growth on marginal worlds; frontier sectors become settleable without waiting for Tier 3 terraforming; excellent on montane/arid archetypes with many mediocre sectors.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Living systems drift: periodic ecology events (blooms, invasives, die-backs) can overshoot into sectors you wanted industrial, and unseeding is far more expensive than seeding. Ecoformed sectors are vulnerable to enemy ecological sabotage once tailored pathogens exist. Wilderness spread can strand planned district build-outs.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Long-held ecoformed worlds develop "green belt" identities whose populations resist industrial rezoning — a future source of environmental-faction politics in parliament.

## Tier 2 — Specialization

### Applied Gene Engineering (`bio_gene_engineering`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** bio_genomic_medicine, bio_industrial_biofab
- **Concept:** CRISPR-descended editing matures from single-gene fixes to reliable multi-trait design in crops, livestock, and — under strict license — humans. The genome stops being a given and becomes a medium.
- **What becomes possible:** Designed crop strains for any climate, hereditary disease erased where the archive reaches, and the legal-scientific foundation for editing people, which every faction's parliament immediately starts arguing about.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Gene-mod projects: a new planetary project type with a target and a rollout period. Launchable projects at this tier: climate-strain crops (removes terrain penalty for food buildings in one chosen terrain type per project — desert farms, jungle farms), and hereditary-health programs (permanent small popGrowth bonus on archived planets). Each project is a discrete, sabotage-able, per-planet commitment — not an empire-wide stat.
- **Engine hook:** New order `BIO_LAUNCH_GENEMOD { planetId, projectId }` in the order queue; projects tracked in a `lib/bio/genemod-service.ts` with rollout progress per planet, ticked in game-loop. Crop-strain results write a terrain-exemption flag consumed by the economy service's building-output pass. Unlock flag `ENABLE_GENEMOD_PROJECTS` gates the UI panel. Espionage: gene-mod projects appear in the op catalog as sabotage targets (`ESP_SABOTAGE_FACILITY` variant against the rollout).
- **Required capacity:** Per-planet `genome_archive` (population projects) or `biofabrication_plant` (crop projects) — knowledge is empire-wide, deployment is strictly per-planet infrastructure. Human projects additionally require planetary stability above a floor: nobody rolls out germline medicine mid-insurrection.
- **Interactions:** Gateway to the enhancement fork, `bio_enviro_adaptation`, `bio_longevity`, and `bio_synthetic_biology`. Crop strains combine with `bio_ecoforming` (green the sector, then farm it). A stolen genome archive lets rivals design blights against your specific crop strains.
- **Strategic advantages:** Terrain stops dictating your food map; the health programs compound quietly for the rest of the game; and you enter the enhancement-politics era on your own terms instead of reacting to neighbors who got there first.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Engineered monoculture: because every planet plants the same designed strain, a single blight event (natural or hostile) hits every world running that project simultaneously — efficiency bought with correlated risk. Human gene-mod projects trigger a standing parliament debate; parties opposed generate political-capital costs every rollout, and the press system gains a "designed children" scandal template that fires on any project failure.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** The empire-wide crop monoculture becomes a strategic pressure point rivals actively probe; the human-editing debate hardens party lines years before the Tier 4 species-destiny choice makes them existential.

### Environmental Adaptation (`bio_enviro_adaptation`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** bio_gene_engineering, bio_ecoforming
- **Concept:** Somatic therapy and limited heritable tuning let human physiology meet hostile environments halfway: thicker capillary beds for thin atmospheres, cold-adapted metabolism, toxin-scrubbing liver mods.
- **What becomes possible:** Colonists engineered for the world instead of the world engineered for the colonists — settlement of sectors and archetypes that would otherwise wait decades for terraforming.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Adaptation packages: per-planet colonist templates keyed to planet archetype (frozen, arid, toxic-margin, oceanic). An active package raises what a hostile world can actually hold and how fast it fills — frozen, desert, oceanic and toxic-margin sectors stop being dead space and start carrying people — because the settlers no longer treat that terrain as a penalty. Crucially, adapted colonists are recorded as a distinct population: a new "Adapted" social group is injected into settled regions with its own loyalty/militancy profile.
- **Engine hook:** New order `BIO_SET_ADAPTATION_PACKAGE { planetId, packageId }`. The payout is written on the two fields the population model actually reads: an active package applies a standing modifier to `planet.popGrowth` and `planet.popCapacity` (lib/construction/construction-types.ts:154-155), sized by how far the package closes the gap between the world's archetype and baseline tolerance. This matters because `PopulationService.updatePlanetPopulation` (lib/construction/population-service.ts:23-32) grows population by `popGrowth` scaled by happiness and clamps it at `popCapacity`, and takes no habitability input whatsoever — there is no "habitability floor" to raise until something builds one. Where a package should open specific dead sectors rather than lift the whole world, it does so through the habitability→`popCapacity` pass specified under `bio_ecoforming`: packages write a per-archetype habitability floor into that pass, so an adapted world computes capacity as if its hostile sectors were merely poor. Planet-surface: inject `SocialGroupSeed { id: 'adapted_settlers', loyalty: baseline-10, militancy: baseline+10 }` into regions settled after activation. New per-planet scalar `geneticDivergence` (0–100) introduced in `lib/bio/divergence-service.ts`, ticking upward slowly on adapted worlds and read by lib/government/cohesion-service.ts as a cohesion drag.
- **Required capacity:** Genome archive plus a `germline_clinic` building on the target planet; packages apply to new growth and new settlers, not retroactively — converting an existing population is slow and generates unrest. Toxic and volcanic full-settlement remains locked until `bio_pantropy`.
- **Interactions:** Multiplies with `bio_ecoforming` (adapt the people while the ecology climbs). Alternative to, and much cheaper than, `plan_climate_control`/`plan_terraforming` — but the planetary techs fix the world for everyone, while this forks your population. `soc_federal_autonomy` eases the political cost of adapted worlds; `soc_surveillance_state` suppresses it while raising militancy.
- **Strategic advantages:** Expansion into map regions rivals cannot hold; hostile-archetype worlds make natural fortress and mining specializations; first-mover claims on "worthless" systems along the hyperlane graph.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** This is where species divergence begins. Adapted populations accumulate `geneticDivergence`, dragging planetary cohesion and adding weight to secession scoring; they are also physiologically mismatched to standard worlds, so population transfers off-world are penalized — your frontier pops are stuck on their frontier. Baseline-majority planets develop friction politics against "the Adapted" that enemy `ESP_INCITE_UNREST` ops can fan.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Two generations on, adapted worlds have adapted-majority parliaments of their own; the empire either federalizes, suppresses, or — at Tier 4 — decides what its species even is.

### The Germline Charter (`bio_germline_charter`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** bio_gene_engineering, soc_digital_governance
- **Concept:** The state assumes stewardship of the species genome: standardized heritable enhancements — disease immunity, cognitive resilience, longevity groundwork — delivered universally through natal care, encoded in law, and inherited forever.
- **What becomes possible:** Every child born under your flag is born improved, at negligible marginal cost, compounding generation over generation. Enhancement as public infrastructure, like literacy.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Natal policy: an empire-level standing program (not a one-off project) that permanently upgrades new population on every archive-covered planet — growth quality, baseline happiness resilience, reduced outbreak mortality — and unlocks charter-specific parliament legislation (enhancement mandates, exemption bans). Enhancement becomes universal, compulsory, and heritable: a civilizational commitment you cannot quietly exit.
- **Engine hook:** `mutuallyExclusiveGroup: 'bio_t2_enhancement_path'`. Unlock flag `ENABLE_GERMLINE_POLICY`; a standing modifier pass in PopulationService applied to the growth delta (new pops only — model as a slowly rising planet-level "charter coverage" fraction). Parliament: new policy ids gated on the flag. Divergence-service: charter worlds drift as a bloc — divergence accrues empire-wide but uniformly, so cohesion drag is low while divergence from other empires' baselines is high.
- **Required capacity:** Genome archive plus digital-governance administrative coverage on each planet (the charter is a registry-and-clinics program; planets without soc_digital_governance infrastructure cannot enforce it). Coverage ramps over years of game time — the payoff is generational, not immediate.
- **Interactions:** Accelerates `bio_longevity` rollout (the groundwork is pre-installed). Uniform genomes make `bio_the_common_genome` at Tier 4 dramatically cheaper. But uniformity cuts both ways: a stolen archive plus your published charter template is a precision-targeting kit for enemy pathogens.
- **Strategic advantages:** The compounding is unmatched — twenty years in, your population is simply better at everything, for free, forever. No inequality mechanics: the charter is universal, so it generates none of the class friction of the market path.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Compulsion: religious and traditionalist social groups treat mandatory germline editing as an atrocity — permanent militancy bump, and any exemption-ban legislation risks a rebellion trigger on low-stability worlds. Irreversibility: charter traits are inherited; a design flaw discovered later is a species-wide defect (rare but catastrophic event hook). Monoculture: maximal correlated vulnerability to tailored pathogens.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** "Charter Generations" cohort politics — enhanced youth versus unenhanced elders — feeding succession crises; other empires' propaganda paints you as a eugenic state, and their refugees' press stories write themselves.

### The Somatic Market (`bio_somatic_market`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** bio_gene_engineering, econ_interstellar_banking
- **Concept:** Enhancement as a service: non-heritable somatic gene therapy — muscle, cognition, immunity, cosmetic speciation — sold by licensed charter corporations to individual buyers, financed by interstellar credit.
- **What becomes possible:** A consumer enhancement economy. Citizens choose their own upgrades; corporations compete on catalogs; the state licenses, taxes, and looks away.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** A new charter-corporation product line: enhancement clinics as corporate ventures generating credit income and trade throughput, with an uptake model — wealthier, happier planets buy more. Enhancement level becomes a per-planet economic stat that boosts productivity and unlocks an "Enhanced professionals" social group. The state's lever is licensing policy (permissive/taxed/restricted), set per-planet through the order queue, trading income against inequality.
- **Engine hook:** `mutuallyExclusiveGroup: 'bio_t2_enhancement_path'`. Unlock flag `ENABLE_SOMATIC_MARKET`; new charter-corp venture type `somatic_clinic_chain` in the corporations system; clinic uptake feeds `PopulationService.getProductivityMultiplier` via a planet enhancement scalar. New order `BIO_SET_CLINIC_POLICY { planetId, policy }`. Espionage: op catalog gains `run_blackmarket_clinics` (hostile op that raises a rival's inequality friction and injects untracked mods).
- **Required capacity:** Interstellar banking rails (econ_interstellar_banking) and at least one chartered corporation to run clinics; uptake requires planetary happiness and trade access — poor, blockaded, or unhappy worlds simply don't buy, so the bonus concentrates where you are already strong.
- **Interactions:** Synergizes with trade_world specializations and econ_automated_markets (clinic revenue rides existing trade throughput). Somatic mods being non-heritable means near-zero `geneticDivergence` — the cohesion-friendly path. Black-market clinics give rival spymasters a lever inside your cities.
- **Strategic advantages:** Immediate economic return instead of generational payoff; reversibility (no species-level commitments); no compulsion politics; strong synergy with commerce-focused builds.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Stratification: the Enhanced/Unmodified split is a class wedge — unmodified social groups on poor worlds gain militancy, and the wedge widens exactly where you can least afford it. Corporate capture: clinic corporations become political actors lobbying parliament (political-capital drain to regulate them). Unlicensed mods from black-market ops are invisible until they cause health events.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Enhancement inequality becomes the standard grievance in your rebellions and civil-war seeding; corporations rich enough from clinics start buying press outlets and parliament votes, a corporate-politics storyline the government system can escalate.

## Tier 3 — Dominance

### Synthetic Biology (`bio_synthetic_biology`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** bio_gene_engineering, mat_advanced_composites
- **Concept:** Organisms designed from base pairs up, with no wild ancestor: cellular chassis that secrete structural composites, digest ore tailings into feedstock chemicals, and grow to blueprint like a building-shaped tree.
- **What becomes possible:** Living industry. Structures that are grown, not built; refineries that are fed, not fueled; infrastructure that heals its own battle damage.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Grown construction: an alternative build mode for eligible buildings — near-zero metals cost, paid instead in biomass and time, with permanent self-repair. Plus the living refinery, a new conversion building digesting biomass into chemicals (inverting the biofab chain). Construction strategy forks: mineral-fast versus biomass-cheap-and-regenerating, chosen per project in the build queue.
- **Engine hook:** Construction system: new `buildMethod: 'grown'` on eligible building definitions — consumes biomass from the biofab chain ledger, longer build ticks, adds `selfRepair` handled where siege/bombardment damage is applied in the combat engine. New building id `living_refinery`. Unlock flag `ENABLE_LIVING_STRUCTURES`. Grown buildings register as valid targets for a new blight sabotage vector.
- **Required capacity:** Biomass surplus (a running biofab chain or strong agricultural base) and a `synthbio_foundry` building per planet to gestate seed-stock; grown construction is terrain-limited to habitability above a floor — you cannot grow a fortress on a dead rock without adaptation or ecoforming first.
- **Interactions:** Biofab supplies the biomass; ecoformed sectors grow structures faster; `mat_molecular_manufacturing` empires get the same flexibility from the mineral side, making the two domains rival industrial philosophies. Feeds `bio_tailored_pathogens` and `bio_gaian_synthesis`.
- **Strategic advantages:** Metals independence for civilian build-out (your ore goes to fleets), self-repairing fortifications that shrug off siege attrition, and reconstruction after bombardment at a fraction of normal cost.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Your infrastructure is now alive, and living things get sick: plague events and enemy tailored blights can now damage buildings, not just people. Living industry eats — a famine or biomass blockade starves your factories and your citizens at once. Doctrinal cultures (and some rival civilizations' event tags) treat grown cities as abominations, coloring diplomacy.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Empires that rebuild war zones with grown structures develop distinctive regrown-city identities; besiegers learn to bring herbicides — expect a counter-tech arms race in ecological warfare.

### Radical Longevity (`bio_longevity`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** bio_gene_engineering, bio_genomic_medicine, econ_dev_finance
- **Concept:** Cellular reprogramming and senolytic therapy arrest aging in adults: death by senescence becomes optional for anyone the treatment reaches. The question stops being medical and becomes: who gets it, and who pays.
- **What becomes possible:** Your grandmother's generation stops dying — and stops retiring, stops vacating housing, stops leaving parliament.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The allocation dilemma, set per-planet through the order queue: Universal (mortality collapses; population piles against popCapacity and growth chokes as housing and jobs never free up; enormous ongoing finance cost), Elite (leaders, cabinet, and top strata only; cheap; a standing legitimacy wound and a class grievance), or Withheld (no benefits, no strain, and treated pops on other empires' worlds become a magnet for your emigrants). Separately, leader mortality effectively ends for treated leaders: cabinet and parliament turnover freezes, transforming the government system's succession dynamics.
- **Engine hook:** New order `BIO_SET_LONGEVITY_POLICY { planetId, policy }`. PopulationService: policy modifies the growth/mortality terms and, under Universal, applies an escalating capacity-pressure unhappiness term as population sits at popCapacity. Government/leadership: treated leaders skip age-out events; cohesion-service gains a `gerontocracy` pressure term that grows with average leadership tenure. Finance: Universal drains treasury via an econ_dev_finance-style standing instrument. Press system: Elite allocation periodically fires "immortality for the few" stories.
- **Required capacity:** Genome archive plus `longevity_clinic` buildings scaled to the covered population — Universal on a large world is a major construction and finance program, not a toggle. A germline-charter population is pre-adapted: clinics are half cost. Somatic-market empires can offload Universal's cost by letting corporations sell treatment (inheriting that path's inequality instead).
- **Interactions:** The gerontocracy term compounds with political-capital mechanics — old leadership hoards capital and blocks reform legislation. Frozen leadership is a boon for espionage continuity (long-serving officials are long-cultivated assets, yours and theirs). Universal allocation makes expansion mandatory: mega_orbital_habitats and colonization become demographic relief valves rather than luxuries.
- **Strategic advantages:** Continuity of excellence — brilliant admirals, scientists, and governors serve forever; skilled-population quality compounds; and immortality is the single strongest emigration magnet in the game, quietly draining rivals' talent.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Demographic gridlock under Universal (a full world with no natural turnover and rising capacity pressure), a permanently smoldering legitimacy wound under Elite, and under any policy the gerontocracy drag: succession never opens, young cohorts radicalize, and reform becomes only achievable through coup mechanics the government system already models. Assassination doctrine changes — killing an immortal minister destroys centuries of irreplaceable experience, raising the payoff of enemy decapitation ops.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Century-old parliaments defending century-old policy; youth-led secession movements with "mortal frontier" identities on worlds where treatment never arrived; the Tier 4 species-destiny question arriving with the same ministers who debated Tier 2.

### Pantropy (`bio_pantropy`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** bio_enviro_adaptation, space_advanced_drives
- **Concept:** Full germline redesign for hostile worlds: not tuning humans for thin air, but engineering settlers for sulfur atmospheres, crushing ocean depths, or cryogenic tundra — changing the colonist so completely that the world needs no changing at all.
- **What becomes possible:** Colonization of toxic, volcanic, frozen, and deep-oceanic worlds as-is — no domes, no terraforming, no timeline. The gene-forges ship out on high-thrust drives and the first native generation is born on-site.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Pantropic colonization: found full colonies on hostile archetypes, with settled sectors resolving at high habitability for pantrope populations specifically through the same habitability→capacity pass the rest of this line feeds. Pantrope worlds are demographically sealed — their people cannot resettle on standard worlds and vice versa — and their populations are constitutionally distinct: new social groups with high autonomy expectations, tracked at high `geneticDivergence` from day one.
- **Engine hook:** Unlock flag `ENABLE_PANTROPY_COLONIZATION` consumed by the colonization order path; per-colony `pantropeTemplate` recorded in planet state. Planet-surface: settled regions seeded with `SocialGroupSeed { id: 'pantropes', loyalty: low, militancy: high }`. Divergence-service: pantrope worlds start at divergence 60+, feeding cohesion-service and adding standing weight in secession-service scoring. Population movement: no inter-planet population transfer exists in lib/ today (the distribution layer in lib/logistics moves resources, not people), so sealing is specified as a precondition on whichever order first ships that capability — the megastructure line's `HAB_TRANSFER_POPULATION` is the standing candidate — namely a `pantropeTemplate` compatibility check that rejects transfers between pantrope and baseline worlds in both directions.
- **Required capacity:** A `pantropy_creche` orbital structure at the target world (gestation and first-generation raising happens in orbit before landfall), high-thrust drive logistics (space_advanced_drives) for delivery, and genome archive coverage at the origin. Each hostile archetype needs its own template researched as a project — toxic first, then volcanic, then deep-ocean.
- **Interactions:** Claims systems that `plan_terraforming` empires need decades and vast energy budgets to touch — the two techs are rival answers to the same map. Pantrope fortress/mining worlds pair brutally well with fortress_world and mining_world specializations (nobody wants to invade a sulfur hell defended by people who breathe it). `soc_federal_autonomy` is nearly mandatory politics; `bio_radiant_speciation` at Tier 4 makes pantropes first-class citizens rather than an anomaly.
- **Strategic advantages:** The map doubles: hostile systems along contested hyperlane corridors become claimable fortress anchors; hostile-world resources flow without terraforming's capital cost; invaders can't occupy what their troops can't breathe on (occupation penalties against non-adapted armies).
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You are deliberately creating a second people. Pantrope worlds carry permanent cohesion drag and elevated secession weight; baseline chauvinist parties gain a wedge issue; and because pantropes cannot leave their worlds, every grievance is trapped at high pressure — pantrope rebellions are rarer but far more total (civil-war-service escalation bias). If a pantrope world secedes, you cannot resettle it with loyalists.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Pantrope-led breakaway states that no one else can conquer or inhabit; "the sealed worlds" as a diplomatic bloc; the Tier 4 reconvergence path turning tragic — converting pantropes back to baseline means depopulating the only worlds they can survive on.

### Tailored Pathogens (`bio_tailored_pathogens`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** bio_synthetic_biology, esp_synthetic_identities
- **Concept:** Designed pathogens with genomic targeting: agricultural blights keyed to a rival's specific crop strain, incapacitating agents tuned to a population's archive profile, delivered by cover identities that leave no return address.
- **What becomes possible:** War against a rival's biology instead of their fleets — silent, deniable, and horrifying when it comes out. It always eventually comes out.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The ecological warfare op family in the espionage catalog: crop blight (targets a planet's food chain; devastating against engineered monocultures), industrial blight (damages grown structures and living refineries), and incapacitating pathogen (population op; mortality and unrest). Precision scales with intel: a stolen genome archive turns crude ops into surgical ones. Every op carries a persistent attribution trail that rival counterintelligence — or your own defectors — can uncover seasons later.
- **Engine hook:** New op catalog entries `bio_crop_blight`, `bio_industrial_blight`, `bio_pathogen_release` in lib/espionage, launched through the existing `ESP_LAUNCH_OP` order path with agent assignment; targeting bonus keyed off the `genomeCompromised` flag from `steal_genome_archive`. Health-service processes the outbreak on the victim world. Attribution: each op writes a sealed evidence record with a per-tick discovery roll; on discovery, diplomacy-state penalty, press-system atrocity stories empire-wide, and a parliament confidence shock at home.
- **Required capacity:** A hidden `blacksite_biolab` building (invisible to normal intel passes, discoverable by rival deep-cover ops), a trained agent network on the target world, and — for precision variants — the victim's genome data. The lab itself is a standing liability: it exists, so it can be found.
- **Interactions:** The counter to every biological strategy in this document: monoculture charters, living industry, and biofab chains are all soft targets. Defensively, empires holding this tech get pathogen-response bonuses (you defend against what you understand). `comm_infowar_suite` pairs with it to muddy attribution; esp_predictive_intel warns you when it is about to be used on you.
- **Strategic advantages:** Cripples a rival's food security or grown infrastructure without a declaration of war; the mere credible possession deters bio-heavy neighbors; and it is the only Tier 3 power that scales with the enemy's sophistication — the more they engineered, the harder they fall.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Blowback: releases carry a mutation roll that can jump back along trade routes into your own worlds — living-industry empires are torching their own philosophy. Attribution is civilization-shaking: a confirmed release makes you a pariah (diplomatic mandates against you, alliance exclusion), triggers parliament revolt or coup risk at home, and legitimizes any retaliation. Merely researching it leaks eventually — expect rivals' genome archives to go dark and their counterintelligence budgets to double.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Bio-arms-control diplomacy (inspection mandates, banned-weapons treaties with espionage verification); false-flag blight ops between third parties with your signature forged; the atrocity record resurfacing decades later to destroy a government that thought it was buried.

## Tier 4 — Transformation

### Radiant Speciation (`bio_radiant_speciation`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 4
- **Prerequisites:** bio_pantropy, bio_longevity, soc_federal_autonomy
- **Concept:** Divergence embraced as destiny: humanity deliberately radiates into engineered clades — void-born for orbital life, abyssal for ocean worlds, high-G stock for superterrestrial planets — bound not by shared biology but by shared civilization.
- **What becomes possible:** A polity of many species. Every environment in your territory gets a people who consider it home, and "human" becomes the name of a federation rather than a genome.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The clade system: design clade templates against planet archetypes and orbital habitats, then assign them per world; clade-matched populations get profound habitability, growth, and output alignment on their home archetype. Parliament restructures into multi-clade representation — a permanent new political axis (clade-interest parties, inter-clade coalition mathematics) layered onto the existing government system. Divergence stops being a penalty scale and becomes the constitutional order.
- **Engine hook:** `mutuallyExclusiveGroup: 'bio_t4_species_destiny'`. Unlock flag `ENABLE_CLADE_SYSTEM`; new order `BIO_ASSIGN_CLADE { planetId, cladeId }`. Divergence-service inverts on completion: `geneticDivergence` cohesion drag is retired and replaced by a clade-representation model in cohesion-service — cohesion now tracks whether each clade holds proportionate parliament seats and cabinet posts, not genetic distance. Population transfer rules key on clade-archetype match rather than baseline/adapted.
- **Required capacity:** Pantropy creche network across your territory, longevity clinics (clade design assumes treated lifespans), and federal-autonomy governance actually enacted — a centralized unitary state cannot legally hold a multi-clade polity, and attempting it triggers immediate constitutional crisis events. Each clade template is a major research-and-gestation project.
- **Interactions:** mega_orbital_habitats and mega_orbital_ring populations get a purpose-built void clade (orbital habitats stop being second-class housing). Terraforming becomes nearly worthless to you — a strategic divergence from plan_* empires that reshapes what map territory is worth fighting over. Clade politics interlocks with press, parliament, and secession systems everywhere.
- **Strategic advantages:** Total territorial flexibility — nothing on the map is uninhabitable; per-world output ceilings unmatched by any other strategy; and a resilience monoculture empires can never have: no single pathogen, environment, or catastrophe can touch all your clades at once.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Politics is now permanently plural: every war, budget, and treaty is negotiated between clade blocs, and mismanaged representation produces clade-nationalist secession movements with ready-made identities. Military integration suffers (mixed-clade armies and crews carry coordination penalties). Baseline-purist rival empires gain a standing casus belli and a propaganda gift: you are, by their lights, no longer human.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Clades that outlive the empire that made them — post-collapse successor states organized purely by clade; inter-clade dynastic politics among immortal leadership; the long question of whether the federation's clades, centuries on, still consider each other kin.

### The Common Genome (`bio_the_common_genome`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 4
- **Prerequisites:** bio_longevity, comp_autonomous_administration
- **Concept:** Reconvergence: a single, maximally resilient panhuman template — broad-environment tolerance, pathogen-proof immunity, charter-perfected health — deployed to every citizen and every descendant, ending the era of divergence by decree.
- **What becomes possible:** One people again, everywhere and permanently: a species-wide standard administered at a scale only machine civil service makes possible.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The Reconvergence Campaign: a multi-season, world-by-world conversion megaproject, sequenced by the player through the order queue. Each world in conversion suffers upheaval (unrest, growth pause, resistance events scaling with its divergence score); each completed world permanently zeroes its divergence and joins the unified baseline. Completion empire-wide retires the divergence system entirely: maximum-cohesion configuration, universal population transferability, and near-immunity to genomic targeting — the archive describes a fortress genome.
- **Engine hook:** `mutuallyExclusiveGroup: 'bio_t4_species_destiny'`. New order `BIO_BEGIN_RECONVERGENCE { planetId }` with campaign state in divergence-service; conversion progress ticked by autonomous-administration capacity. Cohesion-service: converted worlds enter a `unified_baseline` state (cohesion floor raised, secession weight from divergence deleted). Adapted/pantrope worlds in conversion get civil-war-service risk multipliers; pantrope worlds require paired habitability works (climate machinery or relocation) since the common genome cannot survive their environments unassisted — the campaign schedules this automatically and expensively.
- **Required capacity:** Autonomous administration (comp_autonomous_administration) to run trillions of individualized conversions; longevity-clinic networks repurposed as conversion infrastructure on every target world; treasury depth for the pantrope-world habitability works. The campaign is the single largest civilian undertaking in the game — measured in seasons, not ticks.
- **Interactions:** Trivially cheap for germline-charter empires (populations already uniform, coverage infrastructure in place) and agonizing for pantropy empires — which is the point: your Tier 2 and 3 choices price your Tier 4 destiny. Unified baseline is the hardest possible target for `bio_tailored_pathogens`. Pairs naturally with centralizing governments; soc_federal_autonomy empires face constitutional fights over conversion mandates.
- **Strategic advantages:** The endgame cohesion machine: divergence-driven secession disappears, populations flow freely to wherever they're needed, one healthcare system serves everyone, and genomic attack surfaces close. Identity clarity has diplomatic weight — baseline-humanist empires treat you as the standard-bearer.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** The campaign itself is the crisis: converting adapted and pantrope worlds against their will is coercion at civilizational scale — rebellion and civil-war risk peaks mid-campaign, rivals openly arm your separatists, and the press era is defined by conversion atrocity stories whether or not you commit any. And the ending is a monoculture: one genome means one shared vulnerability if anything ever does break through, and permanent forfeiture of hostile-world colonization — the sealed worlds must be terraformed, abandoned, or emptied.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Refugee clades and pantrope diasporas in rival empires nursing the grievance for centuries; a unified species whose next existential debate — machine integration, digital consciousness — arrives with no biological factions left to disagree.

### Gaian Synthesis (`bio_gaian_synthesis`)
- **Domain:** biology-medicine | **Tier:** 4 — *the living-world layer above `plan_gaia_transformation`, not a second route to it*
- **Prerequisites:** bio_synthetic_biology, bio_ecoforming, plan_gaia_transformation
- **Concept:** `plan_gaia_transformation` builds a biosphere that regulates a planet without machines. Gaian Synthesis grafts your grown infrastructure, your food chains and your population into that biosphere until the three run one metabolism — at which point the world stops behaving like terrain and starts behaving like a participant with a position on how it is used.
- **What becomes possible:** A garden world that repairs your cities for free and mounts its own immune response against engineered plagues — and that can be read, argued with, and pushed too far. The planet acquires a condition you can see and a threshold you can cross.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The biosphere agent. Every gaia world you synthesize gains a visible three-state condition — flourishing / stressed / wounded — that the rest of the world model reacts to. *Flourishing:* grown structures self-repair at zero biomass cost, and enemy tailored pathogens meet the biosphere's own immune response instead of your health service. *Stressed:* the world begins refusing you — sector rezoning orders are rejected, districts rewild without approval, scheduled extraction quietly fails to arrive. *Wounded:* self-repair suspends, habitability decays off the gaia ceiling for the first time, and the press system carries it empire-wide. The verb that makes this a decision rather than a gauge is `BIO_STEWARDSHIP_DIRECTIVE` — **reserve**, **managed**, or **pressed** — a per-world declaration of how hard the biosphere is worked, changeable at any time and consequential for seasons afterward. Note what this tech deliberately does *not* do: it performs no archetype conversion and installs no second industry-restriction system. `plan_gaia_transformation` already flips the world to `gaia` and already restricts heavy industry there. Gaian Synthesis supplies the state machine that decides when that restriction's biosphere-revolt events fire — and the only mechanism in the game that can lift it, since a flourishing world will grant a time-boxed industrial exemption to an empire that has banked the goodwill.
- **Engine hook:** Unlock flag `ENABLE_BIOSPHERE_AGENCY`; new order `BIO_STEWARDSHIP_DIRECTIVE { planetId, directive }`; new per-planet fields `biosphereState: 'flourishing' | 'stressed' | 'wounded'` and a `biosphereLoad` accumulator. **No archetype write:** the agent attaches only to planets already carrying the `gaia` archetype set by `plan_gaia_transformation`'s terraform machinery. `lib/bio/ecology-service.ts` (introduced by `bio_ecoforming`) is promoted from per-sector seeding to an additional per-world agent tick that advances the state machine from extraction load, bombardment and population pressure, injects a `biosphere_stewards` social group into wilderness regions, and emits press events. Construction: the agent writes no restrictions of its own — it reads the gaia restrictions already enforced in construction validation, and its single construction-side write is the time-boxed `biosphereExemption` flag. Combat engine: bombardment resolution on a gaia world sets `wounded` and applies faction-wide happiness and legitimacy penalties — to the attacker AND to a defender who fortified a garden into a battlefield. The empire-wide legitimacy aura stays where `plan_gaia_transformation` put it (a global modifier in `PlayerTechState.globalModifiers` scaled by gaia-world count); this tech adds no second aura.
- **Required capacity:** At least one gaia world — transformed or naturally occurring — as substrate, a mature synthetic-biology industrial base to graft into it (the self-repair payoff is grown structures or nothing), and seasons of integration per world. Gaian Synthesis is not itself in `mega_t4_worlds_purpose`; it sits strictly downstream of one branch of that fork, which means a world-eater civilization can never reach it at all.
- **Interactions:** The capstone of the ecological line (ecoforming → synthetic biology → gaia) and the only thing that makes `bio_synthetic_biology`'s grown cities free to maintain. Enemy `bio_tailored_pathogens` attacking a synthesized world fight a living immune response — the hardest bio-defense in the game. Gaian worlds are the ideal home for common-genome populations and the spiritual heartland of speciated federations alike; the Tier 4 species fork does not gate this one. Taken alone, `plan_gaia_transformation` still gives you the garden, the habitability and the aura — this is what makes the garden answer back.
- **Strategic advantages:** Self-healing infrastructure at zero upkeep, an unrest floor from biosphere-linked contentment, the best pathogen defense available, and a legible early-warning system: stress is visible for seasons before a revolt, so the careful player never walks into the disaster the greedy one does.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have created something you do not fully command, and unlike the inert Gaia Transformation it can be pushed into refusing your orders — a wounded world loses habitability the transformation alone would have held at maximum forever. De-synthesis is ecocide, with consequences matching the word. Gaian worlds are hostage-shaped: rivals know that wounding one hurts your whole empire's morale, which makes them first-strike terror targets precisely because attacking them is monstrous. And "pressed" is the profitable directive right up until the tick it isn't.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Biosphere-steward movements demanding political representation for living worlds — machine-government debates arriving by an unexpected door; stewardship directives becoming a parliamentary matter that governments fall over; and the very long game: whether a world that gardens itself, given centuries, still needs its gardeners.
