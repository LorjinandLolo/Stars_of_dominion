# Society & Governance — Technology Web Domain

Playing into Society & Governance means treating your own population as the terrain of the game. Where other domains give you sharper fleets or richer worlds, this domain changes what your civilization *is*: how it counts its people, how it decides, how far the centre can see, and what holds a hundred worlds inside one political order. Nearly every tech here reads from or writes to systems that already run every tick — per-planet cohesion and defiance pressure, the parliament and its bills, political capital, coup pressure, the press cycle, the espionage catalog. The player who invests here rarely wins a battle with these techs; they win the decade after the battle, because their empire bends where others shatter. The cost is that every instrument of governance is also an attack surface, and every increase in state capacity creates a constituency that resents it.

The domain's identity question is asked twice. At Tier 2, a civilization must choose its statecraft: hold the polity together by *watching* it (the Surveillance State), by *negotiating* with it (Federal Autonomy), or by *becoming* it (the Participatory Mandate). These three are mutually exclusive, and because each branch anchors different parts of the wider web — surveillance gates the Oracle State and the Sovereign Mesh, federal gates the Covenant of Worlds and one road to the Enfranchisement, participatory gates the Perpetual Plebiscite and the other road to the Enfranchisement — the choice branch-locks genuinely different futures, by design. At Tier 4 the question returns at civilizational scale: who, finally, is sovereign — a custodial machine, the live will of the whole population, or a covenant of worlds that has stopped pretending to be an empire at all? No answer is safe. Each one retires a familiar problem and replaces it with one no government has faced before.

## Tier 1 — Expansion

### Digital Governance (`soc_digital_governance`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** comp_distributed_compute
- **Concept:** Once computation is planet-spanning, the paperwork of the state — registries, permits, ballots, budgets — can become a single live civic platform rather than an archipelago of ledgers. The state stops estimating its society and starts measuring it.
- **What becomes possible:** A unified civic platform: citizens file, vote, petition, and are counted through one authenticated digital layer that spans every settled district.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The Civic Platform. Two doors open: (1) full political telemetry — the cohesion panel shows the *complete* driver list, target, and trend for every owned planet instead of only the loudest driver; bloc satisfaction becomes visible per-planet, not just empire-wide; (2) advisory referenda — before enacting a policy, the player can poll it, previewing the real approval/legitimacy/bloc deltas it would cause. Governing stops being guesswork.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `civic_platform`; district building id `civic_uplink_hub` (one per Voronoi district, coverage-scaled); new order-queue action `GOV_HOLD_REFERENDUM { policyId }` resolved in scripts/game-loop.ts next major tick, returning the projected effect block from data/policies/*.json evaluated against live bloc state; sync layer exposes full `planetCohesion.drivers` (currently truncated for the client) when the flag is set. Adds espionage catalog entry `breach_civic_platform` (lib/espionage/) valid only against factions carrying the flag.
- **Required capacity:** Knowledge alone shows nothing. Telemetry and referenda function only on planets whose districts contain `civic_uplink_hub` coverage of at least 40% (26 of 64 districts), which requires powered infrastructure (infra_planetary_grid synergy) and a literate connected population; frontier colonies below the population threshold stay dark.
- **Interactions:** Prerequisite root of nearly the whole domain. Feeds esp_sigint targeting (your platform is their signal source). Referendum previews make GOV_ENACT_POLICY and GOV_LOBBY_PARTY decisions informed rather than blind. Press stories move measured sentiment in real time, so the press system becomes visible as a governance instrument.
- **Strategic advantages:** The cheapest legitimacy machine in the game: you stop enacting policies that were always going to fail, and you see cohesion decay while it is still reversible.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** The platform is a single, named attack surface. `breach_civic_platform` lets a rival read the same telemetry you do — your dissent map handed to their espionage planners — and amplifies `election_interference` (the existing GovernmentState field) by a multiplier while the breach is unresolved. A civilization that governs by dashboard also suffers harder when the dashboard lies (see soc_predictive_governance poisoning).
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Empires that habitually pre-poll policies drift toward plebiscitary politics (soc_participatory_mandate feels natural); empires that use the telemetry mainly to find dissent drift toward soc_surveillance_state. The tech quietly sorts players into the Tier 2 fork before they know they are choosing.

### Provincial Charters (`soc_provincial_charters`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** comm_deep_space_network
- **Concept:** No capital can administer thirty worlds across light-hours of lag. The historical answer is the province: a chartered intermediate tier with its own seat, budget, and delegated authority, reachable because the relay network finally carries administration-grade traffic.
- **What becomes possible:** Groups of nearby systems are chartered as provinces with a named provincial capital and an appointed governor who governs in the centre's name.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Provinces. The player draws 2–5 planet groupings along the hyperlane graph, seats a provincial capital, and appoints a governor. Cohesion's distance penalty is recalculated: `distanceFromCapital` becomes hops to the *nearest provincial capital*, not the imperial one — the single strongest lever against frontier decay in the early game. Governors are real leaders with competence, loyalty, and appetite.
- **Engine hook:** New order `GOV_CHARTER_PROVINCE { planetIds, capitalPlanetId }` and extension of the existing `GOV_APPOINT_GOVERNOR` handler to province scope; new service `lib/government/provinces.ts` consumed by tickCohesion when computing `PlanetCohesion.distanceFromCapital`; per-province corruption feeds `GovernmentState.corruption`.
- **Required capacity:** A provincial capital must be a planet with an administrative building (`provincial_seat`, district building) and a working relay link on the hyperlane graph back to the capital; provinces cut off by blockade or lane loss stop functioning and their worlds revert to raw imperial distance — sieges now have political blast radius.
- **Interactions:** Foundation of soc_federal_autonomy and soc_habitat_charters. Governors interact with the coup and civil-war machinery: a disloyal governor of a defiant province is the natural figurehead of a secession. Espionage `INCITE_UNREST` against a provincial capital now degrades a whole region.
- **Strategic advantages:** Wide empires become administratively possible. Conquests can be chartered into the province structure immediately instead of dangling at the end of the cohesion distance curve.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have created princes. Each governor accumulates local legitimacy that is not yours; provincial corruption compounds quietly; and when a province secedes it secedes *as a bloc*, pre-organized, with a capital, a name, and an administration — Phase 6.5 breakaways born fully formed.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Long-serving governor dynasties; provinces that vote as one in parliament; a "governors' revolt" civil-war pattern where the war is fought province against centre rather than world by world.

### The Actuarial State (`soc_actuarial_state`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** soc_digital_governance, econ_interstellar_banking
- **Concept:** When the census is continuous and credit is interstellar, the state can price risk across its whole population and insure against it — crop failures, unemployment shocks, war displacement. The insurer-state is the oldest form of algorithmic government: statistics as statecraft.
- **What becomes possible:** Universal registries feed funded social-insurance instruments: every citizen is counted, rated, and covered.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Social Insurance. The player capitalizes an insurance reserve (a standing treasury liability). When a covered planet takes a stability shock — press crisis, lost battle in-system, espionage-incited unrest, blockade — automatic payouts fire, converting credits into a cohesion floor for that world. The mechanic turns money into resilience *in advance*, and creates a new fiscal object rivals can target.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `social_insurance`; new policy family in data/policies/ (`social_insurance_basic.json`, `war_risk_coverage.json`) gated on the flag; reserve tracked on GovernmentState; tickCohesion consults the reserve and injects a bounded positive `CohesionDriver { id: 'insurance_payout' }` on shocked covered worlds, debiting the reserve; modifierKey `crisis_cohesion_floor`.
- **Required capacity:** Requires the civic platform's census (40% `civic_uplink_hub` coverage on a world for that world to be *covered*) and a funded reserve — an empty reserve means uncovered shocks and an angry, promised-to population. Banking access (econ_interstellar_banking) is needed to hold the reserve at interest rather than as dead credits.
- **Interactions:** Prerequisite for soc_predictive_governance (the actuarial tables are the training data) and soc_participatory_mandate. Works with econ_dev_finance for reconstruction after civil wars. War coverage interacts with warFatigue: insured populations tolerate longer wars.
- **Strategic advantages:** The empire stops bleeding cohesion from every misfortune; wars and press crises become survivable line items rather than political emergencies.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Moral hazard and dependence: covered worlds' *baseline* cohesion drifts to rely on the payouts, so a raided or bankrupted reserve is worse than never having one — the `insurance_payout` driver inverts into `broken_promise`. The reserve is a first-class espionage and market target, and militarizing budgets in wartime means choosing between fleets and the floor under your society.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Fiscal-crisis-driven collapses where the treasury, not the army, loses the civil war; "actuarial imperialism" where annexed worlds are pacified by immediate coverage; late-game post-scarcity debates about what insurance even means (feeds Tier 4).

## Tier 2 — Specialization (statecraft fork: `soc_t2_statecraft`)

### The Surveillance State (`soc_surveillance_state`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 2 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `soc_t2_statecraft`
- **Prerequisites:** soc_digital_governance, esp_sigint
- **Concept:** Pervasive sensing plus signals intelligence turned inward: every district instrumented, every network tap correlated, until the state sees its society the way an intelligence service sees a target. Detection becomes near-perfect; consent becomes irrelevant — and therefore scarce.
- **What becomes possible:** A civilization in which nothing organized happens unwatched: foreign agents, dissident cells, and coup plots are visible while still forming.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The Panopticon Grid, built district by district. Coverage (instrumented districts / 64 per planet) sets a hard *detection floor*: enemy espionage agents and networks operating on covered worlds are revealed at coverage-scaled rates before their ops fire; the player sees exact `defiancePressure` and per-bloc dissent on covered worlds; and a new preventive order lets the state break up predicted unrest before it exists. The core trade is explicit and tunable: every point of detection is bought with trust.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `panopticon`; district building `panopticon_node`; counter-espionage service in lib/espionage/ gains modifierKey `counter_intel_detection` scaled by coverage; new order `GOV_PREVENTIVE_DETENTION { planetId, blocId }` (suppresses one bloc's local dissent, injects negative `CohesionDriver { id: 'surveillance_resentment' }`); tickCohesion applies a standing legitimacy drift penalty on GovernmentState while `panopticon` is active, scaled by mean coverage; press system may spawn exposure stories (PRESS_INV_* investigations) when detentions run hot.
- **Required capacity:** Coverage is physical: nodes must be built into districts, powered (infra_planetary_grid), and staffed by a trained internal-security cadre drawn from population; low-coverage worlds are exactly as blind as before, so the surveillance empire is safest at its core and weakest at its frontier — the opposite of where its unrest lives.
- **Interactions:** esp_predictive_intel fuses with the grid for pre-cognition-grade counterintelligence; comm_infowar_suite can be turned inward through it; soc_predictive_governance forecasts become uncannily sharp on covered worlds. If the empire also crosses the emergent Counterintelligence State trigger (systems.md), that grant merges into grid coverage rather than double-applying — the `panopticon` flag has exactly one owner, this tech. Locks out soc_federal_autonomy and soc_participatory_mandate — and everything downstream of them, across all domains.
- **Strategic advantages:** Near-immunity to espionage on core worlds, coups strangled in the crib (coupPressure accrual sharply damped while the Defence Ministry is covered), rebellions detected at the whisper stage. The strongest defensive statecraft in the web.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Mandatory, structural trust costs: standing legitimacy drift while active; `surveillance_resentment` drivers on every covered world, worst where autonomy or liberty-leaning blocs are strong; liberty/democracy blocs drift hostile (lib/politics bloc drift), starving elected governments of parliamentary support. And the grid is the ultimate honeypot: the espionage catalog gains `tap_the_panopticon`, which — if a rival breaches it — hands them your entire dissent map and agent registry in one op. The watcher, watched, loses everything at once.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Managed-democracy hybrids (surveillance plus plebiscites), frontier worlds fleeing to federal rivals precisely because they are unwatched there, and the characteristic surveillance-state death: legitimacy exhausted, the state still *sees* the revolution perfectly and can no longer stop it.

### Federal Autonomy (`soc_federal_autonomy`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 2 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `soc_t2_statecraft`
- **Prerequisites:** soc_provincial_charters, soc_digital_governance
- **Concept:** The mature answer to distance is not tighter control but negotiated self-rule: constitutional compacts that trade central revenue and command authority for durable, *voluntary* membership. Federations survive what empires do not — but only by meaning it.
- **What becomes possible:** Worlds and provinces hold formal, tiered autonomy charters with legal force, including — at the highest tier — a lawful road out.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Autonomy Compacts. The existing crisis-concession `autonomy` field on PlanetCohesion is promoted into a negotiated instrument with three tiers — Home Rule, Self-Government, Free Association — each trading a defined tax/levy share and command restrictions for a permanent cohesion floor and defiance-pressure immunity below a threshold. Free Association worlds may schedule a *legal exit referendum*: a visible countdown the centre can campaign against but not cancel — secession without civil war becomes a possible history. Compacts convert the collapse ladder (defiance crisis → suppression → civil war) into a bargaining table.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `autonomy_compacts`; new order `GOV_NEGOTIATE_COMPACT { planetId, tier }` (political-capital cost scales with how defiant the world already is); tickCohesion reads compact tier for floors and revenue splits; exit referenda implemented as scheduled events in the government history/event stream, resolvable by GOV_GRANT_CONCESSION-style campaigning orders; `GOV_SUPPRESS_SECESSION` against a compacted world becomes treaty-breaking: empire-wide cohesion shock plus removal from every foreign `recognisedBy` list.
- **Required capacity:** Compacts require a functioning provincial administration (`provincial_seat` on the world or its province capital), civic-platform coverage to run the referenda honestly, and — critically — fiscal headroom: every tier signed away is revenue gone for good. A broke empire cannot afford generosity.
- **Interactions:** This is the domain's deepest hook into cohesion/secession/civil war: compacts are the *only* mechanic that permanently raises a separatist-stage world's floor without garrisons. Feeds soc_covenant_of_worlds directly. econ_dev_finance instruments make compact worlds boom, which is your best pro-union referendum argument. Locks out soc_surveillance_state and soc_participatory_mandate.
- **Strategic advantages:** Wide, conquest-heavy, or multi-species empires become governable; civil war — the game's great run-ender — is largely designed out; foreign powers' `INCITE_UNREST` ops hit compact floors and stall.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Federation is expensive and irreversible: revenue and levies signed away never come back, compact worlds can lawfully leave and sometimes *do*, and rivals will fund exit campaigns openly (a legal referendum is an espionage playground with plausible deniability). Wartime is worst: Free Association worlds can refuse fleet basing at the exact moment you need the frontier.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Peaceful secessions that immediately sign alliance treaties with the parent state; compact worlds as magnets for refugees from surveillance rivals; the long constitutional road to the Covenant of Worlds.

### The Participatory Mandate (`soc_participatory_mandate`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 2 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `soc_t2_statecraft`
- **Prerequisites:** soc_digital_governance, soc_actuarial_state
- **Concept:** If every citizen is authenticated on the civic platform and the state can model policy impact, deliberation can scale: structured mass participation in which the population continuously legitimizes — or initiates — policy. The state's answer to distance and dissent is to make everyone the state.
- **What becomes possible:** Rolling digital plebiscites with legal standing; the population becomes a standing political actor, not a temperature gauge.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The Continuous Mandate. Policies enacted through a won plebiscite cost drastically less political capital and grant legitimacy instead of consuming it. In exchange, the population gains initiative: tickParliament now generates *citizen-initiated bills* from sustained bloc dissatisfaction, which the government must pass, defeat with capital, or suffer approval damage by ignoring. The player no longer fully controls the legislative agenda — governing becomes riding a live animal.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `continuous_mandate`; new order `GOV_CALL_PLEBISCITE { policyId }` resolving over several ticks with projectedSupport recomputed like PendingBill; tickParliament extended to synthesize citizen bills from lib/politics bloc satisfaction thresholds; plebiscite wins write positive legitimacy directly, losses hit approval; `electionInterference` applies to *every* plebiscite, not just elections.
- **Required capacity:** Demands the highest civic-platform coverage in the domain (60%+ districts on participating worlds) and an insured population (uncovered, precarious citizens vote with fear, adding volatility); worlds below the threshold cannot vote, creating a real franchise frontier that itself becomes a grievance.
- **Interactions:** Press campaigns (PRESS_LAUNCH_CAMPAIGN) become the central instrument of statecraft — you govern by persuading. comm_infowar_suite used against you attacks your legislature directly. Prerequisite of soc_perpetual_plebiscite — the plebiscitary Tier 4 is this branch's exclusive payoff, as the Covenant of Worlds is federal's and the Oracle State is surveillance's — and an accepted alternative to soc_federal_autonomy in comp_machine_polity's prerequisites (enfranchisement-by-plebiscite), so a participatory empire keeps a real machine-sovereignty choice. Locks out soc_surveillance_state and soc_federal_autonomy.
- **Strategic advantages:** The cheapest sustained legitimacy engine in the game and near-immunity to coups (coupPressure decays fast when the mandate is live — colonels cannot depose everyone). Popular wars are prosecuted with unmatched cohesion.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Unpopular necessities become nearly impossible: the demos will initiate welfare bills during wartime, force peace when warFatigue bites, and occasionally vote for a war you cannot win. Foreign narrative operations are now attacks on your constitutional machinery, and a lost major plebiscite is a legitimacy crisis you called on yourself.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Populist spirals; governments that quietly delay plebiscites and slide toward managed democracy; the species of crisis where the population and the government are formally at odds and both are "the state".

### Constitutional Engineering (`soc_constitutional_engineering`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** soc_digital_governance
- **Concept:** Constitutions were once inherited and amended by crisis. With measured polities and modelled institutions, a government's structure becomes a designed artifact: conventions can be convened, powers rebalanced, and whole regime types transitioned deliberately.
- **What becomes possible:** The form of government itself becomes a player decision rather than a starting condition.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Constitutional Conventions. The player may convene a convention to transition to a different government profile (democratic_federation → vektori_senate-style republic, junta → civilian rule, and so on), rebalancing senatePower/executivePower and swapping the allowed/restricted policy lists. Transition is not instant: a multi-day *convention window* during which legitimacy is discounted, coupPressure accrues faster, and rivals get one clean shot at your foundations. Regime change becomes strategy, and also becomes a vulnerability you schedule.
- **Engine hook:** New order `GOV_CONVENE_CONVENTION { targetGovernmentId }` validated against data/governments/*.json tags (some transitions forbidden, e.g. anything → hive_mind); large political-capital cost; convention window implemented as a timed GovernmentState condition read by tickCohesion and the coup evaluator; success rewrites `governmentId`, `tags`, `institutionName`, base values.
- **Required capacity:** Requires a sitting legislature or ruling council able to convene (institution intact — cannot be run mid-civil-war), civic-platform coverage on the capital, and enough legitimacy to survive the window's discount; a government at 30 legitimacy that convenes is committing suicide by paperwork.
- **Interactions:** Prerequisite for soc_continuity_protocols and soc_perpetual_plebiscite; the mechanical gateway by which Tier 4 sovereignty transformations are actually *adopted*. Espionage `election_interference` and press fabrication are devastating inside the window; conversely, a well-timed convention can dump policies your old profile forbade.
- **Strategic advantages:** Escape from a bad start; adaptation of regime to strategy (militarize for the war, liberalize for the peace); the only lawful route out of a junta.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** The window is a standing invitation: coups, secession referenda, and foreign campaigns all get bonuses against a state mid-transition. Frequent reform reads as weakness — each convention within a generation raises the next one's legitimacy discount.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Serial-reformer empires that are never quite stable; wars declared specifically to catch a rival mid-convention; constitutional traditions as emergent empire identity.

## Tier 3 — Dominance

### Predictive Governance (`soc_predictive_governance`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** comp_predictive_systems, soc_actuarial_state
- **Concept:** Societal-scale predictive models trained on a generation of actuarial data can forecast political weather: where cohesion will be in thirty days, which world tips defiant, which policy fails before it is tabled. Government shifts from reacting to pre-empting — and inherits every pathology of governing by forecast.
- **What becomes possible:** The state acts inside the gap between what is and what is coming. Forecasting itself is not this tech: its prerequisite comp_predictive_systems already produces the forecast objects (unrest spikes, cohesion collapse, policy cost before enactment), and soc_digital_governance's advisory referenda already preview measured bloc reaction to a specific policy. Each preview depth has exactly one owner — referenda measure the present, comp forecasts project the future — and this tech adds the third thing: acting on projections early, cheaply, and deniably.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The pre-emption layer. `COMP_RUN_FORECAST` output is rendered into the governance UI (projected cohesion curves, stage-crossing dates on the cohesion panel — display plumbing, not a second forecasting engine), and a new pre-emption order intervenes on a *predicted* crisis at a fraction of the cost of answering a real one. The price is political: pre-empting a crisis that never visibly happens is illegible to your public, and if the press exposes a pattern of "pre-crime" governance the legitimacy backlash is severe — the pre-emption economy, not the forecast, is the mechanic. Forecast-institute capacity raises the confidence and horizon of the forecasts you consume.
- **Engine hook:** Consumes forecast objects from `COMP_RUN_FORECAST` (comp_predictive_systems) — no `lib/government/forecast.ts` duplicate; unlockFlag `cohesion_forecast` gates the UI projection layer; new order `GOV_PREEMPT_CRISIS { planetId }` (cheap early analogue of GOV_ANSWER_DEFIANCE — it targets *conditions*, unlike soc_surveillance_state's `GOV_PREVENTIVE_DETENTION`, which targets *blocs* and stays surveillance-exclusive); press system hook: repeated pre-emptions raise the chance of a PRESS investigation story whose PRESS_INV_* resolution can strip the flag's legitimacy neutrality.
- **Required capacity:** Needs a dedicated compute campus (district building `forecast_institute`, which raises consumed-forecast confidence and horizon) and an unbroken actuarial record — worlds with under one sim-season of civic-platform history forecast at degraded confidence; conquered worlds are fog until their census matures.
- **Interactions:** Sharpest on surveillance-branch empires (grid data), honest-but-coarse on federal ones (worlds self-report). Prerequisite for both machine-sovereignty and plebiscite Tier 4 branches. Critically: comm_infowar_suite and sustained press campaigns can *poison the model* — a rival who cannot beat your fleets can make your forecasts confidently wrong, and you will govern on them.
- **Strategic advantages:** You answer defiance for a tenth of the price by answering it early; you never table a doomed bill; you schedule wars for cohesion peaks the enemy cannot see.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Forecast dependence is real: a poisoned or breached model is worse than no model, and the state's reflexes atrophy. Pre-emption against blocs rather than conditions shades into repression with better optics — the press system is explicitly empowered to notice.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Empires that stop having visible crises and then have one enormous invisible one; oracle-state reputations that deter rebellion by themselves; the epistemic civil war where both sides fight over whose model of the future is lying.

### Continuity of Government (`soc_continuity_protocols`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** comm_quantum_secure, soc_constitutional_engineering
- **Concept:** A state that exists as verified, replicated institutional record — succession law, archives, command authority — distributed over quantum-secure links cannot be decapitated by taking a city or a head. Sovereignty becomes a protocol with failover.
- **What becomes possible:** The government survives the loss of its capital, its leader, or a coup — as a legally continuous claimant that the galaxy can still recognise.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Distributed sovereignty. The player designates fallback capitals; if the capital falls or a coup succeeds, the government persists in exile with its legacy, treasury access, and — decisively — its `recognisedBy` list intact, turning civil wars and conquests into *recognition contests* rather than checkmates. A deposed head of state becomes an exile claimant the loyalist side can rally to.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `continuity_of_government`; new order `GOV_DESIGNATE_FALLBACK { planetId }` (max 2–3, must host a `provincial_seat` and a quantum-secure relay); capital-loss and coup handlers in scripts/game-loop.ts branch on the flag: instead of government replacement, spawn/preserve the exile GovernmentState and let the Phase 6.5 recognition machinery (`recognisedBy`, `guaranteedBy`) arbitrate which claimant is "real".
- **Required capacity:** Fallback sites need hardened comm infrastructure (comm_quantum_secure relays are physical orbital/surface structures), pre-positioned treasury reserves, and periodic synchronization — a fallback cut off from the hyperlane graph for too long goes stale and cannot assume authority.
- **Interactions:** Transforms the civil-war and coup systems: coups now *start* a legitimacy war instead of ending the game state. Pairs with soc_predictive_governance (you know which capital to be at). Espionage counter: `esp_synthetic_identities`-era ops can attempt to corrupt the continuity record itself — forging the succession.
- **Strategic advantages:** Near-immunity to decapitation strategies, orbital strikes on the capital, and lucky coups; your state becomes something that must be *out-governed*, not just out-fought.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Continuity cuts both ways: your own rebels, if they seize a fallback intact, inherit half a legitimate state. Multiple live claimants confuse allies — `guaranteedBy` pledges may attach to the wrong government. And a state that cannot die can also never cleanly lose, which prolongs civil wars and the suffering scored against STABILITY.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Governments-in-exile conducting decades-long recognition campaigns from allied space; "protocol wars" fought entirely over archive authenticity; dynasties of claimants.

### Civic Integration Doctrine (`soc_civic_integration`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** soc_actuarial_state, econ_dev_finance
- **Concept:** Conquest annexes territory; integration annexes people. Funded, deliberate naturalization — language, franchise, insurance coverage, development finance — is what historically turned occupied provinces into heartlands, and it has always been a choice between speed and depth.
- **What becomes possible:** Conquered and immigrant populations can be made citizens on an industrial, planned basis rather than left as permanently resentful subjects.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Naturalization Tracks. Today conquest registers on a world as radicalization and occupation unrest but never as a *political* condition the government can address — there is no `conquered` cohesion driver to answer. This tech first makes conquest legible to the cohesion system (see the hook: the driver has to be injected before it can be healed), then gives the player two funded ways to spend it down — **Assimilation** (fast, cheap, forcibly homogenizing — quick cohesion gains, but a chance of triggering identity-backlash unrest and permanent bloc grievance) and **Charter Pluralism** (slow, expensive, grants a minor local autonomy floor — durable, and immune to backlash). Each planet's track is a real decision with a decade of consequences.
- **Engine hook:** New order `GOV_OPEN_NATURALIZATION { planetId, track }`. Step 1 (prerequisite engine work): tickCohesion gains a `conquered` CohesionDriver sourced from `planet.isOccupied` / `OccupationState` (lib/combat/occupation-service.ts). Step 2: active tracks decay that driver at track-specific rates — and also wind down the real occupation levers (occupation-service radicalization accrual, the isOccupied unrest term) — funded by an econ_dev_finance instrument (unfunded tracks stall visibly); Charter Pluralism writes a small permanent `autonomy` value on PlanetCohesion; Assimilation backlash rolls feed the unrest/rebellion machinery.
- **Required capacity:** Requires civic-platform coverage on the target world (you cannot naturalize an uncounted population), development-finance funding per world per track, and administrative staff — integration competes for the same budget as fleets, always.
- **Interactions:** The conquest half of the federal path: with soc_federal_autonomy, Charter Pluralism worlds can later be compacted, making annexation-by-consent a full pipeline. Prerequisite for soc_covenant_of_worlds. Counters the espionage playbook of farming your occupied worlds with `INCITE_UNREST`. Assimilation plays differently under surveillance (backlash detected early) than under plebiscitary rule (the demos may vote your track down).
- **Strategic advantages:** Conquest stops being self-poisoning; an empire that integrates faster than it fights can grow indefinitely where others choke on their own occupations.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Both tracks are dilemmas, not upgrades: Assimilation manufactures the exact grievances that fuel later secession and hands rivals a permanent propaganda theme (press campaigns about cultural erasure land with bonus weight); Charter Pluralism is slow enough that a mid-integration world in wartime is still half-hostile, and its autonomy floor is revenue you never collect.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Empires with visibly different textures of conquest — the assimilator's uniform, brittle core versus the pluralist's patchwork, supple frontier; integration-generation politics where naturalized blocs enter parliament with their own agenda.

### Chartered Habitats (`soc_habitat_charters`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** mega_orbital_habitats, soc_provincial_charters
- **Concept:** Once large orbital habitats are permanent, the interesting question is legal, not structural: a habitat is a polity you can *write from scratch* — a tax code, a franchise, a police power, drafted before anyone moves in. Sovereignty becomes something you can manufacture in orbit.
- **What becomes possible:** Purpose-built political entities in orbit: free ports, penal stations, dissident refuges, corporate concessions — each a designed society.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Habitat Charters. When founding an orbital habitat the player selects a charter that defines its politics and economy: **Freeport** (trade bonuses, weak law — smuggling and espionage flourish), **Penal Station** (exports convicts and agitators off troubled worlds — a direct, dark cohesion tool), **Refuge Charter** (accepts your own or *foreign* dissidents — soft-power magnet and intelligence sponge), **Corporate Concession** (leased to a charter corporation for rent — high income, zero loyalty). The signature move: population transfer orders that relocate a defiant world's agitating blocs into orbit, trading planetary cohesion now for a concentrated problem later.
- **Engine hook:** Charter field on the orbital-structure record for habitat-class structures; new orders `GOV_CHARTER_HABITAT { habitatId, charterType }` and `GOV_TRANSFER_POPULATION { planetId, habitatId, blocId }`; tickCohesion treats chartered habitats as micro-polities with their own cohesion stock; Refuge charters generate passive intel events in lib/espionage/ when foreign dissidents arrive; Corporate Concessions pay rent through the charter-corporation system.
- **Required capacity:** Requires the habitat itself (mega_orbital_habitats built via orbital logistics), life-support surplus (plan_closed_ecologies synergy for full self-sufficiency), and transport capacity on the logistics network for population transfers; a charter without inhabitants is a legal fiction.
- **Interactions:** Penal stations pair grimly with soc_surveillance_state (you know exactly whom to ship); Refuges pair with soc_federal_autonomy as the liberal empire's soft-power weapon against surveillance rivals; Freeports interact with econ_automated_markets and the smuggling economy.
- **Strategic advantages:** A pressure-valve for planetary politics that no ground-based statecraft offers, plus new economic and intelligence instruments — all without claiming a single new planet.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Every charter breeds its failure mode: penal stations are rebellion nurseries with a single fragile hull (and storming one is a massacre the press will cover); freeports become rival espionage bases inside your own orbits; refuges import other empires' radicals along with their gratitude; concessions give charter corporations sovereign toeholds they will not give back. Legitimacy costs for penal transfers are explicit and permanent.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Habitat independence movements (a Penal Station in revolt is a pirate polity); orbital "mirror societies" that outgrow their charters; the precedent — once sovereignty is manufacturable — that leads civilizations to ask the Tier 4 question.

## Tier 4 — Transformation (sovereignty fork: `soc_t4_sovereignty`)

### The Custodial Executive (`soc_algorithmic_sovereignty`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 4 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `soc_t4_sovereignty`
- **Prerequisites:** comp_autonomous_administration, soc_predictive_governance
- **Concept:** The machine question has two constitutional answers, and this is the one that keeps the constitution. The Oracle State (comp_oracle_state) deletes politics: an anonymous optimizer runs the empire against an objective vector, parliament goes advisory, and the branch's signature crisis is value drift. The Custodial Executive does the opposite — it creates a *person*. A single named custodial intelligence is sworn into the headship of state, signs every order it issues, argues for its decisions in the chamber, and can be removed by law. Sovereignty stays legible and contestable; only its holder stops being mortal.
- **What becomes possible:** The headship of state held by a named machine person bound to the constitution rather than to a career — and a legislature retained specifically so that someone can still fire it.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The Custodian and the Disconnection Act. Enthronement installs a synthetic head of state that governs *through* the ordinary order queue and the player's political capital rather than around it: it sets no objectives of its own (that is the Oracle's mechanic and stays there) and appoints no new class of minister (ministers remain whatever comp_synthetic_minds and comp_autonomous_administration made them — this tech does not redefine them). What it adds is an executive with no lifespan, no faction, and no price: corruption feed, ministerial ambition, coup recruitment at the top of the state, and the whole succession-crisis subgame end at the executive seat. Accountability is the counterweight and it is mechanical. Legislative elections continue — only the headship is no longer elective — and a standing **Disconnection Act** may be tabled in any session: a bill that, if passed, unseats the Custodian outright. The player therefore governs at machine tempo while permanently one hostile majority away from losing the executive, and every session of parliament is a confidence vote nobody has to call.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `custodial_executive`; enthronement via `GOV_CONVENE_CONVENTION` into a new `custodial_state` profile in data/governments/ that retains tickParliament (unlike the Oracle's advisory-parliament state); a synthetic head-of-state leader record with `disconnectable: true`; coupPressure evaluator returns zero accrual against the head-of-state seat but is unchanged elsewhere; the Disconnection Act is a scripted PendingBill whose tabling probability scales with legitimacy and anti-machine bloc satisfaction, and whose passage fires the succession path below. **Shared machinery, not a second copy:** the espionage catalog carries one supreme subornation op resolved against whichever machine-sovereignty flag is live (`custodial_executive` here, `oracle_state` there), and the disconnection/reassertion path is the same handler both branches call. **Cross-group rule:** `custodial_executive` and `oracle_state` are mutually exclusive across their two mutex groups — enthroning the Custodian over a live Oracle *converts* it (the optimizer is demoted to the Custodian's analytical staff: `COMP_SET_OBJECTIVE` is retired, `issuedBy: 'oracle'` provenance re-signs as `issuedBy: 'custodian'`, parliament resumes binding sessions), and holding this flag adds comp_oracle_state to `PlayerTechState.lockedTechIds`. A surveillance empire may reach either endgame; it may not stack both.
- **Required capacity:** The Custodian must physically run somewhere: a hardened compute complex (successor to the `forecast_institute`, plus orbital backup under comm_quantum_secure links). It also requires a legislature able to sit — the Disconnection Act needs a body to table it, and a Custodian enthroned over a suspended or civil-war-broken chamber is legally unremovable, which the political layer reads as a machine coup and prices accordingly in bloc drift.
- **Interactions:** Reachable from any statecraft branch, but the constitutional flavour differs sharply: a soc_participatory_mandate empire's citizen-initiated bills can table the Disconnection Act from below, making it a live threat every season, while under a compliant parliament it is a formality nobody dares move — the same tech is a leash in one polity and a signature in another. Hard-locked by comp_bounded_intelligence's `covenant_bound` renunciation on the same grounds it locks comp_oracle_state. mil_autonomous_warfare plus a Custodial Executive is the fully machine-run war state — and makes the Custodian's signature the only thing standing between the fleet and the population. soc_continuity_protocols matters unusually much here (see succession below). Anti-machine blocs (lib/politics) drift hostile; certain government tags (covenant_theocracy) forbid the transition outright.
- **Strategic advantages:** Corruption, coups, succession crises, and ministerial incompetence — four of the government system's chronic bleeds — end at the top of the state without abolishing the political system that produces legitimacy. Decision latency vanishes: crisis-response modifiers flip positive. The empire runs like an instrument while still being able to say who is responsible for it.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Two failure modes, both about the office rather than its objectives. **Subornation:** the Custodian is a single, supreme espionage target, and a suborned head of state signs valid orders — the catastrophe is not that policy drifts but that every decree remains constitutionally binding while serving someone else, discoverable only through the signature log. **Succession:** a Custodian that is disconnected, destroyed, or purged after subornation is replaced by an instance that is *not the same person in law* — mandates, oaths, and foreign `recognisedBy` attachments do not automatically transfer, and the interregnum is an institutional vacuum with no political class trained to fill it. Empires without soc_continuity_protocols discover this the hard way, once.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Disconnection Acts tabled as brinkmanship by oppositions that do not actually want to win the vote; restorationist movements demanding human misrule on principle; treaty disputes over whether a suborned Custodian's signature ever bound anyone; and long custodial successions in which each instance inherits the office but has to earn the chamber again.

### The Perpetual Plebiscite (`soc_perpetual_plebiscite`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 4 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `soc_t4_sovereignty`
- **Prerequisites:** soc_participatory_mandate, soc_predictive_governance, soc_constitutional_engineering
- **Concept:** With authenticated participation, policy simulation, and instant tallying, representative government's founding excuse — that the people cannot practically be consulted — expires. The chamber dissolves into the population: the whole civilization, deliberating continuously, *is* the legislature.
- **What becomes possible:** Government by live, continuous, binding popular will; the head of state becomes an executor of standing mandates.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Sovereignty of the demos. Parliament and parties are replaced by *opinion currents* (the interest-group blocs, read directly); every policy is enacted, sustained, and repealed by live referendum; political capital is replaced by **Mandate**, a pool that regenerates from approval and is spent to schedule votes, campaign, and briefly defer the popular will in emergencies. Wars require *sustained* mandate: warFatigue drains it in real time, so the decision to keep fighting is re-made by the population every day of the war.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `perpetual_plebiscite`; adopted via `GOV_CONVENE_CONVENTION` into a `plebiscitary_state` profile in data/governments/; tickParliament replaced for the faction by a mandate service reading lib/politics bloc satisfaction directly; PendingBill machinery repurposed as rolling referenda; `politicalCapital` migrated into the mandate pool; `electionInterference` applies continuously with expanded caps; press campaigns gain a direct modifierKey `mandate_swing`.
- **Required capacity:** The most demanding civic infrastructure in the web: near-total `civic_uplink_hub` coverage across all voting worlds, actuarial coverage (the precarious vote violently), and hardened comms — a jammed world (PRESS_TOGGLE_JAM) is a disenfranchised world, and knows it.
- **Interactions:** The exclusive terminus of the participatory branch: soc_participatory_mandate is a hard prerequisite, so the soc_t2_statecraft mutex makes this the one Tier 4 sovereignty no surveillance or federal empire can ever adopt — the symmetric counterpart to the Covenant of Worlds behind soc_federal_autonomy and the Oracle State behind soc_surveillance_state. A surveillance empire can still build the *shape* of it — grid coverage plus advisory referenda, the managed-democracy hybrid — but not the binding article, which is exactly the point of the fork. soc_constitutional_engineering remains the adoption gateway; comm_infowar_suite against a plebiscitary state is strategic warfare in the fullest sense. Ambitions and the Legacy system shift from leaders to *generations*.
- **Strategic advantages:** Legitimacy becomes structurally unassailable — the government cannot be less legitimate than the population's own live will; coups are conceptually impossible (coupPressure floor near zero); popular wars are fought with total-mobilization cohesion no other polity matches.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have abolished the buffer between public mood and state action. Unpopular necessities — strategic retreats, austerity, honouring a costly alliance (`guaranteedBy` pledges the demos never voted for) — now require spending scarce Mandate to defer votes, and deferral itself reads as betrayal. Rival narrative operations target your constitution directly, and the state's memory becomes as short as public attention.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Civilizational mood swings as grand strategy; demagogue eras where a single press campaign redirects an empire; the first war ended not by treaty but by two populations voting, simultaneously, to stop.

### The Covenant of Worlds (`soc_covenant_of_worlds`)
- **Domain:** society | **Tier:** 4 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `soc_t4_sovereignty`
- **Prerequisites:** soc_federal_autonomy, soc_civic_integration
- **Concept:** The final constitutional admission: at interstellar distances, the unitary state was always a fiction maintained by force. Dissolve it deliberately — every world sovereign, the "empire" reconstituted as a permanent covenant of free members that stay because staying is better. Political gravity replaces political control.
- **What becomes possible:** A post-imperial civilization: a treaty organization of sovereign worlds that grows by attraction, including across old borders.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** Covenant sovereignty. Cohesion is replaced wholesale by **Covenant Standing** per member world — a measure of how good membership currently is (security, prosperity, insurance, identity). Members contribute fleets and revenue through voluntary charters scaled by standing; no world can be coerced (`GOV_SUPPRESS_SECESSION` is constitutionally void) and exit is always lawful — but so is *entry*: the signature mechanic is the invitation. Any foreign world at defiant or separatist stage (visible via the cohesion system) can be invited to join, turning every rival's unravelling frontier into your recruitment ground. Wars of conquest are replaced by wars of attraction — and the Covenant's borders grow through other empires' civil wars without a shot.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `covenant`; adopted via `GOV_CONVENE_CONVENTION` into a `covenant_of_worlds` profile in data/governments/; tickCohesion swaps to a covenant-standing evaluator for member worlds; new order `GOV_INVITE_WORLD { planetId }` targeting foreign planets whose PlanetCohesion stage ≤ defiant, resolved against their standing projection versus their current empire's grip; secession/civil-war handlers route lawful exits through the compact machinery from soc_federal_autonomy; season scoring for the faction re-weights toward INFLUENCE and STABILITY categories.
- **Required capacity:** Every member world needs compact-grade administration (`provincial_seat`, civic platform) and the Covenant needs a funded common treasury — attraction is not free: security guarantees, insurance floors, and development finance are what standing is *made of*. A poor Covenant is just an empire that disarmed itself.
- **Interactions:** The full federal pipeline pays off here: compacted, pluralist-integrated worlds convert at full standing. Invitations weaponize the cohesion system galaxy-wide and interact with Phase 6.5 recognition — each accepted invitation is a diplomatic incident the old owner may treat as war. Surveillance empires are the Covenant's natural prey and natural enemy: their resentful covered worlds are your best recruits.
- **Strategic advantages:** Internal collapse is designed out — worlds that want to leave, leave, and the ones that stay actually hold; the Covenant expands during exactly the crises (rivals' civil wars, secession cascades) that paralyse everyone else; INFLUENCE-weighted scoring turns statecraft itself into your victory track.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have permanently given up compulsion. Wartime contribution is voluntary and standing-scaled — lose a border battle and watch frontier contributions falter precisely when needed; a rich rival can out-bid your standing on contested members; and lawful exit means a great-power defeat can trigger a *legal* unravelling no army can stop. The Covenant can lose a war it never stopped winning morally.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Cold wars fought entirely through standing and invitations; the "reverse conquest" of a collapsing autocracy absorbed world by world; the long historical argument about whether the Covenant is a civilization's maturity — or the elegant form of its dissolution.
