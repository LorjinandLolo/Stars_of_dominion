# Physics, Energy & Spaceflight

Playing into this domain is playing with the throttle of your civilization. Every other domain spends energy and movement; this one decides how much of each exists and what shape it takes. The early game is about breaking two bottlenecks — the energy budget of planets and the cost of getting mass off them — and every tech here converts one of those constraints into a piece of infrastructure that can be built, allocated, fought over, sabotaged, and lost. Nothing in this region is a passive bonus: fusion gives you a fuel cycle to defend, lift capacity gives you a channel to ration, drives give you burns loud enough to announce your invasions. The player who invests here is constantly making allocation decisions under scarcity, and then — precisely when the scarcity ends — discovers that abundance has a geography, and geography can be attacked.

The identity this domain enables is the civilization defined by *where its power comes from and where its ships can go*. The Tier 2 power-doctrine fork decides whether you are a centralized industrial titan, a decentralized society no siege can turn off, or an orbital state whose planets are wired to the sky. By Tier 3 you are producing antimatter over your own capital and parking deterrence batteries in orbit — power that doubles as hostage. By Tier 4 the questions stop being about output at all: whether to mine your own star while your people watch it dim, whether to redraw the hyperlane map that every other civilization's strategy assumes is permanent, whether to anchor your future to a black hole eight systems from home. A physics-and-spaceflight civilization ends the game not merely stronger but *located differently* — its energy, its routes, and its vulnerabilities all sitting in places no other civilization's do.

## Tier 1 — Expansion

### Commercial Fusion Power (`phys_fusion_power`)
- **Domain:** Physics & Energy | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** none (root anchor)
- **Concept:** Magnetic-confinement fusion of deuterium and helium-3 reaches sustained net-positive output at utility scale. The fuel is seawater, comet ice, and gas-giant atmospheres — abundant, but not free and not everywhere.
- **What becomes possible:** Planets stop budgeting scarcity-era energy. Industry, shields, and computing electrify; energy becomes a planetary budget with a surplus or a deficit instead of a per-building afterthought.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **fusion fuel cycle**: a new extractable resource, `fusion_fuel`, harvested from ocean/ice worlds and gas-giant skimming, consumed by Fusion Plant districts. Planetary energy becomes an explicit budget with surplus/deficit states — deficit planets suffer rolling **blackout events** that throttle the four logistics channels in `distribution-service`. The player now runs fuel logistics as a strategic supply line, not a background number.
- **Engine hook:** `UNLOCK_BUILDING: fusion_plant` (data/buildings.ts, district tile building); new resource `fusion_fuel` stored under the `volatile` storage class (`lib/logistics/storage-service` targets); `unlockFlag: 'fusion_fuel_cycle'` read by the economy tick in scripts/game-loop.ts; `modifierKey: 'energy_output'`. Blackout events emitted by the tick processor when budget < demand.
- **Required capacity:** Knowledge alone changes nothing. Deployment needs district tiles for plants, access to a fuel source (ocean/ice planet tags or a gas giant in-system), haulage in the `manufacturing` logistics channel to move fuel, and trained plant crews (manpower upkeep). Landlocked desert colonies must import fuel over trade segments — which can be blockaded.
- **Interactions:** `infra_planetary_grid` turns per-district budgets into one planetary pool; `binary_star` system tag boosts sibling solar income; fuel freighters ride `space_cycler_network` lines; fusion surplus is the input every later energy tech (storage, beaming, antimatter) draws on.
- **Strategic advantages:** The single broadest enabler in the domain — nearly everything at Tier 2+ assumes fusion-era surplus. Fuel-rich systems become genuinely valuable territory.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Fuel dependence is a new attack surface: blockade a planet's fuel imports and its grid starves within ticks; fusion plants are prime `ESP_SABOTAGE_FACILITY` targets. The legacy energy workforce is made redundant — an unemployment shock the press system will happily narrate, and a grievance for interest blocs. Tritium-handling incidents generate local unrest stories even without sabotage.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Fuel-lane raiding as a pirate economy; "fuel diplomacy" between fuel-rich and fuel-poor factions; the first energy-driven war over a gas giant nobody previously wanted.

### Grid-Scale Energy Banking (`phys_grid_storage`)
- **Domain:** Physics & Energy | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** phys_fusion_power, infra_planetary_grid
- **Concept:** Molten-salt thermal banks, superconducting magnetic loops, and flow-battery farms let a grid hold hours of planetary output. Storage decouples when energy is made from when it is spent.
- **What becomes possible:** A planet can save its surplus and spend it all at once — on defense, on industry, or on surviving the loss of its generators.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **energy reserve**: each planet gains a charge bar filled by grid surplus. The player chooses, per crisis, what to discharge it into — one of four surge channels: **shield surge** (temporary orbital_shield_strength), **beam surge** (directed-energy weapons fire above sustained grid rating), **industrial overdrive** (temporary manufacturing-channel boost), or **blackout bridging** (ride out a fuel cutoff). One bank, one discharge, competing claimants — a genuine decision every siege.
- **Engine hook:** New field `energyReserve` on `PlanetProduction` (lib/economy/economy-types); order type `GRID_DISCHARGE { planetId, channel }` handled in the scripts/game-loop.ts order switch alongside the ESP_* cases; building `storage_bank`; `modifierKey: 'energy_reserve_cap'`.
- **Required capacity:** Storage banks occupy district tiles and want cool/stable terrain; charging requires actual surplus (a deficit planet banks nothing). The surge channels require the target system to exist — shield surge does nothing without a shield_projector_array in orbit.
- **Interactions:** The natural partner of `phys_directed_energy` (beam surge) and the shield_projector_array orbital structure; `phys_microreactor_swarm` planets barely need it, `phys_gigascale_power` planets need it desperately; siege gameplay reads the reserve as a countdown clock.
- **Strategic advantages:** Converts economic surplus into military endurance. A charged bank is the difference between a siege lasting days and lasting hours.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** A charged bank is a bomb: bombardment or sabotage of a full storage_bank releases its energy into the surrounding districts (splash damage to adjacent Voronoi tiles). Banks occupy tiles that could have been production. Hoarding energy instead of selling it costs trade-season score — misers are measurably poorer.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** "Discharge doctrine" arguments in parliament during sieges; espionage ops timed for the moment after a defender spends its bank; deliberate self-discharge before surrendering a planet.

### Standardized Orbital Logistics (`space_orbital_logistics`)
- **Domain:** Spaceflight & Propulsion | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** mat_advanced_composites
- **Concept:** Containerized cargo standards, reusable heavy lift, and orbital transfer tugs — the shipping-container revolution applied to the surface-to-orbit interface. Cost-to-orbit collapses when nothing is bespoke.
- **What becomes possible:** Orbit stops being an expedition and becomes a freight terminal. Everything a civilization builds above its atmosphere becomes an extension of its surface economy.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **lift channel**: planetary storage and orbital warehouses merge into one network limited by a planet's lift capacity, and that capacity must be *allocated* — between orbital construction supply, surface↔orbit trade, and military embarkation (army loading onto transport fleets). A planet with big ambitions and one mass-lift complex is a planet making painful choices. Also unlocks rapid army embarkation and mass civilian evacuation as explicit operations.
- **Engine hook:** New `lift_capacity` modifierKey consumed by lib/logistics/distribution-service.ts as a fifth channel beside manufacturing/construction/military/civilian; building `mass_lift_complex`; order `LIFT_PRIORITY { planetId, channel }`; orbital structure build costs read delivered lift, not raw stockpile.
- **Required capacity:** A spaceport district (tile building), fuel and credits per lift, and calm enough conditions — planets with violent climates lift at a penalty until `plan_climate_control`. Low-gravity worlds lift cheaply and become natural export hubs.
- **Interactions:** Prerequisite texture for the whole orbital-structures catalog; `infra_orbital_elevator` later multiplies lift capacity by an order of magnitude; `mat_orbital_industry` consumes lift as its feedstock; blockades now meaningfully strangle a planet's orbital layer without firing on it.
- **Strategic advantages:** The gateway to every orbital ambition, and the difference between an army that embarks in a day and one that embarks in a week.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Lift concentrates in one or two complexes per planet — a single sabotage or strike closes the planet's door to space. Standardized manifests are legible manifests: `esp_sigint` reads them, revealing what a planet is building in orbit. Port labor becomes a political bloc with strike leverage the press system will amplify.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Port-city boomtowns; dockworker strikes as espionage-incited unrest; evacuation-under-fire set pieces when a planet falls.

### Cycler Transit Networks (`space_cycler_network`)
- **Domain:** Spaceflight & Propulsion | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** space_orbital_logistics, econ_interstellar_banking
- **Concept:** Cycler ships ride permanent resonant orbits between planets, never accelerating, never stopping — passengers and freight catch the bus with small taxis. Transport cost falls to nearly the cost of the rendezvous.
- **What becomes possible:** Scheduled, fleet-free mass transport inside a system: ore, grain, colonists, and mail moving on timetables rather than on warships.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Scheduled transit lines**: in-system trade segments and population migration run automatically on cycler schedules without fleet escort. Opens two standing decisions: **migration policy** (who may ride — open, permit-based, closed; feeds demographics and unrest) and **line ownership** (state-run vs chartered to a corporation from the charter catalog, which is cheaper but extracts rents and votes its interests).
- **Engine hook:** `TradeSegment` gains an `isCycler` flag with schedule-driven throughput pulses; order `CYCLER_CHARTER { systemId, corpId }` granting a charter-catalog corporation the line; migration pulses handled in the demographics tick; `unlockFlag: 'cycler_lines'`.
- **Required capacity:** Cyclers must be built (shipyard time, metals) and inserted — one-time high cost per line; both endpoints need functioning lift (`space_orbital_logistics`). Lines only exist between planets in the same system.
- **Interactions:** Moves `fusion_fuel` and ore cheaply, feeding `phys_fusion_power` economies; migration interacts with rebellion/cohesion mechanics (letting dissidents leave lowers unrest but exports it); chartered lines create corporate actors the parliament and espionage systems can lean on.
- **Strategic advantages:** The cheapest logistics in the game — an in-system economy that runs itself while your fleets fight elsewhere.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Timetables are predictable: pirates and raider-doctrine fleets can time attacks against cycler windows (lawlessness feeds on it). Cyclers cannot be rerouted in wartime — the schedule is the schedule, and abandoning it strands whatever was aboard. Corporate lines resist nationalization; taking them back costs political capital and possibly a parliament fight.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Cycler heists as pirate signature moves; refugee flows on open lines during civil wars; a corporation that owns every line in three systems becoming a political problem.

## Tier 2 — Specialization

### Directed-Energy Systems (`phys_directed_energy`)
- **Domain:** Physics & Energy | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** phys_fusion_power, mat_advanced_composites
- **Concept:** Phased-array lasers and neutral-particle beams reach weapons-grade coherence at range — and the same physics that puts a megawatt on a hull can put a megawatt into a receiver. Every aperture is dual-use.
- **What becomes possible:** Light-speed weapons, point defense, and wireless power transmission from planetary grids to orbit.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **dual-use aperture**: each surface beam_array runs in one of two modes — **weapon mode** (contributes orbital_defense_power, enables point-defense against AirSortie bomber waves) or **relay mode** (feeds grid surplus to orbital structures and slowly recharges the supplyLevel of friendly fleets in-system). Switching takes ticks, so the mode you're in when the attack starts is the mode you fight in. Also unlocks DEW refits for ships via the combat engine (unit_stat changes attached to a refit, not free).
- **Engine hook:** Building `beam_array` with a persisted mode state; order `BEAM_MODE_SET { arrayId, mode }` in the game-loop order switch; new orbital effect `orbital_power_feed`; ship refit unlock `dew_refit` in ship-production-service; point-defense interception hook in the air-sortie resolution path.
- **Required capacity:** Arrays draw hard on the planetary energy budget — firing during a deficit browns out districts mid-battle. Weapon-grade output effectively requires `phys_grid_storage` beam surge or a gigascale grid. Refitting ships needs spaceyard time; arrays need clear-sky districts.
- **Interactions:** The transmission physics behind `phys_orbital_power_relay`; launch infrastructure for `phys_relativistic_kinetics`; beam surge from `phys_grid_storage`; counters `mil_orbital_strike` bombardment by thinning sortie waves.
- **Strategic advantages:** Planetary defense that scales with your energy economy instead of your ammo stockpile, plus a logistics trickle (fleet recharge) nobody else's defense grid provides.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Arrays can be turned inward: firing on your own surface during a rebellion is mechanically available and politically catastrophic — legitimacy loss, cohesion damage, and a press-system atrocity cycle that follows you for seasons. Relay mode is a standing temptation for espionage: a compromised array (op-catalog target) can be defocused across its own city. High-energy apertures make your grid deficit a combat statistic your enemies can compute.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** "Aperture politics" — cities lobbying against arrays overhead; blinding incidents as diplomatic crises; the first government to answer a secession with beam fire discovering what that does to every other planet's loyalty.

### Torch Drives (`space_advanced_drives`)
- **Domain:** Spaceflight & Propulsion | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** phys_fusion_power, space_orbital_logistics
- **Concept:** Continuous-thrust fusion torches replace coast-and-correct transfers with brachistochrone trajectories — accelerate to the midpoint, flip, decelerate. Travel time collapses; fuel appetite explodes.
- **What becomes possible:** Fleets that arrive when the strategist wants them to, not when orbital mechanics permits — at a price paid in fuel, wear, and visibility.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **hard-burn order**: any fleet movement can be flagged to burn at military thrust, cutting ETA sharply while multiplying supply consumption and logisticsStrain — and spiking the fleet's detectability (a torch at full burn is the brightest object in the system). Creates the assault-window game: arrive before the defender's reserves activate, or arrive quietly — never both. Also unlocks tanker-extended range for in-system operations.
- **Engine hook:** Upgraded `HyperdriveProfile` LayerModifiers (higher speedMultiplier on `trade`/`corridor` layers); `FleetOrder` gains a `burnLevel` flag read by movement-service; detectability spike routed through visibility-service; drive module unlock in ship-production-service.
- **Required capacity:** Torch-rated hulls must be built or refitted (spaceyard); hard burns consume `fusion_fuel` from fleet supply — a fleet without a supply line hard-burns exactly once. Sustained torch operations effectively require forward fuel stocks.
- **Interactions:** The propulsion basis for `space_deep_navigation` and `phys_relativistic_kinetics`; hard-burn arrivals pair with `mil_orbital_strike` first-strike doctrine; `space_expeditionary_logistics` tenders are what make repeated burns sustainable; Raider-doctrine fleets love the speed and hate the visibility.
- **Strategic advantages:** Operational tempo. The faction with torch drives picks when battles happen.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Hard burns announce intent: your invasion is visible in the defender's sensor picture hours before arrival, feeding `esp_predictive_intel`. Torch exhaust is a weapon — maneuvering at military thrust near stations and cyclers causes incidents the press system reports as atrocities whether or not they were accidents. Fuel logistics become a second front; strand a torch fleet and it is a very expensive monument.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Feint burns as deception plays; fuel-denial campaigns; treaties regulating military thrust in civilian traffic lanes — and the incidents that break them.

### Gigascale Concentration (`phys_gigascale_power`)
- **Domain:** Physics & Energy | **Tier:** 2 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `phys_t2_power_doctrine`
- **Prerequisites:** phys_fusion_power, infra_planetary_grid
- **Concept:** Confinement physics rewards scale: a stellarator the size of a city is disproportionately more efficient than a hundred small plants. Civilizations that follow the physics build cathedrals of power.
- **What becomes possible:** Continent-spanning fusion campuses that power an entire planet with margin to spare — the doctrine of concentration.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **fusion campus**: the game's first deliberate multi-district structure, occupying 3–5 contiguous Voronoi districts as a single facility. One campus powers the whole planet and generates export-grade surplus — the only Tier 2 doctrine that can feed antimatter production, orbital elevators, and beam arrays at full rating simultaneously. Surplus can be committed to standing **industrial overdrive** on chosen districts.
- **Engine hook:** Building `fusion_campus` with a multi-tile footprint (extends the construction system's single-tile assumption — flagged as engine work); campus loss triggers a `grid_cascade` event in the tick processor: planet-wide blackout plus damage to adjacent districts; `modifierKey: 'energy_surplus_export'`.
- **Required capacity:** 3–5 contiguous flat districts (real geometry on the Voronoi map — some planets simply cannot host one), heavy fuel throughput, a large resident workforce, and dev-finance-scale capital. Effectively one campus per developed planet.
- **Interactions:** The natural partner of `infra_orbital_elevator` and `phys_antimatter` (both are energy gluttons); pairs with `phys_grid_storage` since everything rides one source; `soc_surveillance_state` civilizations like how legible one big plant is.
- **Strategic advantages:** The highest ceiling of the three doctrines — if you intend to run antimatter foundries and an elevator on the same planet, this is the only doctrine that can.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** One point of failure per planet: a successful `ESP_SABOTAGE_FACILITY`, a bombardment run, or a rebellion holding the campus districts turns off an entire world. Campus construction displaces whatever stood on those districts — expect unrest and press coverage. The campus workforce is a concentrated labor bloc with enormous strike leverage.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Campus towns with their own political identity; enemy war plans that are really just campus target folders; the slow horror of defending a civil war in which the rebels hold the campus.

### Distributed Microreactor Swarms (`phys_microreactor_swarm`)
- **Domain:** Physics & Energy | **Tier:** 2 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `phys_t2_power_doctrine`
- **Prerequisites:** phys_fusion_power, mat_advanced_composites
- **Concept:** Factory-built, sealed, cartridge-swapped microreactors — power as a mass-manufactured commodity instead of a civil-engineering project. No grid required; every consumer is its own utility.
- **What becomes possible:** A civilization that cannot be switched off. Every district, army column, and frontier outpost carries its own power.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **District self-power**: districts keep functioning through grid failure, blockade, siege, and bombardment — blackout events and grid-targeting sabotage largely stop working against you. Unlocks **mobile power** for armies (supply-decay reduction, faster entrenchment) and **power drops** for new colonies (skip early energy infrastructure entirely, accelerating frontier claims through the claim/anchor/integrate phases).
- **Engine hook:** `unlockFlag: 'district_self_power'` checked by blackout/siege logic in the tick processor; army modifier `field_power` (supply decay reduction) in the ground-combat path; frontier-claim presence bonus in the movement/frontier service; espionage op-catalog addition: `reactor_core_theft` targeting your districts.
- **Required capacity:** A reactor fabrication line (dedicated factory building) and continuous cartridge logistics through the civilian channel; the swarm is only as good as its replacement-core flow. No planetary surplus is generated — this doctrine has no export margin.
- **Interactions:** Makes `plan_closed_ecologies` habitats and hostile-world colonies genuinely independent; blunts enemy `mil_orbital_strike` counter-grid doctrine; weak partner for `phys_antimatter` (no surplus to feed foundries — antimatter production runs at reduced rate under this doctrine).
- **Strategic advantages:** Resilience as identity. Sieges, blockades, and infrastructure sabotage — three whole enemy toolkits — lose most of their teeth against you.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Proliferation cuts both ways: rebel districts and secessionist planets keep *their* power too — suppression campaigns and cohesion enforcement get measurably harder against your own citizens. Stolen cores become dirty weapons and black-market exports. The missing export surplus locks you out of the energy-glutton megaprojects at full rate: this doctrine has a real ceiling.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Insurgencies that never run out of lights; a black market in cores flowing through free ports; the awkward moment when your own doctrine powers the civil war against you.

### Beamed Sky Power (`phys_orbital_power_relay`)
- **Domain:** Physics & Energy | **Tier:** 2 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `phys_t2_power_doctrine`
- **Prerequisites:** phys_fusion_power, phys_directed_energy, space_orbital_logistics
- **Concept:** Collectors in orbit see the sun without night, weather, or atmosphere; microwave and laser downlinks deliver the harvest to surface rectennas. Generation moves off-planet entirely.
- **What becomes possible:** Planets powered from the sky — and, for the first time, energy that travels between worlds.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Off-surface generation**: a power_relay_constellation in orbit replaces surface plants, freeing their district tiles for anything else (the only doctrine that *returns* districts to the player). Downlinks can be retargeted across a system — one constellation can feed several bodies, creating the game's first **inter-planet energy trade** and making energy itself something a system's owner exports, rations, or cuts off.
- **Engine hook:** New orbital structure `power_relay_constellation` in data/orbital-structures.ts (category logistics, effect `planetary_energy_feed`, requiresStation); surface building `rectenna_field` (cheap, low-footprint); order `RELAY_RETARGET { constellationId, planetId }`; downlink integrity coupled to orbital control checks in the orbital service.
- **Required capacity:** Station + constellation in orbit (lift-intensive to build), rectenna fields on every fed planet, and — decisively — **orbital control**: the doctrine only works over planets whose orbital layer you hold.
- **Interactions:** Extends naturally toward `mega_dyson_swarm` (the same doctrine at stellar scale); synergizes with `mega_orbital_habitats` (habitats feed from the constellation directly); binary_star systems overperform; hostile fleets interact with it very directly.
- **Strategic advantages:** The most district-efficient and most expansion-friendly doctrine — new colonies need one cheap rectenna, not an energy sector.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Orbit dependency is total: lose the orbital layer — blockade, bombardment, or a lost fleet battle — and the planet browns out *immediately*, no siege required. Downlink beams are hijackable: a compromised constellation (espionage op) can defocus a beam across the city it feeds. Surface energy workers are made redundant a second time; the political grievance compounds.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Wars won by taking orbit and simply waiting; energy vassalage — minor factions fed from a hegemon's constellation; rectenna towns that pray, accurately, that the sky stays friendly.

## Tier 3 — Dominance

### Antimatter Production & Containment (`phys_antimatter`)
- **Domain:** Physics & Energy | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** phys_directed_energy, mat_orbital_industry
- **Concept:** Industrial antiproton factories — accelerator rings converting colossal electrical input into the densest energy store physics allows — with magnetic containment reliable enough to bank the output. Antimatter is not an energy source; it is a battery with no equal and no forgiveness.
- **What becomes possible:** Strategic energy stockpiles: fuel for torch fleets beyond any range they had, warheads beyond any yield they had, and the power feed for Tier 4 megaprojects.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **antimatter chain**: orbital foundries convert sustained grid surplus into the `antimatter` resource, which exists *only* in orbital containment rings — a new storage class that cannot be warehoused on a surface, cannot be stockpiled casually, and detonates if its housing dies. Spend it on: fleet range extension (deep-space endurance), antimatter warhead refits (city-killers with treaty-grade diplomatic consequences), or as the committed power source for `space_lane_forging`, `phys_starlifting`, and `phys_singularity_engine` projects.
- **Engine hook:** Orbital structures `antimatter_foundry` and `containment_ring` (data/orbital-structures.ts, requiresStation, high infrastructureRequired); resource `antimatter` with new storage target `containment` in storage-service; combat-engine rule: a containment_ring reduced to zero hull detonates — orbital-layer wipe plus surface devastation on the planet below; production draws from `energy_surplus_export`.
- **Required capacity:** This is the sharpest knowledge/implementation gap in the domain: research is cheap, capability is not. Foundries need `mat_orbital_industry`-grade orbital manufacturing, a station, and a *sustained* energy surplus — gigascale or relay doctrine at full health, or a singularity engine later. Microreactor civilizations produce at a fraction of the rate. Warhead refits additionally need capital-tier spaceyards.
- **Interactions:** Powers all three Tier 4 megaproject techs; extends `space_deep_navigation` endurance dramatically; warheads interact with parliament (launch-authority votes), the press system, and every diplomacy AI's threat model; foundry output is a natural `esp_sigint` collection target.
- **Strategic advantages:** The keys to Tier 4, plus the heaviest conventional stick in the game. A faction with banked antimatter negotiates differently.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have built a bomb over your own capital and everyone knows it: containment rings are hostage, target, and provocation at once — destroying one is an atrocity, but *you* placed it there. Stockpiles invite first strikes and sabotage ops with planetary stakes. Production monopolizes surplus that your industry, shields, and beam arrays wanted — an opportunity cost measured in everything else.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Antimatter-limitation treaties and the espionage ops that verify them; containment-failure disasters that reshape a faction's politics for seasons; deterrence standoffs between ring-owning rivals neither of whom can strike first.

### Deep-Space Navigation (`space_deep_navigation`)
- **Domain:** Spaceflight & Propulsion | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** space_advanced_drives, comm_deep_space_network
- **Concept:** Pulsar-timing positioning, long-baseline inertial reference, and drift-corrected ephemeris charts: the art of knowing where you are when there is no lane, no beacon, and no one to ask.
- **What becomes possible:** The void between the lanes stops being a wall and becomes an ocean — slow, dark, and open to anyone who can navigate it.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Off-graph operations**: the `deepSpace` movement layer becomes a real operational choice — fleets can plot paths between *any* two systems, bypassing hyperlane chokepoints, corridor denial fields, gate access policies, and blockades entirely, at low speed and very low detectability. Additionally unlocks **route charting**: a `NAV_SURVEY_ROUTE` operation that turns a flown deep-space path into a persistent charted route (faster and safer on every subsequent transit) — making route knowledge itself a strategic asset that can be hoarded, shared with allies, or stolen.
- **Engine hook:** `HyperdriveProfile.deepSpace` LayerModifiers unlocked/substantially improved; `FleetOrder.preferredLayer: 'deepSpace'` becomes valid for arbitrary system pairs in movement-service pathing; detectability handled by visibility-service; charted routes persisted as faction assets feeding `ReshapeResult.newDeepSpacePaths`; new order `NAV_SURVEY_ROUTE { fleetId, fromSystemId, toSystemId }`; charted-route theft added to the espionage op catalog.
- **Required capacity:** Navigation suites are a ship refit (yard time); off-lane fleets consume supply with no resupply available — without `space_expeditionary_logistics` caches, deep-space range is a hard wall, and a miscalculated transit is a lost fleet. The comm relay network (`comm_deep_space_network`) must actually cover the departure region.
- **Interactions:** The strategic answer to `mil_command_networks` chokepoint defense and corridor militarization; pairs with Raider doctrine and `esp_synthetic_identities`-style covert insertion; prerequisite for `space_lane_forging` (you must chart space before you can fold it); `phys_antimatter` fuel extends endurance.
- **Strategic advantages:** Strategic surprise as a permanent option. Every defensive line in the galaxy was drawn assuming the lane graph is the map; you now own the margin of that map.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Symmetry: once this proliferates, *your* borders stop meaning what they meant, and every fortified chokepoint you paid for defends less than it did. Off-lane fleets are unsupported by definition — interception in deep space is rare but fatal. Charted routes leak: a stolen chart is an invasion plan for your own backdoor. Lost-fleet events are real and the press reports them as scandals.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Deep-space cat-and-mouse between raiders and pickets; secret allied route-sharing pacts; the slow death of the fortress-chokepoint strategic era and the political fallout among factions that invested in it.

### Expeditionary Fleet Trains (`space_expeditionary_logistics`)
- **Domain:** Spaceflight & Propulsion | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** space_advanced_drives, infra_automated_ports, mil_command_networks
- **Concept:** Tankers, tenders, and mobile dock ships organized as fleet trains — the unglamorous doctrine that navies project power exactly as far as their logistics tail reaches, and not one system further.
- **What becomes possible:** Sustained naval operations far from home: forward bases that persist, fleets repaired at anchor in hostile space, and supply caches hidden where only your navigators can find them.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **The logistics tail as a fleet asset**: (1) ForwardBase entries stop auto-expiring while a tender squadron is assigned — forward bases become persistent, upgradeable staging infrastructure instead of countdown timers; (2) fleet-to-fleet resupply orders transfer supply between fleets at sea; (3) with `space_deep_navigation`, **deep-space caches** — supply dumps at charted off-lane points, invisible to normal sensor coverage, enabling campaigns that never touch a friendly port.
- **Engine hook:** ForwardBase lifecycle change in movement-service (`expiresAtSeconds` suspended while tenders assigned, `supply` refreshed per tick); new order `FLEET_RESUPPLY { fromFleetId, toFleetId }` beside the ESP_* cases in game-loop; ship class `fleet_tender` in ship-production-service; cache entities reusing the ForwardBase shape at charted deep-space coordinates; `modifierKey: 'forward_supply_radius'`.
- **Required capacity:** Tenders are real hulls competing for yard slots with warships; every train ties up freight the civilian economy wanted (automated-ports throughput is the feedstock). Forward bases still need initial establishment and periodic convoy top-up — a *route* that must be defended.
- **Interactions:** Turns `space_advanced_drives` hard-burn from a stunt into a campaign tempo; makes deep-space invasions actually sustainable; convoy routes interact with piracy, blockade InfraActions, and Raider doctrine — your logistics is now a theater; `mil_autonomous_warfare` later automates train scheduling.
- **Strategic advantages:** Reach. Two identical navies are not identical if one of them can stay on station eight systems from home indefinitely.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Fleet trains are the softest high-value targets in the game — raider doctrine exists to eat them, and losing a train can end a campaign without a fleet battle. Permanent forward basing normalizes permanent war: war-weariness, endless-war economics, and parliament factions who profit from the tail and lobby against peace. The standing overhead is enormous.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Convoy-war seasons; tender crews as a political constituency; wars that formally end while the bases quietly stay.

### Relativistic Kinetics (`phys_relativistic_kinetics`)
- **Domain:** Physics & Energy | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** phys_directed_energy, space_advanced_drives, mil_orbital_strike
- **Concept:** Beam-riding sails and staged mass drivers push inert slugs to relativistic fractions; at those speeds kinetic energy dwarfs any warhead, and nothing in physics offers a reliable intercept. The weapon is an argument: it cannot be recalled, so it is mostly built never to be used.
- **What becomes possible:** True strategic deterrence — and the first weapon whose primary function is diplomatic.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** The **deterrence posture**: RKV batteries are built against pre-surveyed target folders (each target must be surveyed — reconnaissance is part of the arsenal). Declaring deterrence is a public act that suppresses enemy AI aggression and reshapes diplomacy — but launch is a one-way order: unstoppable, unrecallable, planet-devastating, and it converts you into a permanent diplomatic pariah with automatic coalition triggers. The mechanic is the gap between *having* the weapon and *being the faction that used it* — a standing commitment trap the player lives inside.
- **Engine hook:** Orbital structure `rkv_battery`; faction flag `deterrence_declared` read by AI evaluators through `GameStateContext.warThreat`; order `RKV_LAUNCH { batteryId, targetPlanetId }` marked irreversible in the order queue; maps directly onto the existing `PrimaryEffectType.COMMITMENT_TRAP`; launch emits a maximum-priority galaxy-wide press-system event; parliament launch-authority votes gate the order (political capital cost).
- **Required capacity:** Batteries need dedicated orbital structures, launch-grade beam arrays (`phys_directed_energy` infrastructure at weapon rating), and surveyed target folders maintained against stale intelligence. Deterrence only functions if *visible* — hiding the batteries wastes them.
- **Interactions:** Interacts with `esp_predictive_intel` (false-alarm events), `comm_infowar_suite` (spoofed launch warnings as an infowar op), parliament and coup mechanics (who holds launch authority is a constitutional question), and every AI's diplomatic stance toward you.
- **Strategic advantages:** Wars you never have to fight. A declared deterrent measurably suppresses aggression against your core worlds and anchors your negotiating position.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Deterrence requires transparency, so it hands your rivals a target list of your own batteries. It triggers arms races and makes you the automatic priority in any first-strike calculus. Launch authority is a coup magnet — a military faction holding the keys is one cabinet crisis from holding the government. False-alarm events force genuine decisions under uncertainty, and the one thing the system cannot survive is being used.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Arms-control diplomacy with espionage-verified limits; a coup plot whose real objective is the launch keys; the galaxy-defining moment when somebody actually fires one.

## Tier 4 — Transformation

### Stellar Husbandry (`phys_starlifting`)
- **Domain:** Physics & Energy | **Tier:** 4 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `phys_t4_stellar_destiny`
- **Prerequisites:** phys_antimatter, mega_dyson_swarm
- **Concept:** Focused swarm mirrors heat a strip of photosphere until the star's own rotation flings plasma free; magnetic scoops centrifuge the stream into hydrogen, helium-3, and metals. The star becomes a mine — and, managed carefully, a garden: matter removed today is stellar lifespan added tomorrow.
- **What becomes possible:** A civilization that harvests its own sun. Matter and energy cease to be economic constraints in the home system; the star's output itself becomes an instrument of policy.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **The star as an economic and climatic dial**: starlifting converts a dyson-swarmed star into a system-level production entry (metals, rares, fusion_fuel at civilization scale) with an extraction-rate slider the player sets. The rate directly couples to stellar output: harder lifting dims the star, imposing climate penalties on every planet in the system (agriculture collapse, famine tags) unless mitigated per-planet by `plan_climate_control`. Gentle lifting extends the star's life — a legacy project measured in eras. One slider, and everything in the system depends on where you set it.
- **Engine hook:** Megastructure stage layered on the `mega_dyson_swarm` asset; system-level production entry in the economy tick; new system tag `starlift_active` (system-tags registry); stellar-output variable coupling into planetary climate/agriculture modifiers; famine tag application through the existing temporary-tag mechanism; season PRODUCTION score channel.
- **Required capacity:** A completed Dyson swarm, antimatter-fed swarm logistics, and orbital industry throughput to process the stream. Full benefit requires climate mitigation on every inhabited planet in-system — without it, the mine pays for itself in famines.
- **Interactions:** Feeds `mat_molecular_manufacturing` and capital shipyards with effectively unlimited feedstock; famine and unrest couple into rebellion and cohesion systems; holy_site populations and religious blocs react to the dimming star through the ideology system; the press system treats extraction-rate changes as news.
- **Strategic advantages:** The deepest economy in the game, concentrated where you are strongest. No blockade can starve a civilization that eats its own sun.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Everything anchors to one system — the ultimate strategic centralization, and every rival knows the address. The dimming is visible galaxy-wide: "they are eating the sun" is a press and legitimacy catastrophe among your own religious populations and a ready-made coalition casus belli abroad. Alterations to stellar output are irreversible on any human timescale; a mismanaged slider is a permanent scar.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Pilgrimage-world secessions framed as heresy trials; coalition wars fought explicitly to stop a dimming; the long argument between generations about whether the star is a resource or an ancestor.

### Metric Lane Engineering (`space_lane_forging`)
- **Domain:** Spaceflight & Propulsion | **Tier:** 4 | **Mutually exclusive group:** `phys_t4_stellar_destiny`
- **Prerequisites:** space_deep_navigation, phys_antimatter
- **Concept:** Hyperlanes are standing topological features of the galactic metric — and features can be engineered. With antimatter-scale energy released along a precisely charted null path, a new channel can be stabilized; with less finesse, an existing one can be collapsed.
- **What becomes possible:** The map stops being terrain and becomes policy. The lane graph every strategy in the galaxy assumes is permanent — yours to redraw.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Graph editing**: two megaproject operations. **Forge** creates a permanent new hyperlane between two systems you have deep-space charted (slow, astronomically expensive, antimatter-fed) — build backdoors into your heartland's defense, shortcut your logistics, or open a second front no defender planned for. **Sever** collapses an existing lane (faster, cheaper, violent) — delete the chokepoint an enemy fortress guards, or amputate a region entirely. Every edit rewrites pathing, trade segments, corridor structure, and cohesion distance for every faction at once.
- **Engine hook:** Writes directly through the existing `ReshapeResult.newLinks` path into the lane-graph rebuild (lib/movement/lane-graph.ts), with corridor recompute via `fracturedCorridorIds`; orders `LANE_FORGE { fromSystemId, toSystemId }` and `LANE_SEVER { fromSystemId, toSystemId }` in the game-loop order switch; instability and escalationLevel spikes on affected SystemNodes; press-system galaxy event on every edit; trade-segment reroute cascade through the degradation system.
- **Required capacity:** Both endpoints deep-space charted; a dedicated forging fleet on-station for the project duration; a banked antimatter budget per edit that competes with every other Tier 4 ambition. Severing inside contested space means defending the project fleet while it works.
- **Interactions:** Charted routes from `space_deep_navigation` are the survey basis; interacts with gate infrastructure (a forged lane obsoletes someone's gate monopoly); cohesion-service distance-from-capital recomputes — a well-placed forge can *save* a fraying empire, a sever can shatter one; corridor militarization investments can be deleted, with the political fallout landing on whoever funded them.
- **Strategic advantages:** Strategic geography as a weapon and a shield. No other capability in the game invalidates enemy investment so completely.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Every forged lane is a two-way door — your shortcut is, forever, also their invasion route. Severing strands populations: humanitarian catastrophe, refugee flows, and a press cycle that does not end. Any use is a galaxy-wide diplomatic shock with coalition triggers — the other factions understand immediately that the map itself is now hostage. Misfired collapses create permanently unstable regions no one can use, including you.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Lane-forging conventions and the wars that follow their violation; extortion diplomacy ("lovely chokepoint; shame if it stopped existing"); a galaxy whose late-game map is an artifact of your decisions, studied by every player who inherits it.

### Singularity Engines (`phys_singularity_engine`)
- **Domain:** Physics & Energy | **Tier:** 4
- **Prerequisites:** phys_antimatter, mat_molecular_manufacturing
- **Concept:** A rotating black hole stores extractable rotational energy — the Penrose process lets infalling matter leave with more energy than it brought, at efficiencies fusion cannot approach. The galaxy's black holes are pre-charged batteries of stellar mass, waiting for a civilization audacious enough to plug in.
- **What becomes possible:** Energy beyond any grid, any swarm, any doctrine — harvested from the most extreme objects in the galaxy.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Unique strategic sites**: the Penrose plant is buildable *only* in systems carrying the `black_hole` tag — roughly a half-dozen exist in the entire galaxy, and their locations are fixed at world generation. A completed plant out-produces every other energy source in the game and can feed antimatter foundries, lane forges, and starlift logistics simultaneously via relay export. The mechanic is the contest: a handful of immovable, ownable, galaxy-defining sites that every late-game power calculus must route through — the game's natural late-era war objectives.
- **Engine hook:** Megastructure `penrose_plant` gated on `SystemNode.tags.includes('black_hole')`; system-level energy export consumed through the `phys_orbital_power_relay` / `energy_surplus_export` pathways; season TERRITORY and PRODUCTION scoring weight on held black-hole systems; feeding-accident catastrophe event class in the tick processor; site contention surfaces through existing frontier-claim and escalation mechanics.
- **Required capacity:** Possession of a black-hole system — likely remote, likely contested, possibly currently someone else's; molecular-manufacturing-grade containment components; antimatter-budget construction; and a defended relay/logistics corridor from the site back to your industrial heart, because the energy is worthless where it is made.
- **Interactions:** The only single source that runs every Tier 4 megaproject at full rate at once; `space_deep_navigation` reaches remote sites off-lane and `space_expeditionary_logistics` sustains the garrison; `dyson_remnant` and `black_hole` tag lore intertwine — surveys of remnant systems hint at who tried this before; espionage against a plant is the highest-stakes sabotage target in the game.
- **Strategic advantages:** The energy ceiling of the game, plus a form of power rivals cannot replicate by spending more — there are only so many black holes, and you hold one.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Absolute dependence on one or two remote systems at the end of long, raidable supply and relay lines — your civilization's heart now beats outside its body, and every rival can read the map. Catastrophic failure modes (feeding accidents, containment excursions) are permanent, system-scale, and public. Holding the site means garrisoning the far frontier forever; losing it means an enemy inherits your engine intact.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Black-hole systems as the Jerusalem of late-game wars, changing hands across seasons; energy-vassal networks fed from a single plant; the quiet realization, upon surveying a Dyson remnant near a dead plant, of exactly how the previous owners' story ended.
