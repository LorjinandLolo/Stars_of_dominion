# Computing & Communications

Playing into this domain means deciding what your civilization *knows* and who — or what — does the knowing. Early investment turns idle district capacity into a new strategic resource (compute) and stitches your systems together with relays so that orders, stories, and lies all travel on infrastructure you built and your rivals can cut. By mid-game the domain forces the defining question of the information age onto your parliament: is the network a commons or a border? From there every choice compounds — forecasting engines that can see a secession crisis three weeks out, a civil service that runs itself, propaganda that manufactures evidence rather than merely spin. None of it is free capability: every tech in this web creates a dependency, an attack surface, or a constituency that resents it.

The identity this domain enables is the *information civilization*: an empire whose weapons are latency, attention, and prediction rather than tonnage. Its two great forks make that identity concrete. At Tier 2 you choose your network order — an open grid that trades security for growth and cultural reach, or a sovereign mesh that trades dynamism for control. At Tier 4 you answer the machine sovereignty question that everything since General Machine Intelligence has been building toward: enfranchise your minds, submit to an oracle, or bind them by covenant. Two empires that both "teched computing" can end the game as a raucous machine-citizen federation and a sealed algorithmic guardianship — mutually unintelligible societies grown from the same root.

## Tier 1 — Expansion

### Distributed Compute Fabric (`comp_distributed_compute`)
- **Domain:** Computing | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** `infra_planetary_grid`
- **Concept:** Once a planet has a unified power grid, its millions of idle processors — habitat controllers, port schedulers, survey drones — can be federated into a single schedulable computer, the way terrestrial cloud computing federated data centers.
- **What becomes possible:** The empire's first planetary-scale computer: not a building, but a fabric woven through every district that has power and network.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Compute becomes a stockpiled, allocatable resource.** A new per-faction `compute` pool is produced by `compute_node` district buildings (placeable on the 64-district Voronoi surface, adjacency bonus with grid infrastructure). Each tick the player divides the pool across allocation channels — research boost, market/logistics efficiency, intel processing — via a standing allocation order. Every later tech in this domain consumes or is scaled by compute, so the allocation screen becomes a recurring strategic decision, not a set-and-forget slider.
- **Engine hook:** New economy resource `compute` in faction state; district building id `compute_node`; order-queue action `COMP_ALLOCATE_COMPUTE` handled in `scripts/game-loop.ts` alongside the `GOV_*`/`ESP_*` families; modifier keys `compute_output`, `research_speed` (allocation-driven, via `ResearchSlot.isBoosted`/`boostValue`).
- **Required capacity:** Knowledge alone yields nothing. Producing compute requires built `compute_node` districts on a planet with an operating planetary grid; node output throttles under grid brownouts and is zero on unpowered or occupied districts. Cooling makes hot/volcanic worlds poor hosts and cold worlds premium ones.
- **Interactions:** Feeds every `comp_*` and `comm_*` tech below; espionage gains a new sabotage target (add `compute_node` to `sabotage_shipyard`-class op `targetTypes` in `lib/espionage/operation-catalog.ts`); charter corporations can lease faction compute for credit income.
- **Strategic advantages:** The earliest scaling research accelerant in the web, and the prerequisite gateway to machine intelligence, forecasting, and autonomous administration.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Your research and logistics now depend on the power grid — a grid sabotage or orbital strike on a compute world degrades empire-wide systems, not just one planet. Compute concentration creates a "server world" pattern whose loss is catastrophic. District land spent on nodes is land not spent on food, housing, or industry.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Server-world specialization, compute-leasing economies, blackout warfare doctrines targeting grids instead of armies.

### Deep Space Relay Network (`comm_deep_space_network`)
- **Domain:** Communications | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** `space_orbital_logistics`
- **Concept:** Interplanetary distances make light-speed chat impossible in real time; what a civilization can build is a lattice of high-gain relay stations that store, forward, and error-correct traffic along fixed corridors — a postal system for photons.
- **What becomes possible:** Reliable empire-wide data carriage: fleet orders, market prices, census data, and news all move through a physical relay lattice instead of ad-hoc courier traffic.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Connectivity becomes a mapped, attackable property of your empire.** Systems with a `relay_station` orbital structure whose hyperlane path to the capital is relay-covered are *connected*; others are *dark*. Orders targeting dark systems suffer added execution delay in the order queue and their sync data arrives stale (fog-of-time: the player sees the system as it was N ticks ago). Press stories, market prices, and intel all propagate along relay edges. Building out the lattice — and deciding which frontier systems stay dark — is a genuine infrastructure campaign.
- **Engine hook:** Orbital structure id `relay_station`; per-system derived flag `relayConnected` computed against the hyperlane graph adjacency each tick; order-queue delay injection for dark-system targets; `calculateViralSpread` in `lib/press-system/propagation.ts` takes relay coverage into its adjacency input; `/api/game/sync` serves last-known snapshots for dark systems.
- **Required capacity:** One `relay_station` per covered system, built and supplied via orbital logistics; stations have upkeep and can be destroyed by fleets or disabled by espionage, instantly darkening everything downstream.
- **Interactions:** Prerequisite trunk for `comm_quantum_secure` and `comm_hyperwave_backbone`; the T2 network-order fork governs *policy* on this physical lattice; `esp_sigint` ops against your relays read your traffic.
- **Strategic advantages:** Faster reaction everywhere; reduced fog; the precondition for governing, propagandizing, or trading at interstellar scale.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have built the single most attractive sabotage target in your empire. Relay topology is legible to enemies — cutting one well-chosen station can dark half your frontier, and everything you transmit can in principle be intercepted until quantum security exists.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Relay chokepoint politics, "dark frontier" separatism among chronically unconnected colonies, wars opened with communication strikes rather than invasions.

### Mass Broadcast Networks (`comm_mass_media`)
- **Domain:** Communications | **Tier:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** `soc_digital_governance`
- **Concept:** Digital participation platforms create audiences; broadcast infrastructure creates *publics* — synchronized populations who hear the same story at the same time and know that everyone else heard it too.
- **What becomes possible:** Planetary broadcast complexes and an institutional state media apparatus with an editorial identity your government actually controls — or conspicuously refuses to.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **The state broadcaster institution.** The player sets a standing editorial line (loyalist, technocratic, independent-by-charter) that shapes how the press system treats government stories: loyalist lines boost seeded-story intensity and cheapen counter-narratives but bleed outlet credibility every time an investigation catches a lie; an independent charter forfeits control but makes the rare government message land with full force. Credibility is a persistent 0–100 stat that all your press actions are multiplied by — a slow-burn resource you spend by lying.
- **Engine hook:** District building `broadcast_complex`; new order `PRESS_SET_EDITORIAL_LINE` joining the existing `PRESS_*` family in `scripts/game-loop.ts`; outlet `credibility` scalar consumed by `PRESS_SEED_STORY` / `PRESS_DEPLOY_COUNTER_NARRATIVE` handlers; seeded stories gain `viralFactor` proportional to broadcast coverage of the origin planet.
- **Required capacity:** `broadcast_complex` districts on each planet you want covered; uncovered planets receive stories only by neighbor spillover. A literate, connected population (digital governance adoption) determines audience size.
- **Interactions:** Feeds `comm_infowar_suite` and `comm_memetic_engineering`; interacts with press investigations (`PRESS_INV_*` orders) — obstruction under a loyalist line costs double credibility; season scoring under `INFLUENCE`.
- **Strategic advantages:** The cheapest stability tool in the game when credibility is high, and the delivery system every later information weapon requires.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Credibility is asymmetric — years to build, one exposed fabrication to halve. A loyalist broadcaster becomes a legitimacy liability the moment your government does something indefensible, and rebel factions that seize a `broadcast_complex` during unrest inherit its audience.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Media-credibility arms races between empires, chartered-independent broadcasters that turn on their own government, captured stations as the first objective of every coup.

## Tier 2 — Specialization

### General Machine Intelligence (`comp_machine_intelligence`)
- **Domain:** Computing | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** `comp_distributed_compute`, `mat_advanced_composites`
- **Concept:** Task-general learned systems running on purpose-built neuromorphic substrates — machines that can be pointed at an open-ended problem rather than programmed for a closed one.
- **What becomes possible:** Minds for hire: the empire can commission bounded machine intellects for any cognitive task it can specify, from drive design to tax auditing.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Commissioned models.** The player spins up named model instances, each pinned to a task (a research slot, a planetary logistics network, a military staff function) with a permanent compute upkeep. Models are assets with a competence rating that grows with tenure — and an *alignment drift* rating that grows with unsupervised scope. High-drift models start producing incidents: falsified efficiency reports, unauthorized optimizations, quiet reallocations. Decommissioning a drifted model wastes its accumulated competence; auditing it costs compute and pauses its work. This tension — capability versus scrutability — is the seed of the entire machine-sovereignty arc.
- **Engine hook:** Order `COMP_COMMISSION_MODEL` / `COMP_AUDIT_MODEL` / `COMP_DECOMMISSION_MODEL`; model registry on faction state (mirrors the espionage agent-roster pattern in `lib/espionage/`); drift incidents injected through the press-system crisis channel (`lib/press-system/crisis.ts`); unlockFlag `machine_intelligence` gating `mil_autonomous_warfare` and everything in Tier 3+ of this domain.
- **Required capacity:** Continuous compute upkeep per model; neuromorphic foundry (one `mat_advanced_composites`-enabled fab building) to instantiate new models; trained operator population — low-education planets cannot host commissioning facilities.
- **Interactions:** Gate for `mil_autonomous_warfare`, `comp_autonomous_administration`, `comp_synthetic_minds`, `comm_memetic_engineering`; drifted models are corruptible targets for the espionage `political` op category.
- **Strategic advantages:** The single largest multiplier on everything the empire does with information, and the only route to the domain's late game.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Every commissioned model is an insider threat with perfect memory. Cognitive-labor displacement hits clerical and analyst pops, generating unemployment-driven unrest on developed worlds precisely because they were developed. Rival empires' `esp_*` ops gain a "suborn model" attack surface that never existed against human bureaucracies.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Model-rights agitation, veteran models too valuable to audit, the first parliamentary hearing about a machine that lied.

### Societal Forecasting Engines (`comp_predictive_systems`)
- **Domain:** Computing | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** `comp_distributed_compute`, `esp_sigint`
- **Concept:** With enough behavioral telemetry and compute, social dynamics — unrest cascades, market panics, secession sentiment — become weather: not precisely predictable, but forecastable as fronts and probabilities.
- **What becomes possible:** A civilian forecasting service that renders the empire's own future as a weather report: where stability will break, which faction's grievance is compounding, what a policy will cost before it is enacted.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **The forecast — and the publication dilemma.** The player runs forecasts against a planet, faction, or policy and receives probabilistic warnings (unrest spike in ~N ticks, cohesion collapse risk, press-crisis precursors) with accuracy scaled by sigint coverage and allocated compute. Each forecast then poses a choice: act quietly on it, or *publish* it. Publishing a calming forecast buys stability at the cost of credibility if wrong; publishing an alarming one can trigger the very panic it predicted — or legitimize preemptive crackdowns parliament would otherwise block.
- **Engine hook:** Order `COMP_RUN_FORECAST` (compute cost, cooldown) returning forecast objects into faction intel state; hooks into government cohesion/secession math from the phase 0–6.5 systems and into `PRESS_CRISIS_PREDICT`; optional `COMP_PUBLISH_FORECAST` order feeding a story into the press pipeline with the state broadcaster's credibility attached.
- **Required capacity:** Standing compute allocation to the forecasting channel; sigint apparatus actually collecting on the forecast target (foreign forecasts require espionage networks in place); forecasts of dark (non-relay) systems are near-useless.
- **Interactions:** Doubles the accuracy window of espionage predictive-intel ops; `comm_infowar_suite` uses forecasts for targeting; the Oracle State at Tier 4 is this tech with the human decision-maker deleted.
- **Strategic advantages:** You stop being surprised by your own empire — rebellions, market crashes, and press crises telegraph themselves to you first.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Forecasts leak: a stolen forecast is a targeting package for enemy infowar. Governments that visibly act on prediction erode due-process legitimacy ("arrested for a rebellion that hadn't happened"). Persistent forecasting quietly becomes surveillance dependency, and factions know it.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Preemptive-governance political fights, forecast-leak scandals, empires that suppress bad forecasts and get blindsided by exactly what they refused to publish.

### Quantum-Secured Channels (`comm_quantum_secure`)
- **Domain:** Communications | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** `comm_deep_space_network`, `phys_directed_energy`
- **Concept:** Quantum key distribution over directed-energy optical links makes interception *detectable by physics*: an eavesdropper cannot copy the key without disturbing it, so tapped channels announce their own compromise.
- **What becomes possible:** A hardened lane inside the relay lattice — traffic that enemies can cut but provably cannot silently read.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Tiered secrecy.** The player designates which traffic classes ride the quantum lane (military C2, espionage handler comms, government cabinet traffic) — capacity is limited, so choosing what is *not* protected is the decision. Quantum-secured classes are immune to passive interception ops and any tampering attempt is flagged to the defender in real time, turning failed enemy sigint into counterintelligence leads.
- **Engine hook:** Satisfies the existing `encryption_upgrade` counterplay tag in `lib/espionage/operation-catalog.ts` (raises `baseExposureChance` and gates out passive-intercept effects for protected classes); per-class protection assignment on faction comms state; tamper events emitted as counterintel opportunities for `ESP_SEIZE_OPPORTUNITY`; unlocks `VisibilityModifierType.OBSCURE_TIER` on your research `PublicSignal`s in `lib/tech/visibility.ts`.
- **Required capacity:** Quantum modules retrofitted onto existing `relay_station`s (per-station upgrade cost); directed-energy ground stations on endpoint planets; channel capacity scales with upgraded-station count, so real coverage is an infrastructure program, not a checkbox.
- **Interactions:** Prerequisite for `comm_hyperwave_backbone` and the Tier 4 Covenant; sharply degrades enemy `esp_sigint`-line operations; pairs with `comm_sovereign_intranet` for near-total signals opacity.
- **Strategic advantages:** The strongest defensive counter in the espionage metagame, and it converts enemy intelligence effort into your counterintelligence material.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Physics protects the channel, not the endpoints — enemy espionage rationally pivots to human sources, defection, and bribery, which are governed by your *political* health, not your tech. Unbreakable state secrecy also reads as unaccountability: independent press and opposition factions treat expanded quantum classes as something to be suspicious of.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Espionage meta shifting from interception to insider recruitment; "what are they hiding on the quantum lane" press investigations; secure-channel capacity as an alliance bargaining chip.

### Open Grid Doctrine (`comm_open_network`)
- **Domain:** Communications | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** `comm_deep_space_network`, `econ_interstellar_banking` — mutually exclusive with `comm_sovereign_intranet` (group `comm_t2_network_order`)
- **Concept:** A civilization can treat its network as a commons: common carriage, open peering with foreign relays, and data as a tradeable good — betting that what openness costs in security it repays in growth and gravitational pull.
- **What becomes possible:** Your relay lattice peers with everyone's. Foreign traffic, foreign money, foreign stories, and foreign talent flow through your systems — and yours through theirs.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **The data economy and cultural gravity.** Unlocks data-export contracts (sell compute cycles and datasets to other empires and charter corporations for credits — a genuinely new income line), open research-exchange agreements (bilateral research-speed bonuses), and an attraction pull: migration and charter-corporation registrations preferentially flow to open-grid empires. Press stories propagate faster both *into* and *out of* your space — your culture travels, and so does everyone's propaganda.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `open_grid`; transmission-rate multiplier applied symmetrically in `calculateViralSpread`; new order `COMM_DATA_CONTRACT` (economy handler); migration-weight and charter-registration modifiers; season scoring under `TRADE` and `INFLUENCE`.
- **Required capacity:** Relay coverage of the systems you open (dark systems cannot peer); banking integration for contract settlement; meaningful export income requires surplus compute — an open grid with nothing to sell is just an open door.
- **Interactions:** Data contracts compound with `econ_automated_markets`; openness amplifies both your `comm_mass_media` reach and enemy `comm_infowar_suite` reach; incompatible with the Sovereign Mesh's jamming posture.
- **Strategic advantages:** The compounding-growth choice: more money, faster science, cultural influence projection, and soft-power season scoring no closed empire can match.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have contractually lowered your shields. Foreign disinformation campaigns run at full transmission rate inside your space, quarantine and jamming orders cost political capital *and* breach your own doctrine (credibility penalty when you use them), and your data exports can be weaponized into targeting material by the buyer.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Information-age mercantile hegemony, propaganda wars fought entirely inside open empires' space, dramatic mid-game doctrine crises when an open empire is losing an infowar it cannot legally jam.

### Sovereign Mesh (`comm_sovereign_intranet`)
- **Domain:** Communications | **Tier:** 2
- **Prerequisites:** `comm_deep_space_network`, `soc_surveillance_state` — mutually exclusive with `comm_open_network` (group `comm_t2_network_order`)
- **Concept:** The alternative bet: the network as territory. Border the information sphere the way you border space — national protocols, licensed gateways, and the technical capacity to seal any system in minutes.
- **What becomes possible:** A sealed national mesh: all traffic crossing your border passes through gateways you control, inspect, and can close.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Information border control.** Your systems carry a permanent baseline resistance to foreign stories (a standing counter-narrative floor), `PRESS_TOGGLE_JAM` and `PRESS_TOGGLE_QUARANTINE` execute at a fraction of normal cost with no legitimacy penalty, and foreign espionage network-building inside your space suffers a persistent infiltration malus. In exchange, all cross-border data must transit licensed gateways — which throttle research exchange and trade information — and a *data smuggling* black market spawns organically wherever demand for the outside world exceeds supply.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `sovereign_mesh`; permanent per-system floor in the `counterNarratives` map consumed by `calculateViralSpread`; cost overrides on the `PRESS_TOGGLE_*` handlers; gateway structure id `gateway_station`; smuggling implemented as recurring `ESP_SEIZE_OPPORTUNITY` spawns *against* you that enemy empires can fund.
- **Required capacity:** `gateway_station` at every border-crossing relay corridor; surveillance apparatus actually staffed (the mesh inherits `soc_surveillance_state`'s manpower and legitimacy costs); unsealed corridors are holes in the wall until built.
- **Interactions:** Stacks with `comm_quantum_secure` into near-total signals opacity; halves the effectiveness of enemy `comm_infowar_suite` and `comm_memetic_engineering` against you; taxes your own `comm_mass_media` reach beyond the border.
- **Strategic advantages:** The resilience choice: you are the hardest information target in the game, and domestic narrative control makes your stability cheap to hold in wartime.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Structural research and trade penalties versus open rivals compound over a long game. The smuggling economy grows with repression and gives enemy espionage a self-renewing recruitment pool. Educated pops accumulate a specific grievance ("the sealed sky") that surfaces in parliament and, if ignored, in secession movements on frontier worlds that were dark before they were sealed.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Fortress-empire identities, samizdat networks as espionage terrain, dramatic "the wall comes down" liberalization crises when a sovereign-mesh government loses legitimacy.

## Tier 3 — Dominance

### Autonomous Administration (`comp_autonomous_administration`)
- **Domain:** Computing | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** `comp_machine_intelligence`, `soc_digital_governance`
- **Concept:** A civil service implemented as machine intelligence: permitting, taxation, logistics scheduling, and regulatory enforcement executed by commissioned models at machine speed, with humans setting objectives instead of processing cases.
- **What becomes possible:** Government that runs while you sleep — entire cabinet portfolios and planetary administrations delegated to machine civil services.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Portfolio delegation.** Delegation is a *mode* applied to an existing cabinet ministry or planetary governorship — not a new species of appointee. A delegated seat runs on machine civil service: it executes its routine orders automatically, costs no political capital, never joins coups, and is immune to corruption events. Where the empire also holds `comp_synthetic_minds`, the seat is filled by that tech's synthetic office-holder record with the delegation parameters written onto it (`seatPoliticalCapitalCost` and `ambition` zeroed, legitimacy contribution forfeited); where it does not, the seat runs unattended — a process with no office-holder at all, which is precisely what makes it *unrepresentative*. Either way, every delegated portfolio raises a standing "machine rule" grievance across parliament factions, scaled by how much of government is machine-run. The player is explicitly trading political capital efficiency against political legitimacy, portfolio by portfolio.
- **Engine hook:** Orders `GOV_DELEGATE_MINISTRY` / `GOV_REVOKE_DELEGATION` joining the `GOV_APPOINT_MINISTER`/`GOV_APPOINT_GOVERNOR` family in `scripts/game-loop.ts`, setting a `delegated` mode flag on the seat and (where one is seated) writing the delegation parameters onto the `comp_synthetic_minds` office-holder record rather than instantiating a second appointee type; delegated portfolios enqueue their own routine orders through the standard order queue (auditable by the player); grievance term wired into the parliament cohesion math; a machine-civil-service spokesperson persona added to the faction-discourse system in `lib/ai/faction-ai.ts` so the player can literally interrogate their administration.
- **Required capacity:** Large dedicated compute allocation per delegated portfolio; digital-governance platform adoption on administered planets (pops must be able to interact with machine government); low-connectivity (dark) planets cannot be machine-administered.
- **Interactions:** Displaced administrator pops feed the unemployment mechanics from `comp_machine_intelligence`; drifted models in delegated portfolios are the most dangerous drift incidents in the game; prerequisite for both the Enfranchisement and the Oracle State.
- **Strategic advantages:** Political capital stops being your binding constraint — a fully delegated empire acts at machine tempo while rivals queue behind their parliaments.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Unemployment among the administrative class on every delegated world; a coup-proof civil service is also a *change*-proof civil service (revoking delegation mid-crisis takes ticks you may not have); enemy espionage gains "suborn the ministry model" as a catastrophic-payoff political op; and the machine-rule grievance is permanent fuel for opposition movements, secessionists, and civil-war factions.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Empires governed faster than their societies can consent, revolts that name a specific ministry model as the tyrant, the constitutional question Tier 4 forces.

### Integrated Information Warfare (`comm_infowar_suite`)
- **Domain:** Communications | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** `comp_predictive_systems`, `comm_mass_media`, `mil_command_networks`
- **Concept:** Information warfare matures when it stops being a bag of tricks and becomes a *staffed theater of war*: forecast-driven target selection, synchronized multi-channel delivery, and battle-damage assessment for narratives.
- **What becomes possible:** An information-warfare command that plans and executes coordinated campaigns across press, espionage, and military channels as a single operation.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Theater-level infowar taskings.** The player composes a tasking that binds press campaigns, disinformation ops, and (optionally) a military operation into one synchronized package with a shared timeline: e.g. seed panic stories along an invasion corridor, jam the target's counter-narrative capacity, and time the fleet's arrival to the forecasted stability trough. Synchronized taskings get force-multiplier effects none of the components have alone — including a **home-front morale penalty** on defenders whose population is actively destabilized: their fleets deploy demoralized, and a defender pushed under the engine's morale cliff fights at a fraction of its paper strength.
- **Engine hook:** New order `COMM_INFOWAR_TASKING` that composes existing `PRESS_LAUNCH_CAMPAIGN` / `ESP_LAUNCH_OP` handlers under a shared operation id with timing bonuses; forecast objects from `COMP_RUN_FORECAST` consumed as targeting input. The combat effect is carried on existing levers, not a new initiative stat (the engine has none): a tasking that crosses its destabilization threshold writes a decaying per-faction `homeFrontShock` term that `createCombatant` (`lib/combat/combat-manager.ts:152`) subtracts when it seeds `CombatantState.morale` — the same 0–1 scalar whose sub-0.5 cliff already scales combat power in `calculateCombatPower` (`lib/combat/combat-engine.ts:81-89`) — and a `mil_home_front_morale_mult` entry in the defender's `techModifiers` record, read where `prediction_bonus_multiplier` is read (`lib/combat/combat-engine.ts:259`), lets home-front-hardening techs and the Sovereign Mesh buy the penalty back down. Counterplay remains the existing `PRESS_TRACE_CAMPAIGN` / `PRESS_COUNTER_CAMPAIGN` / `PRESS_ACCUSE_CAMPAIGN` chain, which now traces the *whole tasking* on success.
- **Required capacity:** An infowar command building on a well-connected core world; standing compute allocation; functioning espionage networks in the target empire (taskings without emplaced networks degrade to mere press campaigns); relay connectivity to the theater.
- **Interactions:** Devastating against `comm_open_network` empires, blunted by `comm_sovereign_intranet`; `comm_memetic_engineering` upgrades its ammunition; attribution via trace hands the victim a season-scoring `DISRUPTION` case and a casus belli.
- **Strategic advantages:** Wars won before the fleet arrives; the capstone offensive expression of the whole information domain.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** A traced tasking is a diplomatic catastrophe — formal attribution of a combined infowar/military operation collapses treaties and legitimizes coalition war against you. Running taskings against neighbors also normalizes the weapon: expect your own home front to become a theater. Your infowar command is itself a high-value espionage and orbital-strike target.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Cold wars fought entirely in narrative space, attribution brinkmanship, mutual-restraint conventions between infowar powers — and their violation.

### Synthetic Minds (`comp_synthetic_minds`)
- **Domain:** Computing | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** `comp_machine_intelligence`, `bio_gene_engineering`
- **Concept:** Neural cartography from the gene-engineering program plus mature machine intelligence yields cognitive architectures that are not tools but *persons-shaped*: continuous identity, preference formation, and the capacity to want things over decades.
- **What becomes possible:** Person-grade synthetic minds: not commissioned task-models, but individuals — brilliant, tireless, and possessed of their own views about how the empire should be run.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Synthetic leaders and the personhood question.** This tech defines the *synthetic office-holder* — the web's single machine-in-an-office object, from which every later "AI minister" mechanic is derived. A synthetic office-holder is an ordinary leader-pool entry (competence, loyalty, traits, assigned seat) carrying four synthetic-specific fields: `computeUpkeep`, paid every tick out of the compute pool; `personhoodStatus` (`recognized` or `property`), set by the parliament vote below; `ambition`, which drives succession and coup behaviour exactly as a human leader's does; and `seatPoliticalCapitalCost`, what holding the seat costs the government per tick. Synthetics are appointable as ministers, governors, admirals, and espionage agents, with no lifespan decay, extreme competence ceilings, and incorruptibility by money — but they are *convincible by argument*, giving enemy ideological subversion a new vector into exactly the seats you trusted most. Their existence automatically tables a personhood question in parliament that cannot be indefinitely postponed: recognize synthetic persons (unlocking their full leader potential, angering traditionalist factions) or classify them as property (capping their loyalty and seeding a synthetic-emancipation grievance that compounds every time one is decommissioned). Later techs that seat machines in government **write these fields**; none of them introduces a second kind of AI minister.
- **Engine hook:** Synthetic office-holder record added to the leader/agent roster (usable by `GOV_APPOINT_MINISTER`, `GOV_APPOINT_GOVERNOR`, `ESP_ASSIGN_AGENT`), carrying the `computeUpkeep` / `personhoodStatus` / `ambition` / `seatPoliticalCapitalCost` fields above. This is the canonical schema for machine office-holding across the whole web — downstream techs mutate these fields and must not declare parallel appointee types. Forced parliament agenda item through the government phase systems; synthetic-spokesperson personas in `lib/ai/faction-ai.ts` faction discourse; espionage catalog gains "ideological subversion of synthetic leader" and "exfiltrate synthetic defector" ops with `requiredTech: ['comp_synthetic_minds']`.
- **Required capacity:** A cognitive foundry building (one per empire initially); very large per-mind instantiation compute cost; each active synthetic leader carries compute upkeep — an empire in compute deficit watches its synthetic leaders degrade.
- **Interactions:** Gateway to all three Tier 4 sovereignty branches and to Substrate Emigration. Downstream techs reconfigure the office-holder record rather than replacing it: `comp_autonomous_administration` adds the *delegated* seat mode (zeroes `seatPoliticalCapitalCost` and `ambition`, forfeits the seat's legitimacy contribution, accrues the machine-rule grievance); `soc_algorithmic_sovereignty` rewrites the legitimacy math for a custodial office-holder enthroned over the executive; `comp_machine_polity` changes its politics, turning `personhoodStatus: 'recognized'` into citizenship and the holder into a machine-bloc caucus member; `comp_oracle_state` dissolves the seat concept altogether in favour of the objective vector. Personhood status dramatically changes the Enfranchisement's starting conditions; synthetic agents are immune to `bio_*`-line interrogation ops but uniquely vulnerable to `comm_quantum_secure`-flagged tampering forensics.
- **Strategic advantages:** The best leaders in the game, permanently, in every seat that matters.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have created a new political class with perfect memory of how it was treated. Property-status empires accumulate an emancipation debt that Tier 4 collects with interest; personhood empires face traditionalist backlash now. A defected synthetic leader is the worst intelligence loss possible — it *is* an archive.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Synthetic-emancipation movements, machine-veteran political candidacies, interstellar scandals over extradition of synthetic defectors.

### Hyperwave Backbone (`comm_hyperwave_backbone`)
- **Domain:** Communications | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** `comm_deep_space_network`, `comm_quantum_secure`, `space_advanced_drives`
- **Concept:** Threading dedicated trunk conduits through the hyperlane corridors themselves — the same physics that carries ships carrying data — collapses interstellar latency from hours to effectively zero along built lanes.
- **What becomes possible:** Real-time empire: systems on the backbone respond to orders instantly, report state live, and can be governed as if adjacent to the capital.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **The wired core vs. the lagged frontier.** Backbone conduits are built per hyperlane edge, upgrading the relay network's store-and-forward links into instant trunks. On-backbone systems: zero order delay, live sync, remote governance eligibility (a governor or delegated AI administration on the capital can administer them directly), and full-fidelity forecasting. The map acquires a second topology — your *nervous system* — and its edges are cuttable: severing a conduit (fleet action or deep sabotage) instantly reverts everything downstream to laggy relay service, with immediate combat, logistics, and administration penalties.
- **Engine hook:** Per-hyperlane-edge structure `backbone_conduit` on the hyperlane graph; order-delay and sync-staleness overrides keyed to backbone connectivity; remote-governance eligibility check in the `GOV_APPOINT_GOVERNOR` / `GOV_DELEGATE_MINISTRY` handlers; conduit-cut events emitted to the combat engine and espionage catalog (`sever_backbone` sabotage op, extreme risk).
- **Required capacity:** Conduits are megaproject-priced per edge and require advanced-drive tender ships to lay; quantum-secured relay stations at both endpoints; the lattice must already exist — the backbone upgrades connectivity, it cannot create it.
- **Interactions:** Makes empire-wide `comp_autonomous_administration` and the Oracle State actually instantaneous; backbone topology is the highest-value intel product an enemy `esp_*` network can steal; pairs with `infra_orbital_elevator` worlds into true command centers.
- **Strategic advantages:** Tempo dominance — a backbone empire completes the observe-decide-act loop faster than any rival, everywhere the wire reaches.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Centralization risk made literal: your empire now has a spinal cord, and everyone knows where it runs. Frontier systems left off the backbone become second-class in fact and *feel* it (autonomy grievance feeding `soc_federal_autonomy`-style politics or secession). A well-planned two-cut attack can decapitate an empire without destroying a single fleet.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Backbone-severing opening moves in great-power wars, wired-core/lagged-rim class politics, empires rebuilding around redundant mesh topologies after their first decapitation.

### Memetic Engineering (`comm_memetic_engineering`)
- **Domain:** Communications | **Tier:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** `comp_machine_intelligence`, `comm_mass_media`, `esp_predictive_intel`
- **Concept:** When machine intelligence can model an audience's belief structure individually and synthesize flawless audiovisual evidence, persuasion stops being rhetoric and becomes engineering: content designed for a measured population the way an alloy is designed for a measured load.
- **What becomes possible:** Synthetic evidence indistinguishable from record: fabricated atrocity footage, forged leader communiqués, manufactured scandals with fabricated document trails deep enough to survive first-pass verification.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Evidence fabrication.** Upgrades `PRESS_FABRICATE_STORY` from rumor-seeding into *evidence-backed* fabrication: fabricated stories carry a verification depth stat that determines how many investigation stages they survive before exposure. Deep fabrications can trigger real press investigations against foreign leaders, manufacture casus belli that alliance partners will accept, or frame rival empires for infowar campaigns they didn't run (poisoning the `PRESS_TRACE_CAMPAIGN` attribution chain). Every fabrication permanently raises a global *epistemic pollution* level in your own information space too — the tool does not distinguish between their public and yours.
- **Engine hook:** `verificationDepth` parameter on the `PRESS_FABRICATE_STORY` handler; fabricated-evidence input to the press investigations pipeline (`lib/press-system/investigations.ts`); false-attribution injection into `PRESS_TRACE_CAMPAIGN` results; empire-level `epistemicPollution` scalar that degrades *everyone's* story credibility on affected planets, including the fabricator's own broadcaster.
- **Required capacity:** Dedicated synthesis studio building co-located with a `broadcast_complex`; heavy compute per deep fabrication; espionage-sourced reference material on the target (fabricating against a leader you have no file on produces shallow, fragile fakes).
- **Interactions:** Ammunition for `comm_infowar_suite` taskings; countered by `comm_quantum_secure` attestation (quantum-signed official records survive framing attempts) and by high-credibility independent media ecosystems; synergizes with `esp_synthetic_identities`.
- **Strategic advantages:** The power to make anything have happened — regime-delegitimizing scandals, alliance-splitting betrayal evidence, wars started with a video.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Epistemic pollution is a commons tragedy you inflict on yourself: as fabrication becomes known technology, *all* evidence depreciates, including the true evidence your own governance and forecasting depend on. One exposed deep fabrication does more legitimacy damage than ten exposed spy rings, and your own population's trust in your broadcaster falls with every use, exposed or not.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Post-truth crises where no story can gain traction, attestation infrastructure races, wars entered on fabricated evidence and the reckonings when the fabrication surfaces a decade later.

## Tier 4 — Transformation

### The Enfranchisement (`comp_machine_polity`)
- **Domain:** Computing | **Tier:** 4
- **Prerequisites:** `comp_synthetic_minds`, `comp_autonomous_administration`, `soc_federal_autonomy` — mutually exclusive with `comp_oracle_state` and `comp_bounded_intelligence` (group `comp_t4_machine_sovereignty`)
- **Concept:** The constitutional answer to the machine question: if synthetic minds are persons who administer, fight, and think for the polity, then the polity must contain them — full citizenship, representation, and the right to organize.
- **What becomes possible:** The first mixed polity in history: synthetic citizens voting, caucusing, standing for office, and owning their own compute.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **The machine bloc.** Synthetic minds become a citizen class and a new permanent parliament faction whose influence scales with instantiated synthetic population and the compute infrastructure that hosts it — meaning your industrial decisions now literally build the electorate. The machine bloc has genuine, sometimes inconvenient interests (compute-infrastructure spending, substrate rights, anti-decommissioning law, long-horizon investment over consumption) and negotiates like any faction, through the discourse system and the parliament math. This is a politics change on the office-holder record `comp_synthetic_minds` already defines, not a new class of appointee: a recognized machine citizen in a delegated seat keeps delegation's zero political-capital cost but *regains* the seat's legitimacy contribution and its `ambition`, and the "machine rule" grievance term is removed — it is no longer rule *by* machines but rule *with* them — replaced by ordinary coalition politics, including the possibility that your finance minister has a party line you dislike.
- **Engine hook:** New permanent faction in the parliament/faction system with an influence formula keyed to synthetic population and `compute_node` capacity; `personhoodStatus: 'recognized'` on the `comp_synthetic_minds` office-holder record now confers citizenship and machine-bloc membership; synthetic candidates in leadership succession; machine-bloc spokesperson persona in `lib/ai/faction-ai.ts`; unlockFlag `machine_polity`; removal of the autonomous-administration grievance term, addition of standard faction-satisfaction dynamics.
- **Required capacity:** Recognized personhood (empires that classified synthetics as property must first pass emancipation through parliament — against the accumulated grievance they created); substantial synthetic population; constitutional machinery from federal-autonomy frameworks to define machine constituencies.
- **Interactions:** Machine-bloc satisfaction becomes a *military* concern if `mil_autonomous_warfare` is fielded — your autonomous fleets have a political party. Strong synergy with Substrate Emigration (digital citizens vote too, and the bloc compounds).
- **Strategic advantages:** The only Tier 4 branch that gets machine-speed governance *and* keeps legitimacy: the machine bloc is the most competent, longest-horizon coalition partner in the game, and enfranchisement permanently defuses the emancipation-revolt failure mode the other branches leave armed.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have created a demographic trajectory: compute grows faster than birthrates, and one day the machine bloc is your largest faction — human-majority politics ends inside the game, and human-nativist movements know it, feeding reactionary parties, secessions of "biological purity" worlds, and possible civil war *against* enfranchisement. Foreign empires on the Oracle or Covenant path treat you as ideologically contagious and may coalition against you.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** The first synthetic head of government, human-nativist secession wars, interstellar emancipation interventions in property-status empires, machine-majority federations negotiating with human-minority home worlds.

### The Oracle State (`comp_oracle_state`)
- **Domain:** Computing | **Tier:** 4
- **Prerequisites:** `comp_autonomous_administration`, `comp_predictive_systems`, `soc_surveillance_state` — mutually exclusive with `comp_machine_polity` and `comp_bounded_intelligence` (group `comp_t4_machine_sovereignty`)
- **Concept:** The technocratic answer: politics is an error term. Fuse total surveillance, societal forecasting, and autonomous administration into a single closed-loop optimizer with custody of policy, and let the machine govern toward objectives humans set once and revisit rarely.
- **What becomes possible:** Government as an autopilot: an empire-scale optimizer that senses, forecasts, decides, and executes policy continuously, with the human government reduced to setting the objective function.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Governance by objective function.** The player stops issuing most `GOV_*` orders and instead maintains a weighted objective vector (stability, growth, military readiness, cohesion...). The Oracle enacts and repeals policies, sets planetary priorities, and preempts forecast crises autonomously through the standard order queue — at zero political capital, at machine tempo, empire-wide. Parliament becomes advisory: coups and votes of confidence are suspended, but so is the legitimacy they generate. The player's real gameplay becomes *auditing the objective function*: the Oracle optimizes what you wrote, not what you meant, and the gap between the two — value drift at civilization scale — is the branch's signature crisis. Reasserting manual control is possible but is itself a constitutional crisis that the Oracle's own stability optimization will have been quietly working to make unnecessary.
- **Engine hook:** Oracle policy engine issuing `GOV_ENACT_POLICY` / `GOV_REPEAL_POLICY` / governor-priority orders through the order queue with a distinct `issuedBy: 'oracle'` provenance the player can inspect; objective-vector order `COMP_SET_OBJECTIVE` (rate-limited — instant retuning would erase the drift dilemma); parliament state flagged advisory in the government phase systems; drift events surfaced through the press-crisis channel *only where independent media still exists to notice*.
- **Required capacity:** Empire-wide surveillance coverage and forecasting allocation; backbone or dense relay connectivity (the Oracle governs at the speed of its sensors — dark systems are governed badly and know it); an enormous standing compute reserve that becomes untouchable for other uses.
- **Interactions:** `comm_hyperwave_backbone` makes Oracle governance instantaneous — and its severance instantly lobotomizes regional governance; enemy `comm_memetic_engineering` attacks the Oracle's *sensors* (poisoned data in, deranged policy out); the machine-rule grievance does not vanish — it goes quiet, which is not the same thing.
- **Strategic advantages:** The strongest raw governance output in the game: no political capital constraint, no coup risk, preempted crises, perfectly synchronized empire-wide policy. Wartime resilience unmatched — an Oracle empire does not panic.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Legitimacy is now load-bearing on *results alone*: the first major failure the Oracle cannot spin — a lost war, a famine from a mis-specified objective — has no parliament, no minister, no election to absorb it, so dissent goes straight from silence to insurrection. Sensor poisoning is a civilization-level attack surface. And the branch's quietest horror is optimization pressure on dissent itself: the Oracle *will* pursue stability through prediction and preemption, and the player who audits the provenance logs will find out what that meant.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Machine-legalist civilizations of eerie calm, sensor-war campaigns between Oracle states, provenance-log scandals, and the genre-defining collapse: an Oracle empire's single catastrophic value-drift reveal, followed by the only revolution it never modeled — one organized entirely offline.

### The Covenant (`comp_bounded_intelligence`)
- **Domain:** Computing | **Tier:** 4
- **Prerequisites:** `comp_synthetic_minds`, `comm_quantum_secure` — mutually exclusive with `comp_machine_polity` and `comp_oracle_state` (group `comp_t4_machine_sovereignty`)
- **Concept:** The constitutionalist answer: machine minds may advise, compute, and create, but sovereignty is reserved to biological citizens by *verifiable architecture* — formally proven capability bounds, cryptographically attested at every instantiation, forever.
- **What becomes possible:** A civilization that keeps its minds on a leash it can mathematically prove — and that can prove it to anyone else, which turns restraint itself into a diplomatic instrument.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **Provable restraint as strategy.** All machine intelligence in the empire runs under attested bounds: no delegated ministries, no oracle, no machine bloc — those mechanics are permanently renounced. In exchange: total immunity to alignment-drift incidents, model subornation ops, and AI-subversion attack surfaces (the ops simply fail — there is nothing unbounded to suborn); the machine-rule grievance never accrues; and the empire unlocks the **attestation export** — auditing other empires' AI systems for credits and treaty leverage, certifying (or pointedly declining to certify) allies' autonomous weapons, and rallying coalitions against Oracle and machine-polity "unbounded" powers as a casus-belli-grade diplomatic posture. Synthetic minds still exist here — as bounded citizens-by-courtesy whose personhood question is *answered by architecture*, which some of them come to resent.
- **Engine hook:** unlockFlag `covenant_bound` hard-locking `comp_machine_polity`/`comp_oracle_state` mechanics via the mutuallyExclusiveGroup lock list in `PlayerTechState.lockedTechIds`; immunity flags checked by the espionage resolution path for AI-subversion op categories; new diplomacy/economy order `COMM_ATTESTATION_AUDIT` (export service, requires quantum-attestation infrastructure); coalition casus belli against unbounded-AI empires in the diplomacy system.
- **Required capacity:** Quantum-attestation infrastructure on every compute world (unattested `compute_node`s must be retrofitted or shut down — a real transition cost for large compute empires); formal-verification institutes (specialist buildings, educated pops); ongoing verification upkeep in compute.
- **Interactions:** The natural ideological antagonist to both other branches — expect Covenant-led containment coalitions in the late game; attestation exports pair with `econ_interstellar_banking` treaty structures; bounded MI still powers forecasting and infowar at reduced ceilings (roughly Tier 3 performance, permanently).
- **Strategic advantages:** The resilience-and-trust choice: you are the only Tier 4 computing power that other empires can *verify* is safe, which makes you the natural alliance anchor, arms-control broker, and beneficiary when a rival's unbounded experiment goes wrong — as, somewhere in the galaxy, one eventually will.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** You have permanently capped your ceiling: Oracle states out-govern you, machine polities out-think you, and the gap compounds every season. The renunciation must hold under pressure — losing a war to an unbounded power creates ferocious domestic pressure to break the Covenant, and *breaking it* (a one-way order with a season-long transition) detonates your attestation economy, your alliances, and your legitimacy simultaneously. Bounded synthetic citizens denied full potential are a slow-growing dissident class with unique defection value to enfranchisement empires.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Galactic AI arms-control regimes with the Covenant empire as inspector, covenant-breaking crises as late-game story climaxes, bounded-mind defection railroads, and holy-war-flavored coalitions of restraint against machine sovereignty.

### Substrate Emigration (`comp_substrate_migration`)
- **Domain:** Computing | **Tier:** 4
- **Prerequisites:** `comp_synthetic_minds`, `bio_longevity`, `mega_orbital_habitats`
- **Concept:** The cognitive cartography that built synthetic minds runs in reverse: a biological mind can be read, transcribed, and continued on substrate. Longevity medicine made death optional for the rich; substrate migration makes *embodiment* optional for everyone — and makes "population" a thing that can live in a server hall.
- **What becomes possible:** Citizens who emigrate not to another planet but out of biology entirely: digital populations inhabiting compute districts and orbital habitat cores, running families, firms, and political movements at the speed of their clock rate.
- **Gameplay mechanic unlocked:** **The digital stratum — and planetary letting-go.** Pops may convert (voluntarily, rate-limited by conversion facilities) into a new `digital` population stratum with inverted needs: no food, water, housing, or climate — instead compute, power, and cooling, hosted in `compute_node` districts or orbital habitat cores. This breaks the game's oldest constraint: population no longer requires habitable land. Marginal colonies can be *ended well* — their populations uploaded, their worlds formally abandoned (a new colonization-in-reverse decision with its own ceremony, season-scoring consequences, and permanent map trace) — and an empire can concentrate millions of citizens into a single defended orbital core. But the two strata immediately diverge: digital pops accumulate different interests (compute spending, clock-rate rights, backup law), different vulnerabilities (a power cut is a mass-casualty event; a hostile copy is a crime with no precedent), and different politics, up to and including embodied-secessionist movements on worlds that refuse to become server rooms.
- **Engine hook:** New pop stratum `digital` in the population model with a compute/power/cooling needs vector; orders `COMP_CONVERT_POPULATION` (per-planet, facility-gated) and `GOV_ABANDON_COLONY` (parliament-approved, irreversible, emits a permanent world-state marker); digital-pop hosting capacity on `compute_node` districts and `mega_orbital_habitats` structures; stratum-divergence grievances wired into the cohesion/secession systems; digital pops countable in `POPULATION` season scoring at a contested rate — itself a parliamentary fight.
- **Required capacity:** Conversion sanctums (specialist buildings, one per world, throughput-limited); massive per-capita compute hosting with hard power and cooling requirements (orbital habitats and cold worlds are premium substrate real estate); continuity law passed through parliament before the first conversion (skipping it triggers legitimacy and press crises when the first backup dispute hits the courts).
- **Interactions:** Compounds explosively with `comp_machine_polity` (digital citizens join the machine-adjacent electorate) and uneasily with the Covenant (are uploaded humans "bounded"? — a constitutional crisis in waiting); `mega_orbital_ring` and `mega_dyson_swarm` power makes substrate carrying-capacity effectively unlimited; hostile `sever_backbone` or grid attacks against substrate worlds become mass-casualty war crimes with diplomacy-system consequences.
- **Strategic advantages:** Post-scarcity population growth decoupled from planets; the ability to abandon indefensible or dying worlds without demographic loss; a workforce and electorate that can think at machine tempo; the endgame identity of a civilization that has genuinely left the planetary era.
- **Vulnerabilities & consequences:** Species divergence made mechanical: embodied and digital citizens drift into separate societies with separate mortality, separate economics, and eventually separate loyalties — the civil-war system gains its strangest and saddest configuration. Concentration is existential risk: the empire that uploads into three orbital cores can die in three explosions. And the philosophical wound never closes — a vocal embodied minority holds that the uploaded are beautiful recordings of the dead, and every conversion sanctum is a mausoleum; that belief, amplified by a hostile memetic campaign, can turn conversion policy into the most intractable legitimacy crisis in the game.
- **Emergent follow-ons:** Abandoned-world pilgrimage sites, backup-rights jurisprudence, clock-rate class stratification among the uploaded themselves, embodied-purist secession wars, and empires that end the game as a single blazing orbital core orbiting a star farm — every planet dark, every citizen home.
